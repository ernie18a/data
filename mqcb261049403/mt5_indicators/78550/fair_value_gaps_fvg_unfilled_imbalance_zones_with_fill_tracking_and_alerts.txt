A fair value gap (FVG, also called an imbalance) is a three-candle pattern where price moved so fast that the wicks of candle 1 and candle 3 do not overlap:
Bullish FVG: the low of candle 3 is above the high of candle 1.
Bearish FVG: the high of candle 3 is below the low of candle 1.
Price often comes back to such a gap later. This indicator shows the gaps that are still open, so you can see at a glance where those zones are.
What it shows
Every open gap as a box from candle 2 to the right, green for bullish, red for bearish.
The 50 % line of each gap (often called consequent encroachment).
How much of a gap price has filled since it formed. When the gap is filled (100 % by default, or the percent you set, for example 50 %), its box ends at the candle that filled it.
Optionally the filled gaps as grey boxes, so you can see how price reacted to them.
How it works
Gaps are found on closed candles only, so a gap never appears or disappears afterwards (no repaint).
Small gaps can be skipped: minimum size as a part of ATR(14) (default 0.25 x ATR) or in points.
To keep the chart clean only the newest open gaps are drawn (default 10 per direction).
The calculation is incremental: after the first run only the new candle is processed.
Inputs
Bars to scan (default 1500)
Minimum gap size, x ATR(14) / in points
A gap counts as filled at this percent (default 100)
Unfilled gaps shown per direction; keep filled gaps as boxes; filled gaps shown
Draw the 50 % line; colours
Alerts: on a new gap, when price comes back into a gap; push notification and e-mail
Buffers for Expert Advisors (iCustom, not drawn)
0 / 1: top / bottom of the newest unfilled bullish gap
2 / 3: top / bottom of the newest unfilled bearish gap
EMPTY_VALUE when there is none
Example: double top[]; CopyBuffer(handle, 0, 1, 1, top); gives the top of the newest open bullish gap on the last closed candle.
Good to know
It works on any symbol and time frame.
The indicator only draws and calculates; it never trades.
