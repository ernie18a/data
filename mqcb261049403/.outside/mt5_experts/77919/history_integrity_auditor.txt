Every backtest, every indicator and every optimisation result rests on the bar history the terminal happens to hold, and almost nobody audits that history. This tool does: it reads the bars your terminal would hand to an Expert Advisor and counts six classes of defect in them — impossible OHLC values, broken bar times, bars that are missing while the broker says it was quoting, bars with no ticks or no range at all, bars that disagree with the broker's own M1 series, and spreads that are zero or above a limit you set.
Five Market Watch symbols, M15, 5 000 closed bars each; the pass takes about a second. Sess% is the column to read - 99.80 for XAUUSD against 100.00 for the other four - while Grid% ranges over 68-100 and says mostly how much weekend each symbol has. This is the same audit as the journal block further down, repeated two minutes later on a second chart for the screenshot, which is why the file prefix differs; every number is identical. The findings file was switched off for it, and csv rows counts the rows of that file, which is why it reads 0 next to 85 findings. The shipped panel is light text on a dark plate and was recoloured black-on-white here.
The problem
A missing bar is invisible. Candles sit side by side on the chart whether the time between them is one period or three hours, so a hole in the history looks exactly like a quiet market. The Strategy Tester does not warn about it either: it models what it is given, and a strategy tested across a hole is tested on a market that skipped the hour it would have lost money in.
The defects that matter are not exotic. A bar whose close sits above its own high makes a stop-loss test pass that reality would have failed. A duplicated bar time makes every "bars back" calculation point at the wrong bar. A zero-tick bar is a price the market never quoted. A timeframe whose bars do not match the broker's own M1 bars means two of your indicators are reading two different pasts.
The usual test for all of this is to scroll the chart and see whether anything looks odd. That test finds nothing, and it finds nothing in a convincing way.
What it measures
Six independent checks over the last N
closed
bars of one timeframe, for one symbol, a list, or everything in Market Watch. Each check has a definition that can be recomputed by hand, and each finding is written to a CSV with the bar time, so the report can be audited rather than believed.
OHLC consistency.
high < low, open or close outside [low, high], or any of the four prices at or below zero. One finding per bar per violated predicate: an inverted bar legitimately produces several, because open and close are then both above the high and below the low at the same time.
Bar times.
A time that does not increase from one bar to the next (a duplicate or a bar out of order), and a bar whose open time is not on the period grid — time % PeriodSeconds() != 0 — which is how a history built by a bad import or a broken server session shows itself.
Missing bars, against the symbol's own sessions.
For each pair of consecutive bars the tool computes how many openings of the grid are absent between them. Then, and this is the part that makes the number usable, it asks SymbolInfoSessionQuote whether the market was supposed to be quoting at each missing opening. Missing openings outside every quote session are *explained* (nights, weekends, holidays in the session table); missing openings inside a session are
unexplained
and counted separately. A symbol with no session record at all is reported as unclassified — never silently as explained. Neither that case nor an empty M1 window occurred on the broker measured below; they are handled, not demonstrated.
Empty and flat bars.
tick_volume == 0 is a bar built from no ticks. open == high == low == close with ticks in it is a flat bar: legal in a dead hour, suspicious in a liquid one, and worth knowing about before an indicator divides by the bar range.
M1 cross-check.
Each audited bar is rebuilt from the M1 bars inside its own window — first open, highest high, lowest low, last close, summed tick volume — and compared with the bar the broker serves for the higher timeframe. Only windows that the M1 history actually covers are compared; windows beyond the M1 coverage are reported as out of coverage and windows inside the coverage with no M1 bar at all get their own counter. Deviations are measured in points, with an optional tolerance.
Spread sanity.
The number of bars whose stored spread is zero, the number above a limit you set, and the maximum and mean spread over the audited bars. A history full of zero spreads is a history that cannot answer any cost question.
The headline figure is
session coverage
: the share of the openings the broker's own session table says should exist that actually have a bar. Grid coverage — the same ratio over every slot including nights and weekends — is printed next to it, because on its own it tells you mostly how many weekends you audited.

Read the two coverage columns against each other, because that is the whole argument for splitting them. Grid coverage says 68-72 % for every forex symbol and 100 % for BTCUSD, which measures nothing except that forex closes at the weekend and crypto does not. Session coverage - the same ratio over only the openings the broker's own session table claims - says 100.00 % four times and
99.80 % for XAUUSD
. One number in that pair of columns is a defect; the rest is the calendar.
That 0.20 % is a single gap, and it is small enough to check by hand. The findings file holds one row for it:
symbol;period;check;bar_index;bar_time;field;detail;value XAUUSD;M15;C3_GAP;3304;2026.09.07 18:30:00;gap;class=unexplained in_session=10;14
In a C3_GAP row, bar_time is the first opening that is
missing
and bar_index is the index, counted from zero at the oldest audited bar, of the bar that follows the hole, so this row reads: on Monday 2026.09.07 the bar that opened 18:15 is followed by the bar that opened 22:00, and the fourteen openings from 18:30 to 21:45 are absent. Gold's quote sessions for a Monday, as this broker declares them, are 00:00-20:58 and 22:00-24:00. Ten of the missing openings, 18:30 to 20:45, fall inside the first session; the remaining four, 21:00 to 21:45, fall in the declared break. Ten unexplained, four explained, which is what the row says. Two and a half hours of a liquid Monday evening that every backtest on this terminal silently skips, and on the chart it is two candles standing side by side.
The other 2 332 missing XAUUSD openings are the calendar: eleven weekends of 196 openings each, 43 daily breaks of four, and the four break openings inside that Monday hole - 2 156 + 172 + 4. The 1 920 of each forex pair are ten weekends, and nothing else: 10 x 192 openings of 15 minutes is exactly 1 920. Those are the numbers that make grid coverage useless, and they are still worth printing, because a symbol whose "explained" openings do not line up with its session table is telling you the session table is wrong.
The rest of the audit found nothing, which is also a result: across 25 000 bars there was no impossible OHLC value, no duplicated or off-grid bar time, no empty and no flat bar, and all 25 000 bars matched the broker's own M1 series to the last digit - 0.0000 points of maximum deviation. BTCUSD deserves one more line: zero gaps over 52 days, and a spread that is exactly 700 points on all 5 000 bars. A fixed spread is not a defect, but it is not a measurement either, and a strategy tuned on it is tuned on a constant.
XAUUSD M15, 5 000 bars: every missing bar opening by the hour of the server day, split by what the broker's own session table says about that hour. The hatched bars are the openings outside the quote sessions - eleven weekends, which is why they cover every hour, plus the daily break at 21:00. The ten solid ones are the only openings in 25 000 bars the session table cannot explain, all on one Monday evening. Drawn from the bar dump and the session dump of this run; the tool itself writes the numbers, not the figure.
The same five symbols on M1, with the spread limit set to 20 points, is the opposite kind of page and is worth one paragraph because it shows what the checks look like when they do fire: 10 613 findings, flat bars 20 / 22 / 40 on EURUSD / GBPUSD / USDJPY, a spread above 20 points on
all
5 000 gold and BTCUSD bars, and 25 / 28 / 20 missing openings inside a session on the three pairs that had none on M15. The last figure is not a contradiction: 5 000 M1 bars span three and a half days of one week, a different window entirely, and a one-minute hole inside a session is far more common than a fifteen-minute one. On M1 the two coverage columns also converge - 99.44 and 99.44 for GBPUSD - because a window that short contains no weekend for the grid figure to be flattered by.
The same tool on M1 with the spread limit at 20 points: Flat and SprHi now carry numbers, InSess finds holes on pairs that were clean on M15, and the M1bad column prints - because on M1 there is nothing below the audited period, so the cross-check reports itself as off - the result table never shows a zero for something it did not measure.
Verifying the report independently
The counters above are checked by something that shares no code with the tool. A Python script reads only the raw material - the bar dump and the session dump the EA writes - recomputes every counter of the OHLC, time, gap, empty/flat and spread checks from the definitions above, and compares the result with the EA's own summary file.
Over the runs behind this article - 15 symbol/period audits across M1, M15 and W1 - it compared
215 counters and reported 0 mismatches
, including the ten in-session openings of the gap above, recounted from the bar times and the session table alone.
Two things that recount does not cover. The M1 cross-check is not in those 215 counters: the script can pull the M1 series out of the terminal itself and rebuild the bars, but that path was not run for the numbers here, so compared 5000 / mismatched 0 rests on the EA's own count. And a counter that is zero on both sides is confirmed only as a zero - across every run there was no impossible OHLC value, no non-increasing or off-grid time, no gap that was not a whole number of periods, no empty bar, no M1 mismatch, no window inside the M1 coverage without an M1 bar in it, and no deviation for the tolerance to swallow. Those branches were never seen firing on the data measured here.
What pins the definitions of the defects this broker never produces is a second, smaller test of the recount script itself: a 12-bar synthetic history with the defects written in by hand and the expected counts written beside them - 29 assertions, no terminal involved. The EA applies the same predicates in the same order, and that it does is a reading of the two sources side by side rather than a measurement. The fixture is still where two of the decisions in this tool come from: that an inverted bar must produce five findings rather than one, because with the interval turned inside out the open and the close are each both above the high and below the low; and that a single undivided coverage figure was close to meaningless.
The tool also audits its own arithmetic and prints both sides of each identity, so the report can be checked without any Python at all:
audit 1        : bars 25000 + missing 8102 = 33102, expected_openings 33102 -> OK
audit 1        : expected_openings 33102, grid openings in the audited spans 33102 -> OK (5 symbols OK, 0 not)
audit 2        : C1 0 + C2 0 + C3 85 + C4 0 + C5 0 + C6 0 = 85, findings total 85 -> OK
audit 2        : findings handed to the report 85, rows in the findings file 85, dropped by the guard 0 -> OK

Note SprHi - in the table and above limit - in the C6 line. The spread limit was 0, which means the check is off, so the column prints a dash and not a zero. The tool applies that rule to every column of the result table and every figure of the per-symbol lines: a check that did not run prints -, a check that ran and found nothing prints 0, and the two are never the same character. It matters more than it looks — with the gap check off, a coverage figure computed as bars / (bars + missing) would read a confident
100.00 %
.
Five files come out of a run, in MQL5\Files\HistoryIntegrityAuditor\:
<prefix>_overview.csv — one row per symbol of the list, including the symbols that could not be audited, with their status.
<prefix>_findings.csv — one row per defect: symbol;period;check;bar_index;bar_time;field;detail;value.
<prefix>_<symbol>_summary.csv — every counter of one symbol, 62 columns, including the arithmetic identity.
<prefix>_<symbol>_sessions.csv — the symbol's quote sessions as the broker declares them, which is the input that decides whether a gap is explained.
<prefix>_<symbol>_bars.csv — optional, the audited bars exactly as the terminal served them.
The last two are what make the report checkable by something other than this tool, which is the subject of the section above. The prefix carries the symbol tag, the timeframe and the chart id, so two charts cannot overwrite each other's evidence, and a tester run writes into the agent's own sandbox rather than over a live run's files.
How to use it
Attach it to a chart of the symbol and timeframe you actually test on, set the number of bars to at least the range your backtests use, and read the journal.
Start with
session coverage
and
missing in session
. Those two numbers decide whether your backtests ran on the market or on a sketch of it.
Sort the C3_GAP rows of the findings file by the hour in bar_time. Holes concentrated at one hour of the day are usually a server or a session problem; holes spread evenly are usually an incomplete download.
Press
Refresh
in the terminal's Symbols dialog (or delete the history and let it re-download) and run the audit again. The difference between the two runs is what your terminal was missing locally rather than what the broker lacks.
Switch the M1 cross-check on for the timeframe your EA trades. A mismatch that is still there after a history refresh is worth a question to the broker: it means two of its own series disagree.
Re-run it after changing broker, server, or anything about how history is fetched. Data quality is a property of a particular terminal at a particular time, not of a symbol.
Limits, stated plainly
It audits the history
your terminal holds
, which is the history your tests use — not whatever the broker's server could still send. Refreshing the history can change every number here, and step 4 above exists for exactly that reason.
A gap the session table calls explained can still be a real hole, because the session table is also the broker's statement. The tool shows both figures so the two claims can be compared instead of blended.
The M1 cross-check can only speak where the M1 history reaches. It is silent, by design, about older bars, and the count of windows it skipped is printed next to the count it compared.
Tick data is not audited. Real-tick backtests have a second data set with its own defects, and this tool does not look at it.
It reports; it does not repair. Nothing here writes to the history, and no finding on its own proves the broker is at fault — a terminal that was offline for an hour produces the same missing bars as a feed that stopped.
No trading functions, no DLL, no network access, no external files: it reads bars and writes CSV files to MQL5\Files.
