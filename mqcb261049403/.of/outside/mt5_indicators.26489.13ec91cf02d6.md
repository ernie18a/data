Calculate relative asset-strength lines from a cluster of instruments sharing one currency. For each instrument, optionally smooth its price, invert it when the common currency is the base, and multiply by contract size. Normalize the common currency to the reciprocal of one plus the sum of these adjusted prices; multiply it by each adjusted price to obtain the other asset values. Use each value's bar-to-bar proportional change as its strength; optionally blend this change with its bar-to-bar difference to include momentum. Calculate positive and negative volatility bands from the root mean square of the strength lines, using the number of instruments as the divisor and configurable multipliers for inner and outer bands.

The described alternative trading rules are: buy the rising asset and sell the falling asset when their lines cross; buy an asset when its line crosses above zero and sell it when it crosses below; buy an asset that breaks above an outer positive band and sell one that breaks below an outer negative band; sell after a line touches an upper outer band and returns downward inside the upper inner band, or buy after it touches a lower outer band and returns upward inside the lower inner band. Another suggested rule trades on high relative speed between lines or momentum peaks, but gives no precise trigger. For a traded pair, buying its base and selling its quote corresponds to going long, and the reverse corresponds to going short. No exit rule or rule for resolving conflicting signals is specified.
