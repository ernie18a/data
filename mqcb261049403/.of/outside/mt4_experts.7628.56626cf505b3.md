When flat, go long if either: (1) the previous candle crosses upward through the pivot calculated from the prior day’s high, low, and close, has a materially bullish body, current ask is above its close but below the current bar’s high, and the average of the latest three closes exceeds the average of the latest nine; or (2) the Donchian channel expands from narrower than the pivot’s first resistance-support range to wider, its upper boundary rises while its lower boundary does not fall, the three-close average exceeds the nine-close average, and ask is below the current bar’s high by a buffer. Go short if either: (1) the previous candle crosses downward through the pivot with a materially bearish body, current bid is below its close but above the current bar’s low, and the three-close average is below the nine-close average; or (2) the Donchian channel expands across that same range, its lower boundary falls while its upper boundary does not rise, the three-close average exceeds the nine-close average, and bid is above the current bar’s low by a buffer. Allow at most one open position. For a long, close if, around three bars after entry, the previous close remains materially below entry; for a short, close if it remains materially above entry. Once the position is sufficiently profitable, trail its stop behind the Donchian lower boundary for longs or upper boundary for shorts, updating only when that boundary moves favorably. Initial stops use the prior candle’s low for longs or high for shorts, with a buffer; take profit is a fixed distance from entry.
