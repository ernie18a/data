On each tick, compare the base instrument's close-price simple moving average on the current and two preceding bars. Three strictly rising values signal a base long; three strictly falling values signal a base short. Otherwise, open no new positions.

For each enabled hedge instrument, calculate correlation over a configurable lookback: at each bar subtract that instrument's close-price moving average from its close, then divide the sum of paired deviations' products by the square root of the product of their sums of squared deviations. A positive configured threshold passes when correlation is at least the threshold; a negative threshold passes when correlation is at most the threshold. A zero threshold does not pass.

When trading is enabled, a directional signal exists, and a hedge passes its correlation test, independently open the missing base and hedge positions for that pair. The hedge direction is either the same as or opposite to the base direction, according to its configuration. Check for an existing position separately by pair, instrument, and direction; an opposite position does not prevent entry. Each pair has its own position group.

For each pair, determine whether the base instrument's long swap rate times its intended position size plus the hedge instrument's swap rate for the signaled hedge direction times its intended size is strictly positive. Use the base long swap rate even for a base short. Retain the last determination when there is no directional signal. On every tick, independently of the entry permission and correlation, close all positions in a pair's group when their combined floating profit strictly exceeds the configured target if that determination is positive; otherwise include accrued swap in the combined profit before comparing it with the target.
