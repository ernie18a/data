On each new chart bar, calculate the previous closed bar’s close-price RSI for every cross among EUR, GBP, AUD, NZD, USD, CAD, CHF and JPY. For each currency, average its seven cross RSIs, using RSI when it is the base currency and the RSI-scale maximum minus RSI when it is the quote currency. Mark a currency strongest or weakest only when its average is strictly above or below every other currency’s average; otherwise retain its previous mark. Marks persist until replaced by the opposite extreme.

Maintain persistent long-signal flags for the supported pairs. When a pair’s base is marked strongest and its quote weakest, set its flag according to whether the chart symbol contains that pair’s name; otherwise leave the flag unchanged. The supported pairs are EUR against GBP, AUD, NZD, USD, CAD, CHF and JPY; GBP against AUD, NZD, USD, CAD, CHF and JPY; AUD against NZD, CAD, CHF and JPY; and USD against CAD, CHF and JPY. GBP/AUD and GBP/NZD share one flag. An additional flag uses AUD strongest and USD weakest but tests whether the chart symbol contains GBP/USD. Process these tests in the stated order, so the later GBP/NZD test can overwrite the shared flag.

A long entry requires any long flag to be set, the fast linear-weighted moving average of typical price to exceed the slow one, the low two bars ago to be below the previous bar’s high, and the absolute difference between the current bid and previous close to exceed a configured distance. Also require the trade-count limit, chart-history, take-profit, margin, broker stop-distance and trading-permission checks to pass. The separate short-entry branch does not consult currency strength; it is evaluated only if the long branch did not trigger.
