Run either of two variants of the same multicurrency grid. Process configured currency pairs in order, with a fixed preferred direction for each pair; the newer variant can reverse every preferred direction. On each permitted, scheduled tick, calculate net exposure per currency across all account positions. Express each position’s volume in a common currency using the ask price of the common-currency/base-currency pair when conversion is needed. Add that volume to the base currency and subtract it from the quote currency for a long; reverse the signs for a short.

Before processing pairs, identify the account’s most losing and most profitable positions. Maintain a balance benchmark initialized from balance and floating profit, with a different divisor in each variant. If startup equity is below a configured fraction of balance, attempt one forced correction of the most losing configured pair. Otherwise, when loss management is enabled, correct it if its volume exceeds one order unit and the balance above the benchmark exceeds the proportional loss on one unit. The older variant sends an opposite order for one unit; the newer variant sends a same-direction order followed by an opposite order, each for one unit. Update the benchmark upward after a correction and end the tick. The newer variant can disable this correction.

If profit-target management is enabled and the largest individual profit exceeds the absolute largest loss, or total floating profit is positive, move the most profitable position’s existing target toward the market by a configured fraction, provided its volume exceeds one unit and the revised target respects the broker’s minimum stop distance. The older variant always enables this management.

For each pair, derive a grid step as the largest of a configured fraction of its existing target, or of bid if flat; a multiple of the broker’s minimum stop distance; and spread plus one price point. Round to the instrument’s price precision. If flat, open in the preferred direction when the exposure of the currency being bought is below its limit. The newer variant also requires the negative exposure of the currency being sold to be below its limit; sufficiently high account margin bypasses both exposure checks. Set the initial target one or more grid steps beyond the entry price and open the corresponding configured number of order units; the older variant uses one unit.

For an existing long, sell one unit when bid rises above the target minus (position units minus one) grid steps. For an existing short, buy one unit when ask falls below the target plus that distance. The newer variant does this only when more than one unit remains, leaving the final unit to its target. Otherwise, prevent additions under the variant’s low-margin checks. Add one unit to a long when ask is at or below the target minus (position units plus one) grid steps; add to a short when bid is above the target plus that distance. An addition also requires the resulting position volume to remain below its limit, the bought-currency exposure minus sold-currency exposure to be at or below the risk limit, and the bought-currency exposure to be below its limit. The newer variant additionally checks that negative sold-currency exposure is below its limit, and sufficiently high account margin bypasses the exposure and risk checks. After a configured date, replace the risk limit with one order unit. Retain the existing target on additions and partial closes. Stop after the first pair order on a tick; the variants differ in whether a particular low-margin exposure check stops the entire tick or skips to the next pair.
