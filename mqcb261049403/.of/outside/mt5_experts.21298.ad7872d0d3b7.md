For each of the seven USD currency pairs, calculate fast and slow moving averages of the selected price on the chart timeframe at multiple scales. Scale both averaging periods by cumulative ratios between successive standard timeframes, starting with the chart timeframe and ending at the weekly scale; for weekly or monthly charts, use the same scales as for a five-minute chart. For EUR, GBP, AUD and NZD pairs, divide the sum of slow averages by the sum of fast averages; for CAD, CHF and JPY pairs, use the inverse ratio. Let S be the sum of these seven ratios. USD strength is S minus the number of pairs; each other currency's strength is (S minus its ratio plus 1) divided by its ratio, minus the number of pairs.

At each new bar, compare the two most recently closed bars for every ordered pair of distinct currencies. Signal when the first currency's strength minus the second's crosses above a configurable positive gap, while the first strength rises and the second falls. If the stronger currency is USD, trade its pair with the weaker currency in the direction favoring USD. Otherwise, trade the stronger currency's USD pair in the direction favoring that currency only if its strength exceeds USD's. If the weaker currency is not USD, also trade its USD pair in the direction favoring USD only if its strength is below USD's. Preserve these implementation exceptions: for stronger EUR and weaker CHF, test GBP strength below USD instead of CHF strength; for stronger EUR and weaker GBP, use the misspelled symbol GPBUSD for the second leg. The second leg is actually opened in the first leg's direction, even though duplicate and opposite-position checks use the second leg's intended direction; if the first leg has no direction, the second leg is not opened.

Suppress a candidate leg when a strategy position already has its symbol, intended direction and ordered-currency signal label. If enabled, close strategy positions on each remaining candidate symbol that oppose its intended direction before opening. Open only when quotes and instrument data are available and enabled stop-loss and take-profit distances satisfy the broker's minimum, or a configurable spread-based fallback when that minimum is zero. Attach configurable stop-loss and take-profit distances. On every tick, trail strategy positions after profit exceeds the trailing distance plus a step, moving the stop to the trailing distance from the current price only when it improves by more than the step. Stops and take profits can exit positions.
