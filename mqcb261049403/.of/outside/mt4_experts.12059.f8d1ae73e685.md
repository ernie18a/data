For each open position on the chart’s instrument, read a Fibonacci retracement or expansion drawn on the chart and form its ordered levels from the zero anchor through its extension. Use a price offset based on spread and point size, with its sign determined by the direction of the Fibonacci range. For a long position, step the stop loss up to just below a Fibonacci level when price has crossed the next level, the current level is below the bid by at least the broker’s minimum stop distance, and the existing stop is still below the proposed stop by a safety margin. For a short position, step the stop loss down to just above a Fibonacci level when price has crossed the next level downward, the current level is above the ask by at least the minimum stop distance, and the existing stop is still above the proposed stop by a safety margin. Preserve the position’s entry price and take profit; this logic only adjusts stops and opens no trades.
