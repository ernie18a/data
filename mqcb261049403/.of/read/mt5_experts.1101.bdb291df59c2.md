Calculation: Parabolic state and level = Process indicator-timeframe bars chronologically. Initialize the state as rising, the anchor as the first processed bar's low, and the anchor bar as that bar. Track the highest high and lowest low since the last reversal, including the current bar. On each bar, calculate distance as the number of bars since the anchor bar raised to a configurable exponent, multiplied by a configurable price coefficient scaled to the indicator-bar duration. The candidate level is anchor plus distance in the rising state and anchor minus distance in the falling state. If rising and the candidate level is greater than the current low, reverse to falling and set the anchor to the tracked highest high plus a configurable price offset. Otherwise, if falling and the candidate level is less than the current high, reverse to rising and set the anchor to the tracked lowest low minus the offset. On reversal, set the anchor bar to the current bar and reset the tracked extremes for subsequent bars. The level on a reversal bar is the new anchor; otherwise it is the candidate level. The indicator timeframe can be the chart timeframe or a higher or lower timeframe. Evaluate a configurable bar offset: it may select the still-forming indicator bar or a completed indicator bar.
Long: Parabolic state is rising AND parabolic level is not zero.
Short: Parabolic state is falling AND parabolic level is not zero.
Exit: long, parabolic state is falling AND parabolic level is not zero.
Exit: short, parabolic state is rising AND parabolic level is not zero.
Exit: long, low is less than or equal to entry price minus the stop-loss distance OR high is greater than or equal to entry price plus the take-profit distance.
Exit: short, high is greater than or equal to entry price plus the stop-loss distance OR low is less than or equal to entry price minus the take-profit distance.
