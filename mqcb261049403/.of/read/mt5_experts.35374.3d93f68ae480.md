Calculation: Divergence components = At the first tick of a new chart bar, index chart bars backward with 0 as the forming bar and 1 as the last completed bar. Compute the close-based MACD main line, rounded to six decimal places, using a shorter and a longer EMA. For bullish divergence use MACD and low; for bearish divergence negate MACD and high. Call these transformed series M and P. A price pivot at index j has P[j] strictly lower than each of P[j+1] through P[j+4]. Set the recent pivot price to P at the first pivot found when scanning from index 0 through the recent search limit; use zero if none is found. Scan older indices i in increasing order, starting beyond the minimum separation. A candidate requires M[i] through M[i-4] all below the negative MACD threshold and M[i-2] strictly below each of M[i], M[i-1], M[i-3], and M[i-4]. Stop scanning when a candidate is not strictly lower than the preceding candidate. For each accepted candidate, scan from i-4 through i plus the older search limit for the first price pivot; use its P as the older pivot price, retaining the previous older pivot price or zero initially if none is found. A divergence exists when an accepted candidate within the maximum candidate count has (M[i-2] > M[2] AND older pivot price < recent pivot price) OR (M[i-2] < M[2] AND older pivot price > recent pivot price).
Long: At the first tick of a new chart bar, position side is flat AND last completed bar's close-based RSI rounded to two decimal places < lower RSI threshold AND bullish divergence exists.
Short: At the first tick of a new chart bar, position side is flat AND last completed bar's close-based RSI rounded to two decimal places > higher RSI threshold AND bearish divergence exists.
Exit: long, low <= entry price - long stop distance OR high >= entry price + long target distance.
Exit: short, high >= entry price + short stop distance OR low <= entry price - short target distance.
