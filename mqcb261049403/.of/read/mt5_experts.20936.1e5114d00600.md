Calculation: Bar index = number of bars before the currently forming bar; index 1 is the last completed bar. Evaluate decisions when a new chart bar starts. For each of the two successively higher timeframes, use its last completed bar and earlier bars.
Calculation: Fan = five close EMAs with strictly increasing periods. Let E1 through E5 run from shortest to longest period. The bullish chart fan satisfies E5 < E4 < E3 <= E2 <= E1; the bullish first-higher-timeframe fan satisfies E5 < E4 < E3 < E2 <= E1; the bullish second-higher-timeframe fan satisfies E5 < E4 < E3 < E2 < E1. Bearish fans reverse every inequality. Before these comparisons, round the three shortest chart EMAs and the two shortest first-higher-timeframe EMAs to price precision. Fan width is the rounded difference between the maximum and minimum unrounded EMA values.
Calculation: Chart fan breakout = last completed chart bar opens below the minimum and closes above the maximum of its five EMAs for an upward breakout; reverse the inequalities for a downward breakout. First-higher-timeframe breakout uses the same test on its last completed bar and its three shortest EMAs, with the two shortest rounded to price precision.
Calculation: Stochastic = low/high stochastic with a smoothed main line and an SMA signal line; the main-line smoothing and signal-line periods are independent parameters. A bullish chart stochastic cross requires main[2] < signal[2], main[2] < lower threshold, and main[1] > signal[1]; a bearish cross reverses the inequalities and uses the upper threshold.
Calculation: Flat at a starting chart bar = scan consecutively older bars from that bar, updating their highest high and lowest low. Stop without a flat if their range exceeds the maximum flat height. The first range with bar count greater than the minimum flat length and range less than bar count multiplied by the flatness coefficient is the flat; stop after the maximum scan length if none is found.
Calculation: Upward flat breakout = either the flat starting at chart bar 2 exists and open[1] < its high < close[1], or the flat starting at chart bar 3 exists and open[1] < its high < close[2] and both bars 1 and 2 close above their opens. Downward flat breakout reverses the inequalities and uses the flat low. Test the bar-2 flat first; if it produces a breakout, do not test the bar-3 flat.
Calculation: Bullish stochastic divergence = the main stochastic at a fixed confirmation offset is lower than each of an equal number of older neighboring values and is strictly increasing through all newer neighboring values up to chart bar 1. Let its price low be the minimum low over that whole neighborhood. Scan older bars from immediately beyond that neighborhood up to a maximum lookback, stopping if any scanned low is below that price low. A qualifying older stochastic trough is lower than every stochastic value scanned since the confirmed trough, is lower than each of its equally many neighboring values on both sides, is more than the minimum divergence separation bars older, and has a neighborhood minimum low above the confirmed trough's neighborhood minimum low. Stop without divergence if an earlier examined trough is below the confirmed trough and does not qualify.
Calculation: Hammer = a chart bar whose high-low range exceeds a minimum, and whose longer shadow exceeds a minimum fraction of the range while its opposite shadow is below a maximum fraction. A long lower shadow is bullish; a long upper shadow is bearish. Select the most recent hammer within the scan limit. For the extreme test, scan completed chart bars over the extreme lookback in oldest-to-newest array order and compare the selected hammer's bar index to the zero-based array index of the lowest low for a bullish hammer or highest high for a bearish hammer.
