On each new bar, estimate the pair’s relative price relationship from historical highs and lows: scale the second instrument’s range to the traded instrument’s range, anchor the scaled prices at their lows, and invert the mapping for negative correlation. Measure the traded instrument’s close against that scaled price in its points. Set the entry boundary to a correction of the largest absolute deviation observed in the training history.

Open or add a contrarian position only when the current absolute deviation is at least the entry boundary and is not larger than its value on the preceding bar. The traded instrument is sold when its deviation is positive and bought when negative. For a two-instrument trade, take the opposite side in the second instrument for positive correlation and the same side for negative correlation. For a substitute cross instrument, map the direction according to whether its quote is direct or inverted. Increase the target position in proportion to how many boundary units the deviation represents, capped by the configured deal limit; add only the volume needed to reach that target.

Close the paired or cross position when the deviation crosses zero against the entry direction, with direction interpreted for correlation and quote orientation. Also close when the absolute deviation contracts below a configured fraction of the entry boundary and the position is profitable, or when combined position profit exceeds a configured percentage of account balance.
