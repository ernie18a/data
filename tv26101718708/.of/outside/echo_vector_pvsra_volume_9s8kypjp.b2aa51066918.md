When a symbol override supplies external volume, compare confirmed volume pivots from that source with confirmed price pivots of the traded instrument. Use the same configurable number of bars on each side to confirm each pivot. A bullish divergence occurs when price and volume lows are confirmed on the same bar, the price low is lower than the preceding confirmed price low, and the volume low is higher than the preceding confirmed volume low. A bearish divergence occurs when price and volume highs are confirmed on the same bar, the price high is higher than the preceding confirmed price high, and the volume high is lower than the preceding confirmed volume high. Compare each series with its own preceding pivot, even if those preceding pivots occurred on different bars.

While no hypothetical position is active, enter long at the confirmation-bar close on a bullish divergence or short at that close on a bearish divergence; give the bullish signal priority if both occur together. Ignore new divergences while a position is active. Set a fixed percentage target and stop on opposite sides of the entry, with the target above entry for a long and below entry for a short. On every bar, including the entry bar, exit when the bar's high or low reaches either threshold. Check the target first, so it takes priority if both thresholds are reached on the same bar.
