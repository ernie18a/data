On each confirmed bar, calculate fast and slow efficiency as the absolute net source change divided by the sum of absolute one-bar changes. Optionally derive their lookbacks from an Ehlers dominant-cycle estimate; otherwise use fixed lookbacks. Track confirmed price pivots, the direction and anchor of the current leg, and a rolling history of positive completed-leg displacements. Rank current directional displacement against that history; require sufficient completed legs before issuing signals.

For exhaustion, require a stretched displacement rank and fast efficiency below its value at the rollover lookback. Apply enabled gates: a trend regime; a variance ratio at or above its trend floor, allowing missing variance ratio; a valid Ornstein–Uhlenbeck half-life no greater than a multiple of the evaluation horizon; no excessive rise in the external volatility index over its lookback, allowing missing index data; and sufficient divergence quality. For divergence quality, use the most recently calculated same-side pivot score for the current leg direction: combine the efficiency decline between consecutive same-side pivots when the newer pivot makes a more extreme price, the time between those pivots, and whether volume declined. Normalize and weight these components before comparing with the quality floor. If the regime gate is enabled, use a valid linked external regime's trend state when available; otherwise use an internal trend classification based on slow efficiency and ADX. The internal classification gives chaos priority when a decaying count of true-range shocks reaches its high percentile. An exhaustion signal goes short after an up-leg or long after a down-leg, subject to a shared exhaustion cooldown.

If no exhaustion signal fires, an enabled ignition signal goes long in an up-leg or short in a down-leg when fast efficiency crosses above its ignition threshold and displacement rank is below the young-leg ceiling. Apply a separate ignition cooldown and, if regime veto is enabled, suppress ignition in internal chaos. Exhaustion has priority over ignition. Calibration statistics and volatility-index elevation do not gate signals. No position exits are specified.
