Generate reversal and pullback candidates from the traded instrument, then optionally filter them using momentum relative to another instrument. Long and short rules are symmetric.

For reversals, calculate a configurable moving-average midline, standard-deviation bands around it, and inner and outer true-range channels. A long candidate occurs when the close crosses back above the lower normalized deviation threshold after being below it; a short candidate crosses back below the upper threshold. Reject a candidate if the preceding excursion touched the outer channel. Apply the configurable long-EMA slope filter, same-direction cooldown, bar-close confirmation, and WaveTrend filter. WaveTrend confirmation can require a recent directional cross, a recent opposite extreme, either, or a regime-dependent choice; extremes can use fixed levels or historical percentiles. Optionally require recent directional WaveTrend divergence, detected by comparing consecutive price and oscillator swing extremes at oscillator crosses.

For pullbacks, require the long EMA to slope in the trade direction and meet the minimum ATR-normalized slope. Price must have recently touched the configured midline or opposite inner channel, freshly touched the midline or closed across it on the preceding bar, and now close beyond the midline with a candle in the trend direction. Optionally require WaveTrend to point in that direction. Apply a separate directional cooldown, bar-close confirmation, and the configured treatment of recent opposite divergence. Suppress a pullback when a same-direction reversal candidate occurs on the same bar.

For external confirmation, select the benchmark automatically by market, or use a manual benchmark. The automatic relative benchmarks are the other major crypto for Bitcoin, Bitcoin for other crypto, the local broad index for the Turkish equity market, silver for gold, gold for silver, and a broad US equity ETF otherwise. The market-dependent mode instead uses absolute momentum for gold, silver, Bitcoin, and Ether. Form the traded close divided by the benchmark close, or use the traded close alone in absolute mode. Smooth the logarithmic change of that series over the configured lookback. Its trend direction is determined by comparison with its moving average, zero, or both, as configured; its turn direction is the sign of its change over a configured interval. For each candidate family, require the configured trend direction, turn direction, both, either, or neither to match the trade direction. A neutral or unavailable direction does not confirm a trade.

In adaptive confirmation mode, apply that filter separately to reversals and pullbacks only after the confirmed subset has enough resolved historical signals and its better average return across the two targets exceeds the better average return of all candidates in that family by the configured margin. Track both subsets from candidate entry at the close, with a stop beyond the relevant swing extreme by an ATR buffer. Reversal targets are the midline when favorable and the opposite inner channel; pullback targets are the favorable inner and outer channels. Substitute the second target when the first is unavailable. Resolve each target independently from the following bar: stop first, including when stop and target are both touched on one bar; otherwise target, or timeout. Score a stop as a unit loss and a target as its absolute reward divided by initial risk; exclude timeouts from average returns.

After confirmation, optionally hide each signal category once enough first-target results exist unless its better target's average return exceeds the configured threshold. Keep separate categories for ordinary reversals, divergence reversals, ordinary pullbacks, and pullbacks warned by recent opposite divergence. Track hidden signals as well. All adaptive decisions use results resolved before the current bar's outcome processing.
