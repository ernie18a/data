An optional external instrument supplies volume. Compare each chart-instrument pivot's own recorded volume with the moving average of that external volume to adjust the pivot's support or resistance strength. If no external source is selected, use chart volume. All prices and other indicators come from the traded instrument, including its higher-timeframe data.

Build separate long and short scores from weighted trend, support/resistance, momentum, higher-timeframe and reward-to-risk components. Trend uses price relative to direction-specific moving averages selected by historical crossover performance, moving-average alignment and slope, MACD, and cloud position. Support/resistance uses higher-timeframe obstacles, Fibonacci levels, pivot levels, trend lines and channels, breakouts and violations. Momentum uses RSI and confirmed price/RSI divergence. Higher-timeframe scoring uses moving-average alignment, RSI, cloud position and relative volume. Adjust the trend and support/resistance weights by the ADX regime and clamp the total score.

Construct support and resistance from higher-timeframe pivots and averages, selected moving averages, Fibonacci levels, cloud boundaries, VWAP, confirmed price pivots, trend lines and channels. Score price pivots by touches minus violations plus the volume adjustment; require sufficient touches and positive strength. Merge nearby levels, sum their strengths and rank the strongest nearby levels. Use buffered opposing levels as targets and buffered same-side levels as stops to calculate reward-to-risk. When the first target is too close, use the second if available. For a proximity gate, sum the strengths of qualifying opposing levels within an ATR-scaled distance: depending on the chosen mode, subtract that sum from the score, set the score to zero, or leave it unchanged.

Lock scores on confirmed bars. Add directional bonuses for a pivot bounce, rejection or engulfing candle at that bounce, adequate reward-to-risk, and a divergence sequence whose length is large relative to its historical average. Issue a strong long or short when the corresponding locked score plus bonuses reaches the strong threshold, ADX passes its filter, the opposite score is sufficiently lower, no nearby higher-timeframe obstacle blocks the direction, and price has not violated the relevant pivot support or resistance. Issue a normal signal at the lower threshold under the same directional and obstacle filters, provided there is no strong signal in that direction and ADX is not ranging. Independently issue a range signal when reward-to-risk reaches its threshold and at least one directional condition holds: recent divergence, pivot bounce, proximity to the appropriate band or channel boundary, an RSI extreme in a weak trend, or directionally favorable RSI in a weak trend. Range signals do not require the score or obstacle filters.

For a recorded position, recommend exit when its instant trailing stop is touched, the locked opposite score reaches the normal threshold, or both cloud position and moving-average trend reverse against it. Also recommend exit after a confirmed close beyond the bar-close trailing stop. Initialize the stop from the user-supplied stop or the strongest eligible same-side level, with an ATR fallback. Once profit exceeds initial risk, move it only favorably to an eligible stronger level that leaves more than initial risk to current price; tighten it after rejection at an opposing level. Combine it with a never-loosening ATR trail. The backtest can enter on enabled signal grades, reverse on an enabled opposite signal, and after a minimum holding period exit on its instant trail or a strong opposite signal.
