When configured to use a continuous futures contract, classify the chart instrument using its current close and data from that continuous contract. For each daily, weekly, and monthly period, calculate an anchored volume-weighted average of typical price from the start of the period, and a central pivot range from the previous completed period's high, low, and close. Use the previous completed daily average true range as a deadband. Classify as flat if required data is missing, the close equals the anchored average, or its distance from the average is less than the deadband. Otherwise classify as long above the average and short below it. A long is strong when the close is above the entire pivot range; otherwise it is a long pullback. A short is strong when the close is below the entire pivot range; otherwise it is a short pullback. For the yearly classification, use the yearly anchored average and its volume-weighted standard deviation in place of the pivot range: a long is strong above the upper band and a short is strong below the lower band; otherwise the directional state is a pullback. Missing or nonpositive yearly deviation yields flat. Where volume is unavailable or zero, use equal weighting for those bars.
