On each bar, calculate a smoothed stochastic oscillator and its signal line; ATR; a selectable moving average of close; and upper and lower price bands around that average. Set band distance to ATR times a volatility multiplier times (1 + (50 - absolute distance of the stochastic from 50) / 100), then exponentially smooth each band. Fit the selected linear, logarithmic, adaptive or leading regression to the moving average; form a channel using its standard deviation, and measure the fitted slope over a lookback in ATR units. Measure bullish and bearish heat as smoothed counts of closes above and below the moving average, divided by their sum. Treat ATR as expanding when it exceeds a multiple of its moving average.

Maintain an optional breakout trend filter. Normalize close within a rolling high-low range, calculate its rolling standard deviation and a weighted average of that deviation. When deviation crosses above its average after a sufficiently long interval since the opposite crossover, capture the interval's highest high and lowest low as a channel. A subsequent close, or open-close midpoint if configured, outside the channel activates the corresponding trend and deactivates the channel. An opposite breakout reverses the trend. End an active bullish trend when close crosses below the average low since its breakout; end an active bearish trend when close crosses above the average high since its breakout. Directional context passes when the filter is disabled, the matching trend is active, or a matching breakout is recent.

A bullish calendar setup requires bullish context, an upward regression slope, close inside the regression channel, a pullback near the lower band or moving average or a sufficiently low stochastic, stochastic crossing above its signal or already above it and rising, bullish heat at least balanced with bearish heat, and no fast bullish trend. The bearish setup mirrors these conditions. A fast trend requires price beyond the corresponding regression boundary or smoothed band, a matching slope, extreme matching heat, and either expanding ATR or an extreme stochastic reading.

For a bullish setup, suggest selling a nearer-expiry call and buying a later-expiry call at the same strike, rounded from spot plus an ATR offset. For a bearish setup, use puts and subtract the offset. Classify the setup as prime only if its strike lies within spot times manually supplied implied volatility times the square root of short-leg time to expiry in years, price is not near the opposing band or beyond the opposing regression boundary, stochastic is not overheated in the trade direction, and its recent history reached a sufficiently deep pullback or rebound. Otherwise classify it as weak. A plotted prime signal is later downgraded if, within a configurable confirmation window, close fails to move a specified fraction of the signal-bar ATR in the intended direction and, if required, regain the appropriate side of the moving average.

A neutral calendar candidate requires a flat regression slope, close near the moving average, balanced heat and no ATR expansion. Suggest selling the nearer expiry and buying the later expiry at the same strike rounded from spot, subject to acceptable implied volatility and event risk; the code does not choose call versus put.

A bullish call vertical candidate requires enabled bullish analysis, a sufficiently positive regression slope, bullish context and dominant bullish heat, close above the moving average, stochastic below its upper limit, and no upper-extension warning. It also requires a strong bullish candle on at least one of: a bullish channel breakout with stochastic above its signal; a cross above the upper band with stochastic above its signal; a reclaim of the moving average after a nearby low with stochastic above its signal and rising; or a retest and hold of the active bullish trend guide. The bearish put vertical mirrors these rules. A strong candle closes in the trade direction near its favorable extreme. Suppress a vertical when the same-direction calendar setup is present, and enforce a separate minimum spacing between confirmed vertical signals of each direction. For a call vertical, buy a call at a rounded ATR-offset strike and sell a higher-strike call at the same expiry, at least one strike step higher. For a put vertical, buy a put at the mirrored offset and sell a lower-strike put at the same expiry, at least one strike step lower.

An upper-extension warning requires price above the upper regression boundary or sufficiently above the upper band, dominant bullish heat, and either a hot stochastic cooling or an upper-band rejection. Suppress it during a healthy active bullish breakout trend and when price and stochastic are still rising with a close near the bar high. The lower-extension warning mirrors this using bearish heat, cold stochastic or lower-band rejection, and suppression for an ongoing strong downward bar. Avoid fresh call calendars under these warnings. A fade-trap warning occurs when price is near or beyond the directional band or regression boundary, the matching regression slope and heat are strong, and stochastic is extreme; avoid an immediate opposite-direction calendar. In fast trends, wait for a pullback or consider a diagonal instead of a plain directional calendar.

For the single displayed scene, apply this priority: upper-extension warning, lower-extension warning, bullish vertical, bearish vertical, bullish calendar, bearish calendar, neutral calendar, fast bullish trend, fast bearish trend, bullish fade trap, bearish fade trap, otherwise wait. Apply the configured call-only, put-only or both-direction mode to the relevant scenes; extension warnings can appear in any mode. These are option strategy candidates and warnings; no automatic position exit rule is specified.
