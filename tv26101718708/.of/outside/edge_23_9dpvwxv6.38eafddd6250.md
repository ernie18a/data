For each instrument and timeframe, evaluate three directional reversal factors: (1) bullish RSI divergence when price makes a lower low while RSI makes a higher low in oversold territory, or bearish RSI divergence when price makes a higher high while RSI makes a lower high in overbought territory; (2) the corresponding bullish or bearish divergence using volume-weighted money flow in its oversold or overbought territory; and (3) high-volume exhaustion with a long lower wick in oversold territory for bullish pressure, or a long upper wick in overbought territory for bearish pressure. Count active factors separately for each direction. If only bullish factors are active, signal long; if only bearish factors are active, signal short; if neither is active, remain neutral. If both directions have active factors, show conflicting pressure and make no directional decision. When volatility is exceptionally high relative to its recent norm, add one point to an existing directional score; volatility alone never creates direction. Cap the score at four. The score represents the number of aligned factors plus any volatility boost; it is a monitoring strength, not an entry or exit instruction. Reassess each instrument independently across the current, intraday, and daily timeframes.
