Bearish market-breadth risk framework that uses daily external US stock market data, not the traded instrument's own price. Inputs: NYSE advancing, declining and unchanged issue counts, NYSE new 52-week high and new 52-week low counts, and the NYSE Composite index close. Evaluate once per day. Total issues = advancing + declining + unchanged. High% = new highs / total issues x 100. Low% = new lows / total issues x 100. A daily Omen signal fires only when all four conditions hold on the same day: (1) Split breadth: High% and Low% are both at or above the same percentage threshold. (2) Uptrend: the NYSE Composite close is at or above its close a fixed number of trading days earlier. (3) Negative momentum: the McClellan Oscillator is below zero. It is the fast EMA minus the slow EMA of the ratio 1000 x (advancers - decliners) / (advancers + decliners). (4) Strength ratio: new highs divided by new lows is below a set limit, so new highs are not much larger than new lows. Cluster confirmation: a signal is Confirmed if a prior Omen signal occurred, strictly before today, within a fixed rolling window of bars. Otherwise it is a Single signal. Decisions: a Single signal is only a warning, so take no trade action. A Confirmed signal is the bearish trigger: go short the traded instrument, or exit and avoid longs, as a systemic crash or correction is likely. Treat the instrument as being in a risk zone, with no new longs, for the window length after the most recent signal of any kind. The framework gives no bullish or long-entry rule. When the window has passed with no new signal, the bearish state ends and the short position or long restriction is lifted.
