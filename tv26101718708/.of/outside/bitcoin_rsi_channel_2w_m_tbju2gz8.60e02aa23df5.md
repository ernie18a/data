Signal source: a Bitcoin index (external reference series, not necessarily the traded instrument), evaluated on the chart timeframe, which must be either 2-week or monthly. Decisions use closed bars only. Compute the standard RSI (fixed, conventional length, on close) of the Bitcoin index close. Build a dynamic RSI channel from two straight lines that are linear functions of elapsed time since a fixed per-timeframe origin date. The top line is fitted to historical RSI cycle peaks and the bottom line to historical RSI cycle troughs. Both slope slowly downward, so the channel narrows and shifts lower as Bitcoin matures. Use separate pre-fitted line coefficients for the 2-week and monthly timeframes; on any other timeframe there is no channel and no signal. Normalize the RSI into a risk metric: (RSI - bottom line) / (top line - bottom line), so 0 is the bottom line and 1 is the top line. Define a buy threshold near the low end of this 0-1 range and a sell threshold near the high end. Decision rules for the traded instrument, which is Bitcoin or an instrument traded alongside it: when risk <= buy threshold (extreme oversold), the state is bullish and the action is to go long or hold long. When risk >= sell threshold (extreme overbought), the state is bearish and the action is to exit longs, and optionally go short. When risk is between the thresholds, the state is neutral and there is no new entry. A natural reading is to exit a long once risk leaves the buy zone and heads toward the sell zone, with a definite exit when it reaches the sell zone. Signals are re-evaluated every bar, and the state is determined purely by where the normalized risk sits relative to the two thresholds.
