On a confirmed bar, watch the previous day’s high and low. A move above the previous high that closes back below it marks a failed upside sweep; a move below the previous low that closes back above it marks a failed downside sweep. Within a limited window after either sweep, require a structural close through the latest confirmed swing in the reversal direction: after the upside sweep, close below the latest swing low; after the downside sweep, close above the latest swing high. The breakout must also show displacement through an unusually large candle body or a fair value gap, unless displacement filtering is disabled. Emit a short after the upside sweep or a long after the downside sweep, subject to enabled filters: directional RSI momentum, oscillator divergence against the swept swing, trading session, above/below a long-term trend average, sweep-bar relative volume, candle-direction volume delta, directional trend strength, adequate volatility, reference-market alignment, and absence of a blackout window. Enforce a cooldown between same-direction signals. If the fair-value-gap entry model is enabled, require a matching gap on the signal bar and use its boundary as entry; otherwise enter at the close. Place the initial stop at the sweep extreme and set profit targets at configured risk multiples; dynamic targeting may substitute a favorable swing or prior-day extreme. For an active position, exit when price reaches the furthest target or the stop is breached/touched; after the first target, optional break-even and ATR-, swing-, or chandelier-based trailing rules can update the stop.
