On supported intraday timeframes, divide time into New York–anchored cycles and independently track each instrument’s cycle lows and highs, using closes, wicks, or both. Compare the traded instrument with a correlated instrument; optionally use a second correlated instrument for bullish signals only. A bullish signal occurs when one instrument’s current cycle low is strictly lower than its reference low while the other’s is strictly higher. A bearish signal occurs when their cycle highs move in opposite directions. Compare close extremes with the preceding cycle. Compare wick extremes with the most recent eligible stored cycle, or search eligible stored cycles from newest to oldest. At each cycle transition, remove stored wick-low references reached or breached by any enabled instrument’s completed cycle low; similarly remove wick-high references reached or breached by either instrument’s completed cycle high, then store the completed cycle’s extremes. If the premium/discount filter is enabled and its timeframe-dependent true open is available, require both traded-instrument lows to be below that open for a bullish signal, or both highs to be above it for a bearish signal. Recalculate current-cycle signals as extremes change. After a cycle ends, invalidate a retained bullish signal if any instrument that made the lower low falls below its recorded signal low; invalidate a retained bearish signal if the instrument that made the higher high rises above its recorded signal high. Use closes for close-based signals and wicks for wick-based signals. The indicator does not specify position entries, exits, or option trades.
