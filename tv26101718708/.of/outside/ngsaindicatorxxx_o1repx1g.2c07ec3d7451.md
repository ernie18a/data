On each bar, score four market stages using the traded instrument and a benchmark. Treat all lookbacks, thresholds, score weights, multipliers, confirmation periods, and cooldowns as parameters.

Calculate a simple moving average of the instrument's close and classify its percentage slope as rising, falling, or flat. For a flat average, classify its slope as curling up or down according to the change in slope. Identify confirmed pivot highs and lows; an uptrend requires both a higher high and a higher low, and a downtrend requires both a lower high and a lower low. Compare average up-bar and down-bar volume to identify accumulation and distribution. Identify churn from above-average volume and a small candle body relative to average bar range; identify expanding range from ATR relative to its average. Relative strength is positive when the instrument-to-benchmark close ratio has increased over its lookback.

Compute weighted stage scores: basing rewards a flat average, neither trend structure, price near the average, accumulation, nonexpanding range, and an upward curl. Advancing rewards a rising average, close above it, uptrend structure, positive relative strength when enabled, and absence of distribution. Topping rewards a flat average, distribution, a lower pivot high, expanding range, churn, and a downward curl. Declining rewards a falling average, close below it, downtrend structure, nonpositive relative strength when enabled, and distribution. Select the highest score, breaking ties in the order basing, advancing, topping, declining. Start in basing and change stage only when the same winning stage persists for the full confirmation period.

Buy on a basing-to-advancing transition only when volume exceeds its average by the breakout multiplier; otherwise mark the transition as unconfirmed. Sell or exit longs on an advancing-to-topping transition. On a topping-to-declining transition, exit longs and signal a short. On a declining-to-basing transition, signal a short cover. The stage display also describes advancing as buy or hold longs, topping as exit longs, and declining as avoid or short.

For profit booking, record the close and high on a basing-to-advancing transition, and maintain the highest high while advancing. Clear this tracking after leaving advancing. Only consider profit booking while advancing and after the close has gained at least the configured minimum from the recorded entry.

Build an exhaustion score from additive weighted signals: progressively larger extensions above the instrument's moving average; short- and long-window parabolic price gains, using timeframe-dependent windows; exceptionally high volume; a candle with a sufficiently long upper wick and a close in its lower half; an extreme RSI reading; a failed recent high, defined by a prior bar making a recent high followed by a bearish close below that bar's low; a cross below a short moving average; and a cross below a chandelier stop based on the highest high since entry minus an ATR multiple. The source's bearish-divergence condition never contributes because it overwrites the previous pivot before comparing it. Use the maximum exhaustion score over the current and preceding bars in the configured scoring window.

For the breadth modifier, calculate the external market index's moving-average slope, distance from that average, RSI and its multi-bar decline, and true-range average relative to its own longer average. Classify the index as advancing when above a rising average, declining when below a falling average, topping when above a flat average, and basing otherwise. Market danger means declining, or topping with falling RSI; market climax requires sufficient extension above the average, high RSI, and falling RSI. A warning occurs for high RSI, expanding index volatility, or a flat index average. Add the configured weights for danger, climax, warning, and a falling index average to obtain the breadth score.

When breadth filtering is enabled and its score reaches the weak-market threshold, lower both the strong and caution exhaustion thresholds. A strong profit-booking signal occurs when the profit gate is open and the windowed score reaches the strong threshold, the instrument is extremely extended, a parabolic gain coincides with volume climax, the chandelier stop is crossed, or enabled breadth is dangerous or climactic and the windowed score contains any exhaustion signal. Strong signals can repeat without cooldown and call for taking most or all profits. Otherwise, a score reaching the caution threshold triggers a partial trim and tighter stops, subject to a cooldown measured from the most recent strong or caution signal.
