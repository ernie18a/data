Compute a moving average and its slope; classify it as rising, falling, or flat using a configurable deadband. Track confirmed swing highs and lows to identify higher highs with higher lows, lower highs with lower lows, or neither. Compare the instrument’s relative performance with a configurable benchmark. Use these features to score four market stages: basing (flat average, little directional structure, price near the average, accumulation, subdued range expansion, or an upward curl); advancing (rising average, price above it, rising swing structure, positive relative performance when enabled, and no distribution); topping (flat average, distribution, lower highs, expanding range, churn, or a downward curl); declining (falling average, price below it, declining swing structure, negative relative performance when enabled, and distribution). Select the highest-scoring stage, resolving ties in the order basing, advancing, topping, declining, and require the same candidate to persist for the configured confirmation period before changing stages. On a transition from basing to advancing, signal a long entry; mark it volume-confirmed when volume exceeds its recent average by the configured breakout multiple. On a transition from advancing to topping, signal selling or exiting longs. On a transition from topping to declining, signal a short entry. On a transition from declining to basing, signal covering shorts.

While in the advancing stage, track the entry price and highest high since entry. Once the gain from entry reaches a configured minimum, evaluate exhaustion signals: increasing distance above the moving average at escalating severity; rapid gains over short or long timeframe-adjusted lookbacks; unusually high volume; a rejection candle with a long upper wick and weak close; bearish divergence between successive swing highs and RSI; extreme RSI; a failed new swing high; a cross below a short-term average; or a close crossing below a volatility-based trailing stop from the tracked high. Combine these signals in a weighted score and include recent prior-bar scores. Emit a strong profit-taking exit when the score reaches its applicable threshold, an extreme overextension occurs, rapid gains coincide with a volume climax, the trailing stop is hit, or a dangerous/overheated market has any exhaustion signal. Market danger is assessed from a benchmark index’s stage, overextension, RSI direction and level, volatility expansion, and average slope; when enabled, weaker breadth lowers the strong and caution thresholds. Otherwise emit a caution/trim signal when the score reaches its applicable lower threshold, subject to a cooldown; strong signals are not cooldown-gated. A single weaker signal may be shown as a watch without a trim signal.
