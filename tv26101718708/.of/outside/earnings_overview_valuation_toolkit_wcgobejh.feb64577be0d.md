Use the following independent strategies, with configurable thresholds and holding periods:

1. Post-earnings drift: Go long shares after a recent earnings release when reported EPS exceeds the analyst estimate, the measured post-release price reaction is positive, and price is below the median historical P/E valuation band. Exit after the holding period or if price falls below the earnings-day low.
2. Pre-earnings volatility: Before an upcoming earnings release, buy a call or put expiring in the week after the release when the recent average EPS surprise is high and the average absolute earnings-day price move is high relative to daily ATR as a percentage of price. Sell the option on the afternoon before the release. The source does not specify how to choose between a call and a put or define a high average surprise.
3. Value rebound: For a high-quality, profitable company, buy shares or a long-dated call when price reaches or falls below the lower historical P/E valuation band and a technical reversal or positive earnings reaction confirms a floor. Target the median and upper valuation bands. The source does not define the quality or reversal criteria or specify how to divide exits between the targets.

Construct valuation bands by summing the latest four reported quarterly EPS values, computing price divided by that sum when it is positive, and applying selected lower, median, and upper percentiles of recent P/E observations to the current EPS sum. Measure EPS surprise as reported EPS minus estimated EPS, divided by the absolute estimate. Measure the earnings reaction as the percentage change from the release-bar close to the close after the configured reaction window.
