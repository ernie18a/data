On the selected analysis timeframe, calculate fast, medium, and slow simple or exponential moving averages of each instrument’s close and a price efficiency ratio: absolute close change over a lookback divided by the sum of absolute one-bar close changes over that lookback, or zero if the denominator is zero. Use timeframe-specific moving-average lengths.

Classify each instrument in order. If efficiency is below the low-quality threshold, classify it as caution. Otherwise, if the medium average is above the slow average: when the fast average is above the medium average, classify a close above the fast average as bullish and a close at or below the fast average but above the medium average as a pullback; classify all other cases as caution. If the medium average is at or below the slow average: when the fast average is above the medium average, classify the instrument as bearish only if the close is at or below both the fast and medium averages; otherwise classify it as caution. When the fast average is at or below the medium average, classify it as bearish. Higher efficiency subdivides bullish and bearish states but does not change the breadth calculation.

Compute the shares of the full active universe classified as constructive (bullish or pullback) and bearish. Otherwise, if the benchmark is bearish and bearish breadth meets the capital-preservation minimum, exit to cash. Otherwise, if the benchmark is bearish or bearish breadth meets the defensive minimum, favor shorts. Otherwise, remain selective and neutral.
