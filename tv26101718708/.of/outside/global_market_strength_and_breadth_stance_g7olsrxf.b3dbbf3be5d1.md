For each index in the selected market universe, calculate three moving averages on the analysis timeframe (daily: fast, medium, and slow; weekly: 10, 30, and 40 bars; monthly: 3, 6, and 10 bars). Also calculate Kaufman’s 10-bar efficiency ratio. Classify an index as follows: if the medium average is above the slow average, label it Caution when efficiency is below 0.20; otherwise, if the fast average is above the medium average, label it Very Bullish when price is above the fast average and efficiency exceeds 0.40, Bullish when price is above the fast average with lower efficiency, Pullback when price is below the fast but above the medium average, and Caution otherwise. If the medium average is not above the slow average, label it Caution when efficiency is below 0.20; otherwise, when the fast average is not above the medium average, label it Very Bearish if price is below the fast average and efficiency exceeds 0.40, and Bearish otherwise. When the fast average is above the medium average in this regime, label it Caution if price is above the fast average or above the medium average, and Bearish otherwise. Across the universe, count Bullish, Very Bullish, and Pullback indices as constructive, and Bearish and Very Bearish indices as bearish. Use the primary index and these proportions to set the market bias in this order: if the primary index is Bullish or Very Bullish, at least 70% are constructive, and at most 10% are bearish, set broad long bias; else if the primary is Bullish, Very Bullish, or Pullback, at least 50% are constructive, and fewer than 30% are bearish, set long bias; else if the primary is Bearish or Very Bearish and at least 75% are bearish, set cash only; else if the primary is Bearish or Very Bearish, or at least 50% are bearish, set short bias; otherwise set selective neutral bias. This is a market regime filter, not an entry trigger.
