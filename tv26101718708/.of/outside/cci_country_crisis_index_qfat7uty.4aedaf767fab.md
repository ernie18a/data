Use country-level external market data to score six stress areas: domestic equities (benchmark downside momentum, bank and small-cap underperformance, volatility and sector breadth); currency and capital flows (currency depreciation, unusual currency weakness, rising yields and sovereign spreads, and depreciation beyond broad dollar moves); inflation (local-currency oil and gold, food commodities, and industrial commodities); sovereign and credit risk (spread level and widening, bank weakness, falling credit prices, and stocks and bonds falling together); external vulnerability (oil, trade-partner weakness, rising US yields, dollar strength, and trade-partner currency moves); and political sentiment (high volatility despite a flat market, alternative-sector underperformance, small-cap drawdown, and sustained volatility). Assign more stress points to more extreme conditions, cap each area at its prescribed weight, then sum them and amplify the total when several areas are stressed together. Smooth the composite so worsening stress is reflected faster than easing stress. Map the resulting score in ascending bands to: deploy, selective, cautious, hedge, reduce, and exit all as crisis stress increases; lower stress therefore favors long exposure, while the highest band exits exposure. Also map score bands to calm, alert, warning, danger, and crisis classifications.
