On each bar, calculate a country crisis score from external market data. Assign tiered stress points in six capped categories: domestic equity stress from benchmark return z-score, bank and small-cap underperformance, volatility and sector breadth; currency stress from direction-adjusted currency depreciation, its z-score, local yield increases, sovereign spread widening and depreciation beyond the dollar move; inflation stress from local-currency oil and gold, wheat, copper and natural gas; credit stress from sovereign spread level and widening, bank underperformance, high-yield credit weakness and simultaneous equity declines and yield increases; external stress from oil, trade-partner equity and currency moves, US yield increases and dollar strength; and sentiment stress from elevated volatility with a flat benchmark, alternative-sector underperformance, small-cap drawdown and sustained volatility. Scale currency thresholds for country volatility and oil stress for import dependence. Sum the category scores, amplify the sum when several categories are active, cap it, and smooth it faster when rising than when falling. At the highest action threshold, exit all positions. At successively lower thresholds, reduce exposure, hedge, act cautiously, or be selective; below all thresholds, deploy capital. Separately, set recommended long exposure in descending steps as the smoothed score rises, shift it according to risk appetite, and clamp it to the permitted range.
