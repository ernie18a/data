For the ratio of a primary asset to a comparison asset, assign each of nine engines a bullish, bearish, or neutral vote: relative strength from the difference in asset returns confirmed by the ratio’s trend; trend from aligned ratio averages and slope, optionally confirmed by the primary asset’s higher-timeframe trend; momentum from volume-weighted RSI and MACD direction; volatility from directional expansion or a squeeze release; statistical extremes favoring reversal from unusually low or high ratio levels; macro conditions from dollar, volatility-index, and Treasury-yield signals interpreted by asset class; liquidity from the direction of Treasury yields, with forex treated inversely; intermarket correlations interpreted by asset class; and volume participation when ratio direction, OBV slope, and relative asset volume agree. Apply asset-class-specific or user-selected engine weights and normalize the net bullish-minus-bearish votes. A sufficiently positive score indicates bullish bias for the primary asset relative to the comparison asset; a sufficiently negative score indicates bearish bias; otherwise remain neutral. Stronger and moderate directional markers use separate configurable score thresholds. This supplies relative-market bias, not entry timing or exit rules.
