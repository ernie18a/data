On confirmed bars within the configured trading dates and entry session, calculate a Hull moving average of market TICK and an EMA of that average. A bullish setup occurs when the Hull average crosses above its EMA, the average of the traded instrument's RSI and its RSI signal average is above the RSI midpoint, raw RSI is below the configured upper bound, the volatility index exceeds its threshold, and a fast EMA of TICK is positive. A bearish setup mirrors these conditions: cross below, averaged RSI below its midpoint, raw RSI above the lower bound, volatility above its threshold, and fast TICK EMA negative. Enter long or short respectively when the instrument's close is above or below its EMA. Allow pyramiding up to the configured limit. If flat, also allow a delayed long entry on the bar after a bullish setup when the close is above its EMA; this entry has its own exit rules. The corresponding delayed short entry is unreachable in the source. Size entries according to the configured capital and option-sizing settings, optionally increasing size based on positive net profit; after closed-trade daily profit exceeds its limit, reduce subsequent entry size to one rather than stopping entries.

For each direction, form a stop candidate from the previous bar's low minus a configured fraction of daily ATR for longs, or previous high plus that fraction for shorts. While a trade is open, use the lower of the current long candidate and its value at the most recent entry bar, or the higher of the corresponding short candidates. For ordinary entries, submit the stop and close on a signal only after the configured minimum holding period measured from the most recent entry; delayed long entries have no minimum holding period. These exits require a confirmed bar within the configured exit session. Close longs when the TICK Hull average is below its EMA and averaged RSI is falling, or when enabled regular bearish divergence occurs. Close shorts when the TICK Hull average is above its EMA and averaged RSI is rising, or when enabled regular bullish divergence occurs. Regular divergence compares consecutive confirmed TICK pivot highs or lows within the configured separation range: bearish divergence requires a lower TICK high and higher instrument high; bullish divergence requires a higher TICK low and lower instrument low. Close all positions during the configured end-of-day closing session.
