Instrument traded: the equity index (S&P 500 / ES). External data: a volatility index (VIX) on the same timeframe. Build a ratio series = volatility index close / equity index close. Compute a Wilder RSI on the equity index close. Detect confirmed pivot highs and pivot lows of the ratio with an asymmetric window (more bars on the left than on the right). A pivot is only known after the right-side bars have elapsed, so act on the confirmation bar, but evaluate the pivot bar's own values (the equity close and equity RSI from that earlier bar, not the current bar). Keep separate memory for the last ratio pivot high and the last ratio pivot low. For each, store the ratio value and the equity close at that pivot bar. SHORT signal (distribution divergence): when a new ratio pivot high is confirmed and a previous ratio pivot high exists, go short if all hold: (1) the new ratio pivot high is lower than the previous ratio pivot high; (2) the equity close at the new pivot bar is higher than the equity close at the previous ratio pivot high; (3) the equity RSI at the new pivot bar is above an upper strength threshold (somewhat above neutral, i.e. overbought or strong). Meaning: equity makes a higher high while volatility demand fails to confirm, so expect a pullback. LONG signal (accumulation divergence): when a new ratio pivot low is confirmed and a previous ratio pivot low exists, go long if all hold: (1) the new ratio pivot low is higher than the previous ratio pivot low; (2) the equity close at the new pivot bar is lower than the equity close at the previous ratio pivot low; (3) the equity RSI at the new pivot bar is below a lower weakness threshold (somewhat below neutral, i.e. panic or oversold). Meaning: equity makes a lower low while volatility pressure is exhausting, so expect a bounce. After evaluating each confirmed pivot, whether or not a signal fired, overwrite the stored previous pivot of that type with the new one (ratio, equity close at the pivot bar, bar position). If the previous pivot is not yet available, no signal fires. If the RSI condition fails (neutral zone), discard the signal. Exits: the script defines no separate exit rule. Treat an opposite signal as the exit, or reversal, of the current position. The risk-on/risk-off state (ratio above or below its moving average) is display only and does not drive trades. Intended for medium to higher timeframes.
