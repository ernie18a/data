On each bar, combine weighted scores from the traded instrument’s RSI, impulse MACD, adaptive average, volume, and candle structure with a sector-relative-strength score. Adapt indicator lengths and component weights to the chart timeframe, unless a manual timeframe setting is used. All lengths, weights, thresholds, multipliers, and scoring fractions are configurable parameters.

Score the components as follows, using zero when no listed condition applies:
- RSI: assign a positive score when above its midpoint but below overbought, or when oversold; assign a negative score when below its midpoint but above oversold, or when overbought. Use a larger magnitude for an extreme reading moving back toward the midpoint, a smaller magnitude for an extreme not moving back, and smaller still for a non-extreme reading moving with its assigned direction or against it. Increase the magnitude, capped at its component weight, for the corresponding price/RSI divergence against the previous lookback window. Optionally smooth RSI first.
- Impulse MACD: assign the full positive or negative weight when both a separate price EMA and the MACD histogram are rising or falling, respectively. Otherwise, assign a partial positive score if the histogram is positive and the EMA is rising, or a partial negative score if the histogram is negative and the EMA is falling. Otherwise, assign a smaller positive score for a negative histogram rising above both its prior and earlier value, or a smaller negative score for the symmetric positive-histogram reversal.
- Adaptive average: calculate a recursive average whose smoothing coefficient is the usual EMA coefficient multiplied by the absolute normalized Chande momentum oscillator of the selected source. Initialize it with the source. When its slope and the close’s position relative to it agree, score in their direction, using full weight if the slope is accelerating and a reduced weight otherwise. When they disagree, assign a smaller score in the slope’s direction.
- Volume: suppress this component during abnormally low volume. Otherwise, only score when volume exceeds its moving average and cumulative signed volume is both on the corresponding side of its moving average and moving in that direction. Assign the largest magnitude when the close is in the corresponding half of the bar and volume exceeds a spike threshold, a smaller magnitude when the close is in that half without a spike, and a still smaller magnitude otherwise.
- Candle structure: for a bullish or bearish body larger than the specified fraction of the bar range, score in the body’s direction, using full weight when range relative to ATR is tight and reduced weight otherwise; increase the magnitude, capped at the component weight, for rejection by the corresponding lower or upper wick. For other candles, assign a small positive or negative score only when the corresponding rejection wick is present without the opposite rejection wick.
- Sector: choose the mapped sector ETF for the traded ticker or a manual ETF. Compare its lookback return with a benchmark ETF’s return on the chart timeframe, using data without lookahead, and smooth the difference with an EMA. Give the full positive or negative sector weight beyond the respective relative-strength thresholds; between them, use a proportional score. Apply the configured sector multiplier and cap the result at the component weight. Use zero if required data is missing or an unmapped ticker is configured to be neutral; otherwise use the fallback ETF, reducing its multiplier for a non-US fallback.

Sum the component scores, normalize by the sum of their timeframe-specific weights, and clamp to the permitted composite range. A long signal requires the composite to reach its bullish threshold; a short signal requires it to reach its bearish threshold. Suppress both signals during abnormally low volume. If enabled, also require volume above its moving average and require the RSI, MACD, and adaptive-average scores all to have the signal’s sign. Apply the configured closed-bar restriction.

In strategy mode, enter long on a long signal and short on a short signal; an opposite entry reverses the position. Close a long on a short signal or when the composite falls below its long-exit threshold. Close a short on a long signal or when the composite rises above its short-exit threshold. Apply the same bar-confirmation setting to score-based exits.
