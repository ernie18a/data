On each bar, calculate the current session's volume-weighted average of typical price and the previous day's daily volume-weighted average of typical price; if the latter is unavailable, use the current session value. A downward cross occurs when the close is below the current average and the previous close was at or above its previous value; an upward cross is the reverse. For each direction, score one point each for a candle closing in that direction, the close being on that side of the current average, the close being on that side of the previous-day average, and the corresponding cross. A signal requires the directional score to meet the configured minimum, the corresponding cross, the current VIX close to be within the configured inclusive range, a bar within the configured New York weekday session, and at least the configured number of bars since the last signal; initialize the last signal's bar index to zero. A downward signal sells an out-of-the-money call and buys a higher-strike call. An upward signal sells an out-of-the-money put and buys a lower-strike put, provided there is no downward signal on the same bar. Update the last signal bar after either signal. Choose strikes by rounding the underlying close to the nearest configured strike increment, shifting by the configured out-of-the-money distance in the appropriate direction for the sold option, and placing the bought option one configured spread width farther out. The displayed credit-close percentage is guidance only and does not generate an exit signal.
