Treat bullish signals as call setups and bearish signals as put setups. On each bar, score each direction by adding one point for each: directional stacking of fast, slow, and trend EMAs; close on the directional side of session VWAP; close on the directional side of a cascaded Gaussian-filtered price with the filter rising for bullish or not rising for bearish; MACD histogram on the directional side of zero and moving farther in that direction; RSI between its midpoint and the directional extreme threshold; volume at least its moving average times a configurable multiplier; and a directional trigger. A trigger is a close crossing the fast EMA or VWAP in that direction, or a close crossing the opening-range high or low after the opening window during regular trading hours. Reset the range each day. The implemented opening window uses a fixed quarter-hour for configured durations under half an hour; otherwise its end is constructed using the configured duration as the minute within the opening hour. Require the trigger, a configurable minimum score, the optional session and lunch filters, optional bar confirmation, and a shared cooldown. A caution signal also consumes the cooldown when no directional signal fires: it requires the high to reach the upper VWAP deviation band, RSI at or above its upper threshold, and a bearish candle, or the low to reach the lower band, RSI at or below its lower threshold, and a bullish candle. Calculate band deviation from cumulative volume-weighted squared differences between typical price and session VWAP, without resetting the cumulative sums each session. Caution signals obey the same session, confirmation, and cooldown gates. Directional signals take precedence over caution. No option exit decision is specified; ATR stop and target levels are informational.
