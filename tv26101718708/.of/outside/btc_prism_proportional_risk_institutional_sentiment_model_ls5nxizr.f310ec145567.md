On each daily bar, calculate ten Bitcoin risk inputs: the scaled logarithm of price divided by its long-term simple moving average; market-value-to-realized-value ratio; the logarithm of active addresses; a constant minus the one-year active-supply measure; a weighted moving average of the sum of two price rates of change; price divided by its shorter-term simple moving average; Bitcoin market capitalization divided by the combined market capitalization of the selected stablecoins; the simple moving average of close-location value times volume divided by average volume; the combined share volume of the selected Bitcoin ETFs divided by average Bitcoin volume plus a stabilizing constant; and Bitcoin RSI. Use the source's neutral substitutes for missing inputs and small positive denominators for zero denominators. For each input, calculate both a fixed-range linear normalization and a rolling z-score normalization, clamp each to the pillar score range, substitute the midpoint when unavailable, and blend them using a configurable weight. Sum the ten blended scores and smooth the sum with an ALMA signal line. Mark saturation when the number of bars whose score is at or above the hot threshold within a rolling window reaches the required count. Enter a persistent defensive sell state when the score crosses below its signal line while above the hot threshold, or when the signal-line slope turns from nonnegative to negative while the score is above a higher extreme threshold. Leave that state only when the score falls below a lower cooling threshold; a new sell trigger takes precedence over cooling. An exit has the strongest confirmation when the defensive state and saturation are simultaneously active. The model does not specify a long entry or short entry.
