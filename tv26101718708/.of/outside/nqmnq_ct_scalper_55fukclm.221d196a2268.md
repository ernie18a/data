Trade mean reversion in the instrument only when a directional trend is present, price is sufficiently stretched from its average, liquidity and volatility are adequate, directional confidence is high enough, and higher-timeframe evidence does not show an excessively strong trend against the fade. Block fades when opposing continuation divergence is active. For a downtrend stretched below its mean, consider a long; for an uptrend stretched above its mean, consider a short. Build the stretch and confidence assessments from price distance to moving averages and VWAP, volatility bands, momentum and its deceleration or reversal, RSI/MFI extremes, volume and order-flow exhaustion, wick or candle rejection, swing structure, volume-zone reactions, and cross-market evidence including breadth, volatility, relative strength, and correlated-market divergence. Adapt evidence requirements to session, trend strength, volatility regime, and persistence or mean-reversion conditions. Enter through any enabled confirmation path: exhaustion fade, price/order-flow divergence, rejected liquidity sweep, aggressive flow reversal, absorption, or volume-zone retest. Each path requires its corresponding reversal or flow confirmation; penalize insufficient confirmation when momentum is strong against the trade, especially in thin sessions. Require the projected mean-reversion target to offer a minimum profit. Set a protective stop beyond the recent swing extreme, with a context-adjusted volatility buffer; for sweep entries place it beyond the sweep extreme. Set a single target toward the nearest relevant mean or structural level, extending it with stronger confidence and adjusting its distance for context. Evaluate stop and target hits only after the entry bar; either hit closes the trade.
