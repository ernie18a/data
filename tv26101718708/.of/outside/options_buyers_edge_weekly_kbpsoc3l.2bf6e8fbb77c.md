Anchor an at-the-money strike to the underlying price at the start of the calculation window, or use a manually selected strike. Read call and put prices and volumes across a fixed ladder around that strike for the selected weekly expiry.

Calculate a directional score as a weighted sum of: underlying price change over a lookback divided by ATR and clipped to a symmetric range; futures-minus-spot basis change over a lookback divided by ATR and clipped to the same range; option pressure; and lower-timeframe underlying volume imbalance. For option pressure, sign each leg's volume positive if its price rose from the previous bar and negative otherwise, weight legs by their distance from the anchored strike, sum calls and puts separately, then divide the call sum minus the put sum by the sum of their absolute values. Use zero if that denominator is zero. If flow is disabled, use zero. If skew gating is enabled and skew is available, include pressure only when positive pressure agrees with a nonpositive difference between the out-of-the-money put and call prices, or negative pressure agrees with a nonnegative difference; missing skew permits pressure. Lower-timeframe imbalance is rising-bar volume minus falling-bar volume divided by their sum; use zero when unavailable. Treat an unavailable futures basis as zero contribution.

Estimate implied volatility from the anchored at-the-money call-plus-put premium divided by spot and the square root of time to expiry, using the straddle approximation's scaling factor. Premium is crushing when this estimate is below its exponential moving average. If the estimate is unavailable, do not classify it as crushing.

When the score exceeds the positive direction threshold and premium is not crushing, classify calls as buyable. When it is below the negative threshold and premium is not crushing, classify puts as buyable. When either directional threshold is crossed and premium is crushing, issue caution instead of a buyable classification. With no directional threshold crossed, avoid if time to the automatically calculated weekly expiry is in the extreme decay zone; otherwise avoid if premium is crushing, and wait otherwise. Extreme decay alone does not suppress a directional buyable classification. Calibration statistics, volatility-index behavior, pin, put-call volume ratio, and implied-versus-realized volatility do not gate these classifications. A buyable alert or marker occurs only when the corresponding directional threshold is newly crossed while premium is not crushing.
