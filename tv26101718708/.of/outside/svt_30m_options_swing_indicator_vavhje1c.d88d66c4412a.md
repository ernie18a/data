On each confirmed chart bar, use the traded instrument’s chart, daily, and confirmation-timeframe data. Treat long as a CALL position and short as a PUT position. Calculate fast, medium, and slow EMAs; standard and fast RSI; an SMA of standard RSI; MACD and its signal and histogram; directional movement and ADX; CCI; ATR; and WaveTrend. Calculate WaveTrend by smoothing typical price with an EMA, dividing its deviation from that EMA by a scaled EMA of the absolute deviation, smoothing the result with another EMA, and comparing it with an SMA of that result. Use configurable periods, thresholds, multipliers, and timeframes throughout.

Define daily bullish bias as close above the medium EMA and RSI above its midpoint; bearish is the reverse. Define strong daily bias by additionally requiring the medium EMA above the slow EMA and RSI above a stronger bullish threshold, or the reverse for bearish. Confirmation-timeframe bullish direction requires close above the fast EMA, RSI above its midpoint, and MACD above its signal; bearish is the reverse. Strong confirmation direction instead requires close above the medium EMA, fast EMA above medium EMA, RSI above its midpoint, and MACD above its signal; reverse for bearish. Bullish recovery requires a rising MACD histogram, RSI above a lower recovery threshold, and close above the fast EMA; bearish recovery uses a falling histogram, RSI below an upper recovery threshold, and close below the fast EMA. Compare the confirmation histogram with its previous value as mapped onto chart bars.

On the chart timeframe, bullish trend requires close above the fast EMA and fast EMA at or above the medium EMA; bearish is the reverse. A directional MACD cross is recent for a configurable number of bars. Bullish MACD momentum requires MACD above its signal and a rising histogram; bearish requires the reverse.

Build separate bullish and bearish scores, each awarding one point for each of: standard RSI on the corresponding side of its midpoint; fast RSI on the corresponding side of standard RSI; WaveTrend on the corresponding side of its signal; CCI on the corresponding side of zero; ADX at or above its minimum with the corresponding directional indicator dominant; directional MACD momentum; and close on the corresponding side of both fast and medium EMAs. A score qualifies when it meets the threshold for the selected aggressive, balanced, or safe mode.

A recent CALL pullback exists if, within the configured lookback, a bar’s low touched or fell below either chart EMA or RSI fell below its pullback threshold. A PUT pullback reverses those tests using high and an upper RSI threshold. A CALL reclaim requires close above the fast EMA, above the previous close, and above the open; a PUT rejection reverses all three. Disallow entries extended beyond a configurable ATR distance from the fast EMA in the entry direction.

When enabled, identify chop only when all hold: ADX is below its chop threshold, the fast and medium EMAs are compressed within a configured ATR distance, the recent high-low range is smaller than a configured ATR multiple, and close is strictly inside that range. Permit trading despite chop if close breaks the prior bar’s rolling range high with bullish MACD momentum and RSI above its midpoint, or breaks its low with bearish momentum and RSI below its midpoint.

In safe mode, CALL bias requires daily bullish bias and either bullish confirmation direction or bullish recovery; in other modes any one suffices. PUT bias is symmetric. A CALL pullback entry requires CALL bias, a recent CALL pullback, a recent bullish MACD cross, bullish MACD momentum, a qualifying bullish score, a CALL reclaim, bullish chart trend, no excessive extension, and permission from the chop filter. A PUT pullback entry reverses these conditions. Alternatively, a continuation entry requires strong daily and strong confirmation trends in the entry direction, matching chart trend, a recent directional MACD cross, a qualifying directional score, close on the appropriate side of the fast EMA, no excessive extension, and chop permission; it does not require a pullback, reclaim, or current MACD momentum.

Enter only after both entry and exit cooldowns have elapsed. A CALL can enter when flat or short, unless the confirmation trend is strongly bearish; a PUT can enter when flat or long, unless it is strongly bullish. Close the opposite position when reversing. Record the entry bar, entry ATR, and subsequent highest high and lowest low. Process entries at bar close and do not pyramid.

Maintain an emergency stop at entry price minus an entry-ATR multiple for CALLs, or plus that multiple for PUTs. Once the favorable high or low reaches a configured profit distance in entry ATRs, replace it with the more protective of a small entry-ATR profit above or below entry and a trailing level based on the highest high or lowest low since entry and an entry-ATR multiple. Submit the stop while the position is open.

After the minimum hold period, close a CALL if any of these occurs: a bearish MACD cross coincides with RSI below its average, close below the fast EMA, and a bearish score meeting the exit threshold; the confirmation trend becomes strongly bearish; or close is below the medium EMA while MACD is below its signal and RSI is below a weakness threshold. Reverse every direction for a PUT. If optional no-progress protection is enabled, exit after its waiting period when the favorable extreme has not reached the required entry-ATR move and the confirmation trend is not strongly supportive. Exit either position at the maximum hold time. Record an exit cooldown when a position becomes flat, and clear trade tracking while flat.
