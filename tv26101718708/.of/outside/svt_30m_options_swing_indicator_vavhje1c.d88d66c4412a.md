On each confirmed bar, evaluate the traded instrument on the entry timeframe and on daily and intermediate higher timeframes. Treat the daily trend as bullish when price is above its medium-term average and momentum is positive, and bearish when both are below/negative. The intermediate trend is bullish or bearish when price, momentum, and trend structure agree; also recognize a recovering trend when momentum is improving or weakening while price is on the corresponding side of its short-term average. Use the configured bias mode: ordinary modes allow either daily or intermediate bullish/bearish bias, while the conservative mode requires daily bias plus aligned or recovering intermediate bias. Never enter against a strongly opposing intermediate trend.

Enter long when bullish bias aligns with the entry-timeframe uptrend and bullish momentum: a recent bullish momentum crossover remains active, momentum is strengthening, and a bullish quality score meets its configured requirement. Require either a recent pullback followed by a strong reclaim of the short-term average, or a strong multi-timeframe trend continuation. A pullback is indicated by a recent touch of a relevant average or a momentum dip; a reclaim requires price above the short-term average, rising from the prior bar, and closing above its open. For continuation, require strong daily and intermediate trends, entry-timeframe uptrend, recent bullish crossover, qualifying score, and price above the short-term average. Reject entries when price is excessively extended from that average or a consolidation filter blocks trading, except when a strong momentum breakout clears the recent range. The bearish entry is symmetric: bearish bias and entry-timeframe downtrend, recent and strengthening bearish momentum crossover, qualifying bearish score, and either a recent rejection/pullback followed by a weak close below the short-term average or strong aligned trend continuation; reject excessive extension or blocked consolidation, except for a qualifying downside breakout.

Build each directional quality score from the agreement of standard and faster RSI, WaveTrend, CCI, directional strength, MACD momentum, and price relative to short- and medium-term averages. Count bullish or bearish conditions and require the configured minimum. The consolidation filter blocks trades when directional strength is weak, short- and medium-term averages are compressed, the recent range is tight, and price remains inside that range.

Enter long only when flat or short, and enter short only when flat or long; close an opposite position when reversing. Enforce entry and post-exit cooldowns. For an open long or short, place an emergency stop at a multiple of the entry-timeframe ATR from average entry price. After a favorable move reaches a configured ATR threshold, trail the stop from the best favorable price since entry and keep it at least slightly beyond break-even; otherwise retain the emergency stop. Exit a long after a minimum holding period if a bearish crossover coincides with weakening RSI, price below the short-term average, and a qualifying bearish score; also exit if the intermediate trend turns strongly bearish or price breaks the medium-term average with bearish momentum and weak RSI. Apply the symmetric conditions to shorts. Optionally exit for insufficient favorable progress after a configured holding interval unless the intermediate trend remains strong, and always exit at the maximum holding duration or when the active stop is hit.
