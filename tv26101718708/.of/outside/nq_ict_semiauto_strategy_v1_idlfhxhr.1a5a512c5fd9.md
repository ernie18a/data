Evaluate on each completed bar using the previous day's high and low, confirmed swing pivots, and same-timeframe S&P 500 data. A manual directional bias permits longs when bullish or mixed, shorts when bearish or mixed, and neither when neutral. Trading is restricted to configured sessions and permitted days, while flat and below the daily trade limit.

For a short setup, price must close above the previous day's midpoint by more than a configurable fraction of that day's range. Within a configurable recent-bar window, price must have swept above the greater of the previous day's high and latest confirmed swing high and closed back below it; a large-range, large-body bar must have closed near its low; and a close must have broken below the latest confirmed swing low. These events need not occur in a particular order. The current bar must form a bearish three-bar fair value gap of at least the configured size: its high is below the low two bars earlier. S&P 500 confirmation must also pass: in strict mode its close is below its exponential moving average; in balanced mode that condition or bearish divergence passes; in loose mode it must not be above the average. Bearish divergence means the traded instrument makes a high above its preceding lookback highs while the S&P 500 does not.

For a long setup, apply the mirror image: close sufficiently below the previous day's midpoint; recently sweep below the lesser of the previous day's low and latest confirmed swing low and close back above it; have a large-range, large-body bar close near its high; and close above the latest confirmed swing high. The current bar must have a sufficiently large bullish three-bar gap, with its low above the high two bars earlier. Strict confirmation requires the S&P 500 close above its average; balanced confirmation also accepts a traded-instrument lookback low without an S&P 500 lookback low; loose confirmation requires the S&P 500 not be below its average.

Store the latest qualifying gap and a proposed stop beyond the more extreme of the setup bar and sweep reference, with a configurable tick buffer. Replace an existing same-direction setup when a new one appears and expire it after a configurable number of bars. Enter when a bar overlaps the stored gap, including the setup bar itself, provided the session, directional bias and S&P 500 confirmation still pass, the position is flat, the daily trade limit permits it, and the distance from close to the proposed stop is greater than one tick and no greater than the configured maximum. Clear the corresponding gap on entry. Reset the trade count at each new daily bar and increment it when a position opens.

On entry, set the stop to the stored proposed stop and the profit target to a configurable multiple of the initial entry-to-stop risk. If price reaches a configurable favorable risk multiple, move the stop to entry if it is not already more favorable. Exit the entire position at the stop or target.
