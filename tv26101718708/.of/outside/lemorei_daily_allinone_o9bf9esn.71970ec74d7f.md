Use regular-session daily data for the traded instrument and a benchmark. Relative strength is the instrument's return minus the benchmark's return over a configurable lookback. Calculate daily exponential moving averages at short, medium, and long horizons, weekly and monthly anchored VWAPs, average true range, the day's range and close location within that range, and the close's distance below the day's high in average-true-range units. Calculate same-time relative volume during live regular hours as cumulative volume divided by its historical average at the same intraday slot; treat it as unavailable outside live regular hours.

Classify the day independently: breakdown if the close is below the previous low, or below the previous close and near the day's low; weakening if there is no breakdown, the close is below the open, and close location is low; healthy if there is no breakdown, the close is at or above the previous close, and close location is sufficiently high; strong if the close exceeds both the previous close and open and close location is high. Classify stretch as dangerous if the range is very large and close location is low, close location is extremely low, or the close has fallen far enough from the high relative to average true range.

For long scalping, prohibit longs if there is a breakdown, a down day closing near its low, price is below the long moving average while relative strength is negative, or stretch is dangerous. Otherwise, permit longs if price is above the long moving average, relative strength is nonnegative, and the day is strong, healthy, or an up day closing at least halfway up its range; during live regular hours, available relative volume must also meet a minimum. Classify all remaining cases as selective.

For holding through the regular close, add configurable point awards for day classification, moving-average trend, relative-strength tier, position relative to the two anchored VWAPs, position relative to the previous high, close and low, range relative to average true range, and directional relative volume. Evaluate each factor's tiers in descending order. Strong and healthy days receive more volume points as relative volume rises; breakdown and weakening days receive fewer as it rises; mixed days use a separate scale. Use a fixed neutral volume award when volume is unavailable. A breakdown closing near the low vetoes holding; otherwise classify the score against descending thresholds as strong, allowed, caution, or no hold.

For after-hours holding, use a separate weighted score based on day classification, moving-average trend, relative-strength tier, anchored VWAP position, close location, previous-session levels, and directional relative volume, with a neutral volume award when unavailable. Veto holding if the close is below the previous low, the close is below the medium moving average on a weakening day, or the day is a breakdown. Otherwise classify the score as allowed, caution, or no hold using descending thresholds.

For overnight or swing holding, use another weighted score based on moving-average trend, day classification, relative-strength tier, anchored VWAP position, previous-session levels, and close location; exclude volume. Veto holding if the close is below the previous low or the long moving average, the moving averages and price are fully bearishly ordered, relative strength is below a negative threshold, or the day is a breakdown. Otherwise classify the score as qualified, conditional, or no hold using descending thresholds. For all scores, preserve separate configurable weights, tier boundaries, and decision thresholds for each factor and holding horizon.
