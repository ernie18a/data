Trade the charted futures instrument using its own price, volume, order flow and higher-timeframe data, with these external-market inputs:

- Compare its rate of change with SPY. Add bullish or bearish weight to the directional score when relative strength exceeds the corresponding threshold.
- Compare confirmed swing highs and lows with ES futures. A higher high in the traded instrument without a higher ES high is bearish divergence; a lower low without a lower ES low is bullish divergence. Retain each divergence for a session- and trend-dependent period. Add its signed weight to the directional score and to opening-drive confidence.
- Add signed, magnitude-weighted NYSE TICK breadth and inverse VIX change to the directional score. Scale the VIX contribution by its level. Combine these with the strategy's weighted price, trend, momentum, structure and order-flow factors; adapt weights and veto thresholds to session, regime, trend strength, volatility and flow. Block entries when this score sufficiently opposes their direction. Opening-drive entries instead require a nonnegative score for longs or a nonpositive score for shorts.
- Map nearby QQQ round-number levels into futures prices using the current futures-to-QQQ price ratio. Include enabled mapped levels among support and resistance candidates. They can change the available room-to-support test for shorts, the profit target, and the structural stop based on the previously calculated nearest level.

Evaluate entry paths in priority order: trend breakout with opening-range, support/resistance or retracement confirmation; structure break; order-block displacement; trend pullback; liquidity-sweep reversal; then opening drive. Apply each path's own confluence, directional, structural, trend-alignment, overextension and session gates, and require its calculated target to meet the minimum profit distance. Opening drive is allowed only while flat; other paths do not enter against an existing opposite position. Permit at most one entry per direction per bar. Set path-specific stop and target orders, and exit when either is hit. Pattern-based smart exits are disabled.
