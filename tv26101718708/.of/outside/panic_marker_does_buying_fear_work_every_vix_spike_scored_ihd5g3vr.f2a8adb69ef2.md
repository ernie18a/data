Long-only event rule on the traded instrument, driven by an external fear gauge (a volatility index, evaluated on daily closes regardless of chart timeframe). Panic condition: the gauge's daily close is at or above a fixed threshold. Episode start: a panic day counts as a new episode only if no panic day occurred in the preceding calm window (a set number of consecutive sessions all below the threshold). While the gauge stays at or above the threshold, or returns to it before the calm window completes, the episode continues and no new signal fires; every panic day resets the calm counter. Entry: when a new episode starts, go long the traded instrument at the next session's open, because the gauge closes after the stock market. Exit/evaluation: the long is scored at two fixed horizons counted in daily sessions from that entry open, a shorter one and a longer one, using the daily close at each horizon. The longer horizon is the effective holding period, and the position is a win if the close is at or above the entry price. Worst drawdown is tracked using daily lows until the longer horizon. There is no short side and no stop. Only one position per episode, and episodes can overlap in evaluation. Baseline comparison: the same forward-return test from every session's open, panic or not, to show the unconditional win rate.
