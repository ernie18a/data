Backtest each candidate on each distinct configured asset and timeframe over the configured history. Compound equity using the previous bar's position times the current close-to-close return. Rank each asset–candidate pair by its selected performance metric averaged across available timeframes; for drawdown, rank smaller drawdowns higher. The default metric is the slope of log equity against time multiplied by its regression R-squared. Select the highest-ranked distinct candidates, or a manually selected candidate when overridden. Apply each selected candidate to the traded instrument at its chart timeframe, regardless of which asset produced the ranking. Confidence does not filter signals.

Candidate positions are long, short, or flat. Unless otherwise specified, retain the previous position when no condition fires. In long-only mode, a short signal exits; in short-only mode, a long signal exits. When both signals occur in the same bar, the long signal takes precedence unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Candidates:
- Always long; always short; or independently choose a random long, flat, or short position each bar. These three ignore the side setting.
- Standard-deviation bands: either go long when close crosses up through the lower band and short when it crosses down through the upper band; use those entries with exits when close crosses the middle band in the position's favorable direction; or go long above the upper band, short below the lower band, and flat inside. In the middle-band version, exits take precedence over entries.
- Volatility channel: go long above an exponential-average-plus-average-true-range channel and short below its lower channel.
- Two simple averages: long when the fast average is at least the slow average, otherwise short; disallowed directions are flat.
- Moving-average convergence/divergence: long when its line is at least its signal line, otherwise short; disallowed directions are flat.
- Momentum: long when close exceeds its configured lagged close and short when below it.
- Price-channel breakout: long when the high exceeds the highest high of the preceding lookback, short when the low falls below its lowest low.
- Relative strength: either go long on an upward crossing of the lower threshold and short on a downward crossing of the upper threshold; use those entries but exit on an upward crossing of the upper threshold or downward crossing of the lower threshold, with exits taking precedence; or go long above the upper threshold, short below the lower threshold, and flat between them.
- Random-walk measures: for both fast and slow lookbacks compute ((current high minus lagged low) minus (lagged high minus current low)) divided by lagged high-low range times the square root of the lookback. Go long when the fast measure is positive and the slow is nonnegative; short when the fast is negative and the slow nonpositive.
- Outside candle: when its high is at least the previous high and its low at most the previous low, go long if it closes above its open and short if below.
- Consecutive closes: go long after the configured number of successive rising closes, short after the configured number of successive falling closes; an unchanged close resets both counts.
- Standard volatility-based trailing trend: long in its bullish direction and short in its bearish direction.
- Composite technical rating: assign bullish, bearish, or neutral scores to simple and exponential averages at several horizons, a hull average, a volume-weighted average, and an Ichimoku configuration; average these as the moving-average group. Separately average scores from relative strength, stochastic, commodity channel, directional movement, awesome oscillator, momentum change, moving-average convergence/divergence, stochastic relative strength, Williams percent range, bull/bear power, and ultimate oscillator. Select either group or the equal-weight average of both. Go long above the selected positive rating threshold, short below its negative counterpart, and flat otherwise; a strong-only setting uses stricter thresholds. Each component uses its configured periods and thresholds: averages score by close relative to the average; the Ichimoku score requires aligned cloud, price and conversion/base relationships; oscillators score their respective extreme, crossover, direction or trend conditions. Momentum decrease scores bullish and increase bearish; ultimate oscillator above its upper threshold scores bullish and below its lower threshold bearish.
- Parabolic stop-and-reverse: go long when close crosses above the stop and short when it crosses below.
- Smoothed stochastic: go long when the fast smoothed line crosses above the slow line while below the oversold threshold; short on a downward cross while above the overbought threshold.
- Wick imbalance: sum, over the lookback, each candle's upper wick minus lower wick divided by scaled average true range. Go long above the positive threshold and short below the negative threshold. Exit on a zero crossing, with the exit taking precedence over entries.

For position-recomputing candidates, disallowed directions are flat. Emit a long or short entry when the selected candidate changes into that position, suppressing repeated same-direction entries until an opposite entry or a flat transition resets the state. Process live entries at confirmed bar close.
