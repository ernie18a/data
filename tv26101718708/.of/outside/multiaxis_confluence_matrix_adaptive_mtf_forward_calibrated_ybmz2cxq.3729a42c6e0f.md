On each chart bar, use the previous confirmed bars of a signal timeframe and a higher bias timeframe. Each enabled axis casts a bullish, bearish, or neutral vote: the sign of the traded instrument’s MACD histogram; the sign of its close minus an EMA fair value, normalized by the deviation’s standard deviation; the sign of cumulative signed volume minus its EMA, where signed volume is estimated from the normal CDF of standardized price changes; the opposite of an implied-volatility instrument’s close-minus-EMA trend; and, optionally, the sign of a weighted average of correlation-adjusted, normalized momentum from external leader instruments. Treat unavailable external readings as neutral, and omit disabled axes or axes whose symbols are blank.

Optionally calibrate each active axis using repeated nonzero signal-timeframe votes sampled on confirmed chart bars. Record the vote direction, chart close, and ATR; resolve each sample against ATR-based profit and stop barriers or a time limit, counting a simultaneous stop and target as a loss. Weight each sample by the inverse of one plus the number of already pending axis samples. Separately sample long and short baseline entries at regular intervals and resolve them with the same barriers. For each axis, calculate its recency-decayed, uniqueness-weighted win rate, subtract the Wilson interval margin and the unweighted average of the direction-matched baseline win rates recorded when its samples resolved. Before enough samples exist, use the minimum axis weight; afterward use that minimum plus any positive lower-bound edge. If calibration is disabled, weight all active axes equally.

Divide the weighted sum of signal-timeframe votes by the sum of all active axis weights, including neutral votes. Its sign is the proposed direction. Count the active signal-timeframe axes that agree with that direction. Conviction is the absolute score scaled down optionally by the percentile rank of realized price-change volatility and by low price trend efficiency, then rounded. Sum the active bias-timeframe votes without weights; if the bias gate is enabled, a nonzero bias sum blocks an opposing proposed direction, while a zero sum permits either side.

Signal long or short when the proposed direction is nonzero, its agreeing-axis count and conviction reach their respective thresholds, and the bias gate does not block it. A shared cooldown limits consecutive signals. After a signal in one direction, suppress another signal in that direction until the raw proposed direction becomes neutral or reverses; a reversal can signal once the cooldown has elapsed. Calibration warm-up changes the displayed verdict but does not prevent these signals. There is no explicit exit rule.
