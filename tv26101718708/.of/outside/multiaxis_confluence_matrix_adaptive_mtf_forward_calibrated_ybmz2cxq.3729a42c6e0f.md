On confirmed bars, evaluate enabled directional inputs on trigger, signal, and higher timeframes. On the signal timeframe, derive votes from price momentum (sign of the MACD histogram), price stretch (sign of price’s deviation from an adaptive average), signed-volume flow (sign of normalized cumulative signed volume), implied volatility (opposite the sign of its deviation from an average), and optionally correlated leader markets (momentum direction adjusted for correlation and combined by configured weights). Treat each enabled input as one independent vote. Weight each vote by its forward-measured edge: compare its triple-barrier win rate with a direction-matched baseline, use the conservative lower confidence bound of that excess edge, and apply a minimum weight floor; before enough outcomes are available, use the floor. Compute the weighted net vote as the difference between bullish and bearish weight divided by total weight. Its sign sets direction; count the enabled inputs agreeing with that direction separately. Scale conviction, based on the absolute net vote, downward for elevated realized-volatility regimes and low trend efficiency. Determine the allowed direction from the net votes on the higher timeframe. A neutral higher-timeframe result allows either side; otherwise block signals opposite its direction. Signal long or short when direction is nonzero, conviction meets its configured minimum, enough independent inputs agree, and the higher-timeframe gate does not block it. Emit a fresh marker only when the signal state is armed and its cooldown has elapsed; re-arm after direction becomes neutral or reverses. The indicator defines no exit decision.
