On each confirmed chart bar, use the external market-breadth index close, high and low from a configurable timeframe and the volatility index close. Calculate a fast EMA of breadth close, a Hull moving average of breadth close and an SMA of that Hull average. Calculate the traded instrument's RSI, a WMA of that RSI, a smoothed stochastic RSI, a close EMA and daily ATR. All periods, thresholds, windows, offsets and feature switches are configurable.

A bullish setup occurs when the breadth Hull average crosses above its SMA, the smoothed RSI is above its midpoint, the unsmoothed RSI is below an upper limit, volatility is above its threshold, the breadth EMA is positive and price is above its EMA. A bearish setup mirrors these conditions. If stochastic timing is enabled and there is no open trade, then during the configured lookback after a setup, replace that direction's setup signal with a rising stochastic RSI below its upper entry threshold for long, or a falling stochastic RSI above its lower entry threshold for short. Outside that lookback, use the original setup.

Optionally enter from a regular breadth divergence when flat: a bullish divergence is a confirmed breadth pivot low higher than the preceding qualifying breadth pivot low while the instrument low at that pivot is lower; a bearish divergence reverses these comparisons at pivot highs. Require the pivots to be within the configured spacing range. For a bullish divergence entry, also require the breadth Hull average above its SMA, RSI above its WMA, volatility above threshold, breadth EMA positive and price above its EMA; reverse the directional comparisons for bearish divergence.

Optionally chase a recent exit when flat: for a long, price must be above its price on the exit bar, the breadth Hull average above its SMA and rising, smoothed RSI above its midpoint, unsmoothed RSI below its upper limit, volatility above threshold, breadth EMA rising, price above its EMA and stochastic RSI below its chase threshold. Reverse these directional comparisons for a short. The exit must be within the configured chase lookback.

Submit long or short entries only inside the configured date and entry sessions, outside any optional intraday pause, and when strategy signals are enabled. Divergence and chase entries require a flat position; ordinary entries may pyramid or reverse subject to the strategy's position rules.

After the latest entry has been held longer than the configured minimum, and only during the configured exit session, place a stop and optionally a profit target. At entry, the long stop is the recent lowest low minus a configured fraction of daily ATR; thereafter use the lower of that entry-time stop and the previous bar's low minus the current ATR fraction. The short stop mirrors this using the recent highest high and the higher of the entry-time stop and previous high plus the ATR fraction. Set the target at the first open trade's entry price plus the configured ATR fraction for long, or minus it for short.

During that same eligible exit period, close a long if the breadth Hull average is below its SMA and the smoothed RSI is falling, if a regular bearish divergence is confirmed, or if breadth high exceeds its upper extreme. Close a short for the mirrored reversal, a regular bullish divergence, or breadth low below its lower extreme. Divergence detection for exits follows its respective enable switch.

At the configured end-of-day time, close all positions unless the optional overnight rule applies; with that rule, close half on the configured overnight weekdays and all on the other configured weekdays.
