Standardize available momentum, trend, location, volatility, volume-flow, and cross-asset readings; average related readings into aspects, then aspects into six factor families and an overall composite. Treat a family as stretched when any aspect reaches an extreme. Flag bullish or bearish divergence when price makes a lower or equal low while the composite makes a materially higher low, or price makes a higher or equal high while the composite makes a materially lower high; also flag hidden divergences when price and composite make the corresponding continuation pattern. Other directional signals are opposing price and composite regression slopes, rolling price-composite correlation turning negative while price slopes in that direction, RSI exhaustion after a sustained move beyond its extreme zone, and a split among aspects within a family while the composite is extreme. Count families at extremes for consensus and compare family readings at successive price pivots for divergence consensus. Treat bullish divergence and exhaustion signals as long hypotheses, and bearish ones as short hypotheses; interpret extreme consensus as a fade or continuation according to the selected mode. These are indicator signals and context, not a complete trading rule: no entry combination policy or exit condition is defined.
