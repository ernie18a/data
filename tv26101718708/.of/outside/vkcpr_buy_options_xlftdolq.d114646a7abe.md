On each bar, calculate the central pivot from the previous completed pivot-period high, low, and close. Calculate the two central-pivot-range boundaries as the high-low midpoint and its reflection across the central pivot; sort them into upper and lower boundaries. Use a configured pivot timeframe, selected manually or by a mapping from the chart timeframe.

On enabled weekdays and within the configured date range, buy a call when the chosen price source crosses above the upper boundary, provided calls are enabled and the current position is not already a call. Buy a put when the source crosses below the lower boundary, provided puts are enabled and the current position is not already a put. A cross compares both the source and the boundary with their respective previous-bar values. An opposite signal exits the existing option position and replaces it; repeated same-type signals do nothing. There is no other exit rule.

Select the call and put strike reference levels using the configured method: the central pivot for both, traditional resistance and support derived from the pivot and prior low and high, or a configured Camarilla resistance and support derived from the prior close and range. Round the call reference up and the put reference down to the configured strike interval, which may instead be selected automatically from price bands.
