Build an adaptive price baseline by blending simple and volume-weighted price averages, with smoothing that increases as price movement becomes more directionally efficient. Form upper and lower thresholds by offsetting the baseline by a smoothed volatility measure. Classify the higher-timeframe trend by comparing its last completed close with its trend average. Measure directional money flow from price changes weighted by volume, limiting unusually large volume bars. For equities, treat volatility-index readings below their trend average as supportive of longs and readings above it as supportive of shorts.

Enter or maintain a bullish regime when the instrument closes above the upper threshold, money flow is positive, the higher-timeframe trend is up, and the volatility filter supports longs; seek long entries on pullbacks toward the baseline. Enter or maintain a bearish regime when it closes below the lower threshold, money flow is negative, the higher-timeframe trend is down, and the volatility filter supports shorts; seek shorts or puts. Otherwise retain the prior regime unless directional efficiency becomes low or money flow turns against that regime, in which case switch to neutral. When neutral, avoid entries; if already positioned, exit or trim.
