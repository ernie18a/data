Use synchronized closing prices for the chart instrument and comparison instruments. Treat lookbacks, thresholds, score multipliers, and position sizes as parameters.

For each chart/comparison pair, calculate the rolling Pearson correlation of prices, its historical z-score and percentile rank, correlation stability from its historical standard deviation, and the rolling z-score of the chart-price/comparison-price ratio. Issue a pair direction only if, in priority order, there is no correlation break, no strong positive or negative correlation passing the R² and stability gates, and no extreme correlation percentile. Then, if the absolute ratio z-score exceeds its threshold, stability exceeds its minimum, and absolute correlation exceeds its minimum, short the chart instrument and long the comparison instrument when the ratio z-score is positive; reverse both legs when it is negative.

For each ordered pair of comparison instruments, calculate price correlation, correlation stability, and the z-score of the first price divided by the second. Among pairs with sufficiently large absolute correlation and positive stability times absolute z-score, rank by that product and show the highest-ranked pairs. For a positive ratio z-score, short the first instrument and long the second; for a negative z-score, reverse the legs. A separate ranked action list requires the product to exceed a threshold and a scaled version of it to pass a quality threshold. Boost that score when rolling log-price regression residuals have a sufficiently negative autoregressive change coefficient; then rank the eligible actions with the other action candidates.

For hedging a long chart position, calculate the rolling covariance of chart and comparison log returns divided by the variance of chart log returns. If this beta exceeds a positive threshold, short the comparison instrument; if it is below the corresponding negative threshold, long it. Reverse the hedge direction for a short chart position. The suggested hedge notional is absolute beta times the chart position notional. The ranked action list also recommends a long comparison position alongside the chart position when their price correlation is sufficiently positive and their quality score passes its threshold; for sufficiently negative correlation and qualifying quality, it recommends the beta-directed hedge. Quality combines absolute price correlation, squared correlation, and correlation stability, with adjustments for correlation breaks and basket stress.
