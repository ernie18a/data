For each enabled instrument, use its manually supplied gamma-derived chop boundaries and entry levels. Evaluate price and volume on each enabled signal timeframe, with optional confirmation from an external market-breadth index. When confirmation is enabled, require the index to be at or above a configured threshold for bullish signals and at or below its negation for bearish signals.

With both chop boundaries positive, issue a bullish reclaim signal when the prior close is below the lower boundary and the current close reaches or exceeds it. If the optional exhaustion filter is enabled, also require prior volume to exceed its own moving average by a configured multiple and current volume to fall below a configured fraction of prior volume. Issue a bullish continuation signal when the prior close is below the upper boundary, the current close is above it, and a prior close within the configured lookback was below the lower boundary. Issue a bearish reclaim signal when the prior close is above the upper boundary and the current close reaches or falls below it. Issue a bearish continuation signal when the prior close is above the lower boundary, the current close is below it, and a prior close within the lookback was above the upper boundary. Both bearish signals require a prior close within the lookback above the upper boundary. Continuation and bearish signals optionally require current volume to exceed its moving average by a configured multiple. Apply the appropriate breadth confirmation to each signal. When signals coincide, prioritize bullish reclaim, bullish continuation, bearish reclaim, then bearish continuation; within each type prioritize the shorter timeframe.

Independently, signal a long entry when the shorter-timeframe close crosses from below a positive supplied long entry level to at least that level. Signal a short entry when it crosses from above a positive supplied short entry level to at most that level. These entry signals do not require the chop, volume, or breadth conditions. Display a suggested stop a configured distance below the long entry level or above the short entry level; the script does not execute exits.
