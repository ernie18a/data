Choose a pivot timeframe, automatically from the chart timeframe or manually. From the preceding pivot-period high, low and close, calculate the central pivot as their average. The central pivot range boundaries are the midpoint of the high and low and its reflection across the central pivot, ordered lower to upper. Calculate traditional first support as twice the central pivot minus the high, and first resistance as twice the central pivot minus the low.

On the chart bar immediately after the first regular-session bar of each pivot period, if that day is enabled and the bar is within the configured date range, compare the selected source on the first bar with that bar's pivot range. Above the upper boundary, sell a put spread; below the lower boundary, sell a call spread; strictly between the boundaries, sell an iron condor. At either boundary, make no decision.

Calculate strikes on the decision bar using its close and a configurable strike increment, chosen manually or from price-based tiers. Round call strikes upward and put strikes downward to an increment; buy the protective option one increment farther out than the sold strike.

For a call spread, select a support and an upper reference using either traditional first pivots, a configured Camarilla pivot level, or a manual offset from the central pivot. For the pivot methods, the upper reference is the greater of the prior high and selected resistance; for the manual method it is the central pivot plus the offset, and the support is the central pivot. If the decision-bar close is below the selected support, use the upper pivot-range boundary as the unrounded call strike; otherwise use the average of the central pivot and upper reference. Sell the rounded call and buy the higher call.

For a put spread, use the corresponding resistance and lower reference. For the pivot methods, the lower reference is the lesser of the prior low and selected support; for the manual method it is the central pivot minus the offset, and the resistance is the central pivot. If the decision-bar close is above the selected resistance, use the lower pivot-range boundary as the unrounded put strike; otherwise use the average of the central pivot and lower reference. Sell the rounded put and buy the lower put. Camarilla levels are the preceding close plus or minus the preceding high-low range multiplied by the configured level's coefficient.

For an iron condor, sell a call at traditional first resistance rounded upward and a put at traditional first support rounded downward; buy the call one increment higher and the put one increment lower. No early exit rule is specified.
