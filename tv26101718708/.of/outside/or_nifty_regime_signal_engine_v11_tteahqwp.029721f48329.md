On each chart bar, calculate the underlying’s ATR-normalized distance from its EMA. Maintain a persistent neutral, bullish, or bearish state: from neutral, enter bullish above the bullish entry threshold or bearish below the bearish entry threshold; from bullish, return to neutral below the bullish exit threshold; from bearish, return to neutral above the bearish exit threshold. A reversal must pass through neutral. Classify realized volatility as expanding, compressing, or normal using the ratio of short- to long-window standard deviations of log returns; an unavailable ratio is normal.

Use the current daily India VIX to select a base lot size from descending volatility bands; missing or extreme VIX gives zero lots. Multiply by the selected drawdown-band factor and, if enabled, a reduced factor when directional bias and expanding volatility coincide. Floor the result to whole lots; zero lots halts trading.

When base lots are zero, select no trade. Otherwise, bullish bias with normal or expanding volatility selects a short put spread; bearish bias with normal or expanding volatility selects a short call spread. Either directional bias with compressing volatility, or neutral bias with normal volatility, selects an iron condor. Neutral bias with compressing volatility selects a wider iron condor; neutral bias with expanding volatility selects no trade. For each selected side, sell an option at a strike calculated by subtracting an ATR multiple from spot for puts or adding an ATR multiple for calls, rounding puts down and calls up to the strike increment. Buy the protective option one wing width farther out. Use separate configured ATR multiples for directional spreads, ordinary condors, and wide condors.

For an active put or call side, flag a stop when spot reaches the configured fraction of the distance from entry spot to that side’s short strike; confirm with a five-minute candle, then exit the threatened leg. Close all positions if current daily VIX reaches the extreme threshold, or if it reaches the shock threshold and its increase from the previous daily close exceeds the shock-rate threshold.
