When the traded instrument has not reported positive volume, use the configured external symbol's volume; otherwise use its own. Use the configured high, low and close sources for structure. Maintain confirmed swing highs and lows and a persistent directional bias. Change the bias when the source close crosses a confirmed swing level and passes the enabled displacement, close-position and pivot-quality filters; a reversal must also cross the protected swing when that filter applies.

On each validated break, find the most recent opposing candle within the search window and create an order block in the break direction. Score its formation using the break's ATR-normalized displacement, the origin candle's body proportion and volume relative to its recent history, and whether a same-direction liquidity sweep occurred recently. Retain only blocks meeting the quality threshold; optionally merge overlapping blocks on the same side and limit the retained set by formation quality. Mark a block as tested when a bar overlaps it, and remove it when the close breaks its far edge.

On the latest bar, consider only untested blocks whose direction agrees with the structure bias or a neutral bias and whose current source close is in the corresponding half of the latest swing range: below the midpoint for long, above it for short. Score each eligible block from its formation quality, the number of nearby reference levels, its volume-profile node, range-half alignment, higher-timeframe and structure alignment, momentum reversal confirmation, and regime. Optionally reduce the score for age and testing, and adjust it using the distance-weighted success rate of sufficiently many previously resolved same-direction zones with similar quality, structure, regime, efficiency, regime changes and volume. Historical success means that, at the evaluation horizon after first touch, the close has moved sufficiently far from the zone midpoint in its direction, measured in ATR. Select the eligible block with the highest positive score, retaining the earlier block on a tie; its direction is the long or short setup. If none qualifies, output no setup. An alert or reference trade map additionally requires its respective score threshold.

For a reference trade map, use the near edge of the selected block as entry and the far edge plus an ATR-based buffer as stop. Choose the nearest and second-nearest eligible levels in the trade direction from the range midpoint, liquidity levels, untested session volume points of control, swing levels, rolling volume point of control and fair-value-gap midpoints. If no target qualifies, use an ATR-based target derived from historical order-block reaction strength or its fallback. The script does not define an executed exit decision.
