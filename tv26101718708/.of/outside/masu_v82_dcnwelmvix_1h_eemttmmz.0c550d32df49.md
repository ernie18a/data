On each bar, consider a position only when flat, within the enabled trading session, and the equity guard is inactive. A long setup is any of: a bullish fast/slow EMA crossover with strong ADX, price above the long-term EMA, and elevated volume; a close above the prior lookback high with elevated volume and price above the long-term EMA; in a range, a close below the lower Bollinger Band followed by an RSI recovery from oversold; or a confirmed close above the prior Donchian upper band with elevated volume and price above the long-term EMA. Short setups mirror these conditions: bearish EMA crossover; close below the prior low; in a range, close above the upper Bollinger Band followed by RSI falling from overbought; or confirmed close below the prior Donchian lower band.

A setup must also pass every enabled directional gate: higher-timeframe trend agrees (fast EMA above/below slow EMA and higher-timeframe close on the matching side of the fast EMA); most adjacent pairs in a Gaussian-smoothed price ribbon are ordered in the trade direction; price is on the matching side of the Donchian midpoint; order flow passes (price on the matching side of daily VWAP, with either matching cumulative volume delta relative to its average and direction, or matching buy/sell volume imbalance); and a matching smart-money pattern exists (order block, fair-value gap, or break of structure; liquidity sweeps contribute to scoring but do not alone satisfy this gate). An enabled neural filter can veto a trade when its learned next-period direction strongly opposes it. Extreme volatility-index fear can optionally veto longs. A risk-on broad-market proxy is required for longs when that filter is enabled; shorts have no corresponding breadth restriction.

Score each direction by adding weighted votes for its higher-timeframe trend, ribbon ordering, order flow, smart-money signals, trend strength, neural prediction, fastest ribbon-line slope, Donchian midpoint position, broad-market condition, and volatility-index sentiment. Calibrate the neural vote weight by its recent prediction accuracy. Require the setup's score to meet the minimum confluence threshold; shared trend-strength votes can support either direction. Enter only the qualifying direction with the higher score.

For an open position, take a partial profit at an ATR-based favorable move. Protect the remainder with a regime-adjusted ATR stop (or the opposite Donchian boundary when enabled); use a trailing stop after a fraction of the regime-based target distance is reached, otherwise use the regime-based profit target. The volatility regime is based on ATR percentile and trend strength: trending regimes use wider stops and targets than ranging regimes, with high-volatility ranges using intermediate distances. Pause new entries after a consecutive-loss limit or peak-equity drawdown limit, with a bar-based cooldown. Position size scales down with weaker confluence and with fearful or sharply rising volatility-index conditions, and scales up with greed conditions.
