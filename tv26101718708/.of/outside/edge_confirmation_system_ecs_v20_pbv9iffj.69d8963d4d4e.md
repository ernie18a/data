Apply the signals to options on the charted instrument. Calculate a trend from three close-price exponential moving averages: bullish when close is above the base average and the fast average is above the slow average; bearish when both comparisons are reversed. Calculate momentum as the percentage difference between a separate fast and slow close-price exponential average, normalized over a rolling window to a range from negative to positive one; use zero if the rolling range is zero. Smooth the normalized value with an exponential average. Momentum is bullish when positive and above its smoothed value, and bearish when negative and below it. Define overbought and oversold using upper and lower momentum thresholds. A directional volume surge occurs when volume reaches a multiple of its rolling average and the candle closes respectively above or below its open.

Buy calls when trend and momentum are bullish, momentum is below the overbought threshold, and, if the volume filter is enabled, there is a bullish volume surge. Buy puts when trend and momentum are bearish, momentum is above the oversold threshold, and, if the filter is enabled, there is a bearish volume surge. Emit an entry only if the previous entry was not in the same direction. Remember the last entry direction; exits do not reset it.

After updating the remembered direction for any entry on the bar, signal an exit from calls if the remembered direction is bullish and momentum is bearish, or momentum is overbought and below its smoothed value. Signal an exit from puts if the remembered direction is bearish and momentum is bullish, or momentum is oversold and above its smoothed value. Exit signals can recur on subsequent bars.
