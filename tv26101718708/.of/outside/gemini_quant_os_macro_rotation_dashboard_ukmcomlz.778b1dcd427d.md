Using external 2-year and 10-year yields, compare their spread and changes over a lookback with configured flat, positive-spread, spread-rise, and 10-year-rebound thresholds. Classify the regime in priority order: bad steepening or a sufficiently large 10-year yield rebound means risk phase (reduce bond duration and tighten risk); otherwise, a sufficiently positive spread with good steepening means rotate from bonds toward equities; otherwise, a flat or inverted curve that is repairing means build bonds; otherwise, an inverted curve means defensive phase; else neutral. Good steepening requires a sufficiently rising spread, a falling 2-year yield, and a non-rising 10-year yield or a smaller 10-year move. Bad steepening requires a sufficiently rising spread, a rising 10-year yield, and a 10-year move large enough relative to the 2-year move. Use the regime to tilt suggested exposure among bonds, equities, gold, and cash toward bonds during curve repair, equities during good steepening, and defensive assets during risk or inverted-curve regimes. If the external equity index drawdown from its lookback high crosses the warning threshold, reduce equity exposure and increase cash and gold; at the hard threshold, apply a stronger risk-off allocation. When enabled, if the regime is inverted or risk phase, or the equity drawdown is in warning, and external gold momentum is positive, shift exposure from equities toward gold. Normalize suggested exposures to sum to the portfolio total.
