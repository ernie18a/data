First classify the market as balanced or imbalanced from its volume profile. In balance, short a rejection at the upper value-area boundary and target the point of control; buy a rejection at the lower boundary and target the point of control. If price breaks beyond a value-area boundary and holds outside it while volume builds there, trade in the breakout direction on a pullback to the broken boundary, targeting the next significant volume level. If price breaks out but returns into the prior value area without building volume outside it, trade back toward the point of control, potentially targeting the opposite boundary. Use higher-timeframe regime and volume levels to set directional bias and targets, with lower-timeframe levels for entry; favor continuation in an imbalanced regime and reversion in a balanced one. In a double-distribution profile, avoid fading the low-volume gap and wait for price to cross it and react at a high-volume area. When real related futures volume is available for a forex or CFD chart, use its profile levels to assess participation and prioritize them when they differ from broker-volume levels.
