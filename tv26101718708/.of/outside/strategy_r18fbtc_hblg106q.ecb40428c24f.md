When flat, compute a fast-minus-slow EMA oscillator and its EMA signal from a selected reference instrument. A bullish signal occurs when the oscillator crosses above its signal while below a scaled recent oscillator low, the reference price is below its moving average and the midpoint of its recent high-low range, and its RSI is below its own moving average. Enter long on that signal. A bearish signal uses the mirrored conditions: cross below the signal while above a scaled recent oscillator high, with price and RSI above their respective averages and price above the range midpoint; enter short. An optional reverse mode swaps the long and short actions. Exit an open position when its entry-relative stop-loss or take-profit level is reached.
