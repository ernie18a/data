Evaluate long and short signals separately on each confirmed bar. Use two configurable comparison instruments at the traded instrument's timeframe. When a confirmed pivot high in the traded instrument exceeds its previous pivot high while either comparison instrument's high at that pivot is lower than at the previous traded-instrument pivot high, record a bearish divergence. Record a bullish divergence when the traded instrument makes a lower pivot low while either comparison instrument's corresponding low is higher. Divergence remains a directional confluence for a configurable number of bars; disabling divergence detection prevents it from contributing.

A confirmed directional setup requires a candle closing in that direction, optionally with an absolute body exceeding a configurable ATR multiple, and at least one of: (1) an enabled liquidity sweep through the latest confirmed pivot, closing back across it with a sufficiently large rejection wick and within a configurable ATR distance of that pivot; (2) overlap with an enabled inverted fair-value gap of the same direction; or (3) overlap with an active order block of the same direction. Pattern selection restricts sweeps and inverted gaps, but order blocks remain eligible in every mode. Divergence alone is not a confirmed setup.

Create a fair-value gap when the current bar's range is separated from the bar two bars earlier by at least a configurable tick distance. Retain a configurable number per original direction. A close beyond the far edge inverts it into the opposite direction; remove an inverted gap when price crosses its original near edge. An order block is the previous candle's body after a directional displacement closes beyond the previous bar's high or low with a sufficiently large body, provided the block is no larger than a configurable ATR limit. Retain a configurable number per direction and remove a block when the close penetrates its configurable mitigation level. Gap and block detection depend on their respective enable switches.

For each direction, count one point for each of: the confirmed setup; a recent same-direction cross-instrument divergence; a recent same-direction liquidity sweep; current same-direction structural trend; overlap with a same-direction original or inverted gap or order block; a fresh same-direction consecutive-close confirmation; price in the favorable outer portion of a configurable recent high-low range; and price on the favorable side of the latest New York session open. Structural trend changes when the close breaks the latest confirmed pivot high or low. Consecutive-close confirmation fires on a confirmed bar after the required sequence of rising or falling closes, subject to alternating direction and a minimum interval; it depends on its enable switch.

Issue a higher-grade long or short when its confirmed setup and minimum confluence count are present, the optional session, volatility, volume, and unfavorable-range-zone filters pass, and the same-direction signal cooldown has elapsed. Issue a lower-grade signal when the setup meets its lower confluence threshold but remains below the higher threshold, the volatility filter passes, and the same-direction cooldown has elapsed; session, volume, and range-zone filters do not apply to this grade. The cooldown updates only when an enabled grade is actually displayed on a confirmed bar, and is shared between grades within each direction.

For a displayed higher-grade signal with risk tracking enabled, use the signal close as entry, place a stop at a configurable ATR distance against the trade, and set two targets at configurable multiples of that risk in its favor. Track the outcome from subsequent bars: a stop without the first target is a loss; otherwise a first- or second-target touch is a win, with the second target counted separately. If stop and target occur on the same bar, the target takes precedence.
