Build adaptive bands around a selectable price basis using the selected deviation estimator, optional tail adjustment, and a controller that adjusts width toward target historical containment. Classify the market as trending when both directional efficiency and trend strength exceed their thresholds; otherwise classify it as volatile when volatility clustering is elevated, or as ranging. Determine trend direction from price relative to a trend average.

In a range, propose a short when the high touches or exceeds the upper main band and the close returns inside it, provided the previous bar was at or beyond that band; mirror this for a long at the lower band. In a trend, propose a long in an uptrend when price has persistently closed above the upper main band, or closes above the upper inner band and rises from the previous close; mirror this for a short in a downtrend. Require volume thrust when volume confirmation is enabled, treating unavailable volume as confirmed. Optionally require bar-close confirmation and block trend proposals opposed by higher-timeframe bias.

Score each proposal with a weighted average of band stretch or band-walk persistence, regime and variance-ratio fit, multi-timeframe agreement, mean-reversion half-life, the historical edge of that signal type, volume thrust, and directional order flow. Estimate historical edge from forward profit-versus-stop barrier outcomes, with stop taking precedence when both are touched, using optional overlap weighting and recency decay, minus the direction-specific baseline success rate for the entry regime; use neutral edge until enough samples resolve. Fade scores favor balanced timeframe readings and short half-lives; trend scores favor aligned timeframes and absent mean reversion.

Act only when the score meets its threshold. Veto a fade during a spike in the optional external volatility index. Veto a trend proposal when enabled timeframe agreement is insufficient, higher-timeframe bias opposes it, or a live optional cross-asset's nonzero momentum, adjusted for its correlation sign, opposes it. If both directions occur, prefer long. Otherwise remain flat.

When flat, track an acted-on entry at the close. Set its initial stop from the selected outer-band, ATR, or half-life-based method. For a fade, set the first target at the basis and the final target at the opposite inner band; for a trend, set both targets at configured multiples of initial risk. Exit when the stop or final target is touched; otherwise move the stop to entry when the first target is touched.
