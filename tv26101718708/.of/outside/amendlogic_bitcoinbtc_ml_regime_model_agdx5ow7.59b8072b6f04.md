On each confirmed bar, compute features from the traded instrument: log returns over short, medium and long horizons; centered and scaled RSI; distance from an EMA in ATR units; volume; and ATR as a fraction of price. Also compute the previous bar's percentage distance from timeframe-scaled EMAs for the traded instrument, the U.S. Dollar Index and TIP. Rolling-standardize all features except RSI, replace unavailable standardized values with zero, and clip them. Add clipped interactions of short return with volume, medium return with volatility, and dollar-index distance with medium return, each scaled by the clipping bound.

Initialize logistic-regression weights and intercept to zero. After the required history is available, on each confirmed bar train sequentially on a configured number of recent, overlapping samples. Each sample uses features from the prediction horizon before its outcome and is labeled positive only if the later close is higher. Update weights by gradient descent with L2 regularization and the intercept without regularization. Count training bars, not individual replay samples. Once the minimum training count is reached, calculate the model's current upside probability. Evaluate reliability as the rolling fraction of resolved past model predictions whose above-midpoint direction matches whether price rose over the prediction horizon. Use the model only when its training count and hit rate meet their minimums.

Otherwise use a fallback probability centered at neutral: add a configured offset proportional to the weighted average of three signs. The traded instrument's previous close above its long EMA is bullish and has double weight; the dollar index below its EMA and TIP above its EMA are each bullish and have single weight. All other cases are bearish. Optionally blend the selected probability with a configurable-weight sensing probability obtained by applying a sigmoid to a scaled average of standardized short return and its volume interaction.

Block entries when absolute standardized short return, standardized volume, or standardized relative ATR reaches its anomaly threshold. A long entry requires probability at or above its long-entry threshold; a short entry requires probability at or below its short-entry threshold. If trend alignment is enabled, the traded instrument must also be above its long EMA for a long, or otherwise for a short.

Maintain a flat, long or short state. On each confirmed bar, propose exiting a long when probability falls below its long-exit threshold, or exiting a short when probability rises above its short-exit threshold. If the proposed state is flat, immediately consider entries, giving long priority; this allows direct reversal. Apply any proposed state change only after the configured minimum holding period has elapsed, and reset the holding counter on every change. Anomalies and trend alignment block entries but do not themselves cause exits.
