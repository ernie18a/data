Use the prior daily close of India VIX to scale option position size: eliminate trades in the extreme-volatility zone, reduce size in high or elevated zones, and use full size in the normal zone. Measure directional bias as the instrument’s close relative to its EMA, normalized by ATR; classify it bullish, bearish, or neutral using configurable thresholds. Compare short-term realized volatility with long-term realized volatility; classify it as expanding, compressing, or normal using configurable ratio thresholds. Combine the classifications: bullish or bearish bias with normal/expanding volatility calls for a directional credit spread on the opposite-side premium; the same bias with compressing volatility calls for an iron condor. Neutral bias with normal volatility calls for an iron condor; neutral with compressing volatility calls for a wider iron condor; neutral with expanding volatility, or extreme VIX, means no trade. Place short strikes at ATR-based distances from the instrument price, farther on the side exposed to directional risk; round puts down and calls up, and place protective long options one wing width farther out.
