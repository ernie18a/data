On each bar, calculate an ATR-normalized oscillator from the difference between a fast and a slow weighted moving average of the traded instrument’s close. Use its rolling standard deviation as a moving trigger band. Evaluate higher-timeframe structure from close and two EMAs; calculate RSI, MACD histogram, Chaikin money flow, average volume, and a cosine of elapsed current wall-clock time since a reference Bitcoin halving.

Enter long when the oscillator crosses above its negative deviation band, provided higher-timeframe close is above the faster EMA and that EMA is above the slower EMA; RSI is below its overbought threshold; MACD histogram is nondecreasing; money flow exceeds a negative tolerance; volume exceeds its minimum relative to average; the halving cosine exceeds its lower limit; USDT dominance is not above its EMA; Bitcoin dominance is above its EMA for a BTC instrument or below it for any other instrument; and the percentage basis of CME Bitcoin futures versus the traded instrument’s close is not excessively positive. Treat an unavailable or invalid futures price as zero basis.

Enter short when the oscillator crosses below its positive deviation band, provided the higher-timeframe structure is bearish; RSI is above its oversold threshold; MACD histogram is nonincreasing; money flow is below a positive tolerance; volume exceeds its minimum; the halving cosine is below its upper limit; USDT dominance is not below its EMA; the Bitcoin-dominance condition for a long is false; and USDT dominance is not both near its rolling high and below its value a specified number of bars ago. Equality with the Bitcoin-dominance EMA makes the long condition false and therefore satisfies this short filter.

Exit long when the oscillator crosses below zero or a short entry occurs. Exit short when it crosses above zero or a long entry occurs. The additional RSI exit clauses in the source cannot fire because they require RSI to be beyond a threshold on the same bar it crosses back through that threshold. For a single displayed action, prioritize long entry, short entry, exit long, then exit short; otherwise wait. Entry and exit alerts are independently evaluated, so an opposite entry can also produce an exit alert.
