On the chart timeframe, build a close index for each of USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, AUD, NZD, CAD, and CHF from its seven crosses with the other currencies. For each valid cross, divide its close by its first available positive close if the currency is the base, or invert that ratio if it is the quote. Take the weighted geometric mean of these ratios, using normalized equal, ATR, or inverse-ATR weights according to the selected mode.

For each currency, calculate a composite force as the weighted average of these factors, each scaled to the range -100 to 100: (1) the index's lookback percentage return, optionally EMA-smoothed, divided by the largest absolute smoothed return across all currencies; (2) breadth, the average of votes across its seven crosses, where each lookback percentage return is signed positively for the base and negatively for the quote, then voted +1 above the positive neutral threshold, -1 below the negative threshold, or zero otherwise; (3) efficiency, the index's net change over its efficiency window divided by the sum of absolute one-bar changes; (4) impulse, the latest index change divided by the average absolute one-bar change over its impulse window and multiplied by 50; (5) persistence, the number of rising bars minus falling bars divided by the persistence window length; and (6) acceleration, the change in smoothed strength since the previous bar minus the equivalent change at the configured comparison lag, divided by the acceleration cap. Clamp each factor and the final force to the stated range. Use zero for unavailable or undefined factors; breadth treats an unavailable cross return as a zero vote and still divides by seven. If all factor weights are zero, force is zero.

For a chart pair whose base and quote are among those currencies, subtract quote force from base force. When the absolute difference is below the chart-pair gap threshold, report neutral; otherwise report long if the difference is positive and short if it is nonpositive. If either currency is unsupported, report no bias. Separately, rank all currencies by force. If the strongest-to-weakest gap reaches the best-pair threshold, recommend the cross between them: long when the strongest is its base and short when the strongest is its quote; otherwise wait. These are directional biases, with no specified entry timing or exit rule.
