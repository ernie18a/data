Compare the traded instrument with a related external market, optionally reversing or time-shifting the external series. Standardize and smooth that series, then compare its confirmed swing highs and lows with the instrument’s corresponding pivots. When their trailing correlation is sufficiently strong, signal long on bullish divergence (the instrument makes a lower low while the external series makes a higher low) or hidden bullish divergence (the instrument makes a higher low while the external series makes a lower low). Signal short on bearish divergence (the instrument makes a higher high while the external series makes a lower high) or hidden bearish divergence (the instrument makes a lower high while the external series makes a higher high). Optionally require the signal to pass a market-regime or time-slot filter. Track each signal’s subsequent favorable-move rate against a comparable baseline and use a confidence-adjusted edge to label a direction confirmed; this confidence label does not suppress the underlying divergence signal. No exit rule is defined.
