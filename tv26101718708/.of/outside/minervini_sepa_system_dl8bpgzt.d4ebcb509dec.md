Long-entry only; no short or exit rules are defined. Enter long on a bar when all of the following hold. (A) Trend template, all eight tests pass on the same bar. 1) Close is above both the intermediate and long simple moving averages. 2) The intermediate average is above the long average. 3) The long average is higher than it was some fixed number of bars ago. 4) The short average is above both the intermediate and long averages. 5) Close is above the short average. 6) Close is at least a minimum percentage above the trailing roughly one-year (252-bar) lowest low. 7) Close is no more than a maximum percentage below the trailing one-year highest high. 8) Relative strength versus SPY, an external benchmark series on the same timeframe, is at least a minimum. Compute it as the instrument's close divided by its close one year (252 bars) ago, divided by SPY's same one-year ratio, times 100. Subtract a fixed offset, then clamp the result to the range 0 to 99. (B) Pivot breakout. Track the most recent confirmed swing-high pivot, a bar whose high is the highest within a symmetric window of bars on both sides, so it is only known after the right-side window completes. Keep that level as resistance until a newer pivot confirms. The current close must be above this resistance level while the previous close was at or below it, so only the first bar of the cross counts. (C) Volume surge. Current volume exceeds a multiple (above 1) of its simple moving average over a fixed length. Enter long when A, B and C are all true. Volatility contraction (current ATR well below its own longer average) combined with volume dry-up (volume well below its average) while the trend template passes is only a watch-list setup marker, not a trade signal.
