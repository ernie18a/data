Use daily instrument data and a manually selected benchmark or an automatically selected market benchmark: a country-specific equity index for equities and funds, a government yield for bonds, and Bitcoin for other cryptocurrencies. Bitcoin, currencies, and commodities use absolute returns instead of benchmark-relative returns. Fall back to a designated alternative benchmark if the automatic choice is unavailable.

Build a relative-strength score as the weighted average of available rolling percentile ranks of long-term return excluding the most recent month, medium- and short-term returns, proximity to the rolling closing-price high, and the change in the instrument-to-benchmark ratio. Use excess returns and the ratio where a benchmark is required; otherwise use absolute returns and price. For indices, smooth this score with an exponential average.

For equities, build a fundamental score from weighted, bounded and normalized year-over-year earnings growth, revenue growth, and return on equity. Prefer quarterly revenue growth to trailing growth, and trailing return on equity to annual return or trailing net income divided by positive equity. Exclude unavailable components and renormalize the remaining weights.

Build a trend score as the weighted average of bounded, normalized measures of price distance from its long moving average, the spread between short and long moving averages, and closing-price drawdown from a rolling high relative to an allowed drawdown. Set the allowed drawdown to the more negative of a configured limit and a volatility-based limit. Renormalize over available components.

Combine the usable relative-strength, fundamental and trend scores using configurable weights, renormalized over available factors. Require a minimum fraction of relative-strength components and an available benchmark where relative comparison applies; use fundamentals only for equities. Smooth the composite with an exponential average for indices. Compute coverage as a weighted average of the available fractions of the applicable factors, and confidence as a weighted combination of coverage and a history-quality score.

A good long confirmation requires the composite to be at or above the buy threshold on both the current and preceding bar, sufficient full history, buy-level coverage and confidence, and an available required benchmark when strict benchmark checking is enabled. If the safety filter is enabled, also require price above its long moving average and drawdown within its limit; equities additionally require minimum average price-times-volume, and funds require it when volume is available.

A strong long confirmation additionally requires the composite to be at or above the strong threshold on both bars, rising versus a configured earlier bar, positive smoothed composite momentum, and a current close at or above the preceding rolling price high. It also requires minimum relative-strength and trend scores, stricter coverage and confidence, a tighter drawdown limit, and, for equities, an available fundamental score above its minimum.

Emit a good long signal when good confirmation first becomes true, unless strong confirmation is already true; emit a strong long signal when strong confirmation first becomes true. Emit an exit when the composite crosses below the buy threshold, provided minimum history and watch-level coverage are available. Separately emit a sell/avoid alert when the composite crosses below the weak-score threshold. No short or options decisions are defined.
