Estimate signed volume from intrabar price direction, falling back to bar shape, and accumulate it until its magnitude exceeds an adaptive significance threshold. Treat each such close as a synthetic bar. Detect bullish or bearish three-bar price gaps when the intervening bar has sufficient signed-volume impulse and the gap is large enough relative to volatility. Track each level’s strength: reduce it on touches and over time, discard it when price breaks through with strong opposing flow, and promote it to a defended breaker when price fully reclaims it with sufficient opposing flow. From surviving sufficiently strong levels, use the nearest bullish level below price as support and the nearest bearish level above price as resistance.

Classify the market using directional efficiency over recent synthetic-bar closes, with an exceptional-move filter: a sufficiently efficient rise is an uptrend, an efficient fall a downtrend, low efficiency is a range, intermediate efficiency is a transition, and an exceptional volatility burst is high volatility. Only trend states permit entries. Fuse the selected level’s strength, whether cumulative-volume flow agrees with the trend, recent flow toxicity, and optionally aligned external bias and falling-versus-rising implied volatility into a confidence score; high toxicity reduces confidence. On a confirmed synthetic-bar close, enter long when the state is an uptrend, a support trail exists, confidence meets its minimum, cumulative-volume flow is positive, and optional external bias does not strongly oppose the trade. Enter short symmetrically in a downtrend using resistance, negative flow, and non-opposing external bias. While long, ratchet the stop upward to the higher of its prior value and the current support trail; exit if price closes below it or the state ceases to be an uptrend. While short, ratchet the stop downward to the lower of its prior value and current resistance trail; exit if price closes above it or the state ceases to be a downtrend. When flat, range, transition, and high-volatility states provide no entry.
