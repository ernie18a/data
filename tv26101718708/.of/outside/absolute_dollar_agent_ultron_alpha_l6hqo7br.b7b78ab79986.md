In vanilla-options mode, use the underlying instrument’s data to generate bullish and bearish entry decisions symmetrically. Buy a call for a bullish entry and a put for a bearish entry. Use configurable periods, thresholds, filters, and target multiples throughout.

Maintain an ATR-offset trail around an EMA of close. In an uptrend, ratchet its lower line upward; flip down when close falls below it and, if enabled, the fast EMA is below the slow EMA. Mirror this for a downtrend. Detect structural reversals when price breaks a confirmed swing level opposite the preceding break. A new reversal zone is the break bar’s high-low range; optionally require sufficient bar width relative to ATR, higher-timeframe regime agreement, and limited extension from the recent swing.

For each direction, count enabled confirmations: macro and fast Hull slopes, trail direction, active structural reversal direction, proximity to its zone, ATR-normalized price momentum, regime permission, and above-average volume. Regime permission requires the local double-smoothed typical-price EMA to slope in that direction or close to be on the corresponding side of a long EMA. Qualify a direction when the trail agrees, its score reaches the minimum, regime permission passes, and the configurable local and/or confirmed higher-timeframe double-EMA regime, price-versus-basis filters, and opposing-regime cooldowns pass. A sufficiently high score with an agreeing trail can override the latter regime filters.

A directional trigger occurs on a qualified trail flip or a qualified new structural reversal whose zone confidence meets the minimum. Compute zone confidence at creation as the fraction of confirmations present: matching higher-timeframe structural direction, passing regime filters before override, nearby unbroken directional liquidity, favorable position within the swing range, sufficient volume, directional momentum, a selected active session, matching higher-timeframe regime, and proximity to the new zone.

On a confirmed bar while the position tool is flat, enter on the directional trigger, or use the configured alternative: a trail retest requires both a current trigger and a touch and recovery of the trail saved at the last flip; a zone retest requires price to revisit the latest same-direction zone within its allowed window while the direction remains qualified. Record entry at close, initial stop at the trail, and three targets at configured multiples of the entry-to-trail distance.

Manage the underlying-based position symmetrically. Before the first target, follow the trail. After the first target, use the more protective of the trail and buffered breakeven; after the second, use the more protective of the trail and either the first target or buffered breakeven. After the third, ratchet the trail without loosening it. Check the stop before target hits, using either confirmed close or intrabar extremes as configured; also exit on a confirmed opposite trail flip. Multiple targets can be reached on one bar, and a new entry can follow an exit on that bar.

Maintain a separate option-direction state. A new bullish or bearish position entry selects the corresponding call or put state. Add to that option direction, up to the configured limit, on the rising edge of a same-direction RSI signal: RSI is beyond its directional threshold but not exhausted, a short EMA of close moves in that direction, and enabled trail, Hull, and local-regime filters agree. Exit the option state on an opposite trail flip or opposite structural reversal. Process a new position entry before scale-in, and scale-in before this regime exit.
