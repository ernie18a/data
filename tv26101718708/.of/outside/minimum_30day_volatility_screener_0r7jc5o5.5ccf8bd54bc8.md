The only decision logic is an option-buying rule. Each bar, measure a medium-length average true range of the traded instrument and express it as a percentage of the current close. Call this the premium-inflation measure. Compare the current price with a fixed high-price threshold and compare the premium-inflation measure with a fixed cap. Both are tunable constants. If the price is at or above the threshold and the premium-inflation measure is at or below the cap, buy a high-delta, deep in-the-money call option, either alongside or instead of buying the stock. If the price is at or above the threshold but the premium-inflation measure is above the cap, treat options as overpriced because of inflated implied volatility. Do not buy options in that case, and use shares only. If the price is at or below a lower low-price threshold, do not buy options. Use the stock only, with leverage if desired. Between the two price thresholds, make no option decision and treat the instrument as borderline. Option buys come only from this rule. This rule has no short, sell-option or exit logic, and nothing in it uses data from outside the traded instrument.
