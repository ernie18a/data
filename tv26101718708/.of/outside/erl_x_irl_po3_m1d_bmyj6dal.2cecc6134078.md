On confirmed chart bars, track bullish and bearish setups separately. Build a sufficiently large range from confirmed higher-timeframe swing highs and lows, using either the latest swings or a held range. For a bullish setup, the lower boundary is the swept level and the upper boundary is the target; reverse these for bearish. Optionally require a prior failed push toward the target. Arm only while the target remains ahead of price and the swept-side swing has not already produced a confirmed setup.

A sweep begins when price crosses the swept level from inside the range with a wick. Track its most extreme wick. Reject it if closes remain beyond the level longer than the reclaim allowance, the target is closed through first, or the sweep expires. After the sweep, confirm a long when price closes back inside the lower boundary and above the latest qualifying chart swing high inside the target, with sufficient upward displacement from the sweep extreme. Confirm a short by the mirrored conditions. Apply the optional session restriction. When peer confirmation is enabled for the supported index futures, require at least one peer not to have breached its corresponding swept-side level; otherwise reject the sweep. Determine each peer level from its extreme around the corresponding range swing, and track breaches after that swing's confirmation window.

A confirmed setup remains active until price closes through the sweep extreme, closes through the opposite range boundary, or the setup expires. The first condition invalidates it; the latter two finish or retire it. When comparing peers' target-side levels, designate the chart instrument as the trade-direction laggard if at least one peer has breached its corresponding target and the chart instrument has not. A same-direction gap formed after the first peer breach marks catch-up confirmation.
