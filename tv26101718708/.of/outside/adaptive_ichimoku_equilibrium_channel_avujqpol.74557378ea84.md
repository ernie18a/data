Use the traded instrument’s high/low range midpoints over fast, slow and cloud-base lookbacks. Form a cloud from the mean of the fast and slow midpoints and the cloud-base midpoint. Form an equilibrium by blending the fast and slow midpoints using the selected price series’ efficiency ratio, or use the slow midpoint if adaptation is disabled. The efficiency ratio is the absolute net price change over its lookback divided by the sum of absolute bar-to-bar changes, or zero if the denominator is zero.

Classify the trend as up when close is above the cloud and either the cloud is bullish or equilibrium is rising; classify it as down when close is below the cloud and either the cloud is bearish or equilibrium is falling; otherwise it is neutral. A strong trend additionally requires cloud orientation and equilibrium slope to agree with the trend, and efficiency and ADX to exceed their strong-trend thresholds.

Build channel rails around equilibrium using ATR times a width multiplier. Optionally multiply width by a bounded ratio of ATR to its long-term average. If an external volatility index is enabled, also multiply width by its bounded ratio to its own moving average; use a neutral factor if that average is unavailable. The lower and upper quarters of the resulting channel are the discount and premium zones. An uptrend entering discount produces a buy-zone signal; a downtrend entering premium produces a sell-zone signal. Entry means the current bar is in the zone and the preceding bar was not in that same zone.

On confirmed bars, maintain at most one trade setup. When flat and setups are enabled, enter long at close in an uptrend when price is in discount and the candle closes above its open; enter short in a downtrend when price is in premium and the candle closes below its open. If enabled, require the higher-timeframe trend to agree, using the same cloud test with the slow midpoint’s slope and higher-timeframe close-based efficiency; require lower-timeframe close to be above its EMA for a long or below it for a short. If clear-trend filtering is enabled, also require chart-timeframe efficiency above its threshold and either an agreeing higher-timeframe trend when that filter is enabled, or a strong chart-timeframe trend when it is disabled.

Set the long stop below the cloud bottom by an ATR buffer and the short stop above the cloud top by the same type of buffer. Target the nearest unswept equal-high pool above entry for a long or equal-low pool below entry for a short when liquidity targets are enabled and available; otherwise target the corresponding channel rail. Detect pools by matching confirmed price pivots against recent same-side pivots within an ATR tolerance, using the higher matched high or lower matched low as the pool level. Remove high pools when a bar’s high exceeds them and low pools when a bar’s low falls below them. Enter only if the stop is on the correct side of entry and target reward meets the minimum reward-to-risk requirement.

On each later confirmed bar, exit a long if its low reaches the fixed stop, otherwise if its high reaches the fixed target, otherwise if the trend turns down. Mirror these conditions for a short. Give the stop priority if both stop and target are touched on the same bar. After an exit, allow a new setup on that same confirmed bar.
