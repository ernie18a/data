Compare the selected instruments using the percentage spread between their fast and slow close EMAs. Use daily data, except that an optional intraday mode computes the EMAs on chart bars with their lengths scaled by estimated bars per day. Maintain an active instrument independently of whether a risk exit has been signaled.

With two instruments, select whichever has the higher spread, including catching up when it already leads; retain the active instrument on a tie. Before a leader is established, default to the second enabled instrument, or the first if the second is disabled. With more instruments, select the enabled instrument with the highest available spread, breaking ties by selection order. When the broad-market index is above its trend SMA, switch only if the candidate's spread exceeds the active spread by a configurable fraction of the absolute active spread; below or at that SMA, select the highest spread without this buffer. The first selection does not require a buffer.

Before a subsequent rotation, apply the enabled risk and calm guards. Measure each instrument's volatility as the standard deviation of daily percentage returns. Enter the risk guard when the systemic risk state is high or its smoothed score reaches the high-risk boundary; leave it only after a configurable number of days without that condition. Enter the calm guard when risk is not high and either the state is low or the score is below the low-risk boundary; leave it after a configurable number of days without that condition. Risk takes precedence if both guards are active. In the risk guard, allow a switch to a less volatile instrument or any switch within a configurable window after a risk exit signal; otherwise require the candidate's spread to exceed the active spread by a configurable absolute gap. In the calm guard, allow a switch to a more volatile instrument or any switch within a configurable window after entering calm; otherwise require its spread to exceed the active spread by another configurable absolute gap. Outside these guards, rotate without an additional gap. On a change, signal a long in the new instrument and an exit from the previous one, unless its exit was already signaled by a risk event.

Calculate systemic risk on the broad-market index from four daily measures: trailing annual return, the proportion of positive daily changes over a shorter window, distance from a long SMA in ATR units, and return over that shorter window divided by ATR normalized by average price. For each measure take the higher of its short- and long-history percentile ranks, substituting a neutral percentile when unavailable. Sort the four ranks, interpolate an upper quantile, add a penalty for an exceptionally high maximum, cap the result, and invert and normalize it to obtain the raw risk score. Smooth it recursively with an EMA whose length changes when the raw score is extreme. Classify the smoothed score as low, neutral, or high using two boundaries, with a persistent state, volatility-adjusted maturity, confidence, transition resistance, and stronger thresholds that can override resistance.

On a confirmed bar, signal an exit for the active instrument when the smoothed systemic score exceeds the extreme threshold, the index is below its intermediate EMA or at or below its trend SMA, and the fixed consumer-staples reference has a higher fast/slow EMA spread than the fixed technology reference. Suppress this event after an oversold-RSI event in the current confirmed regime, after an extreme event in the current risk-band episode, and during its cooldown. Reset the episode only after a configurable time outside both risk bands. Separately, signal an exit when the average daily RSI of the index and the fixed technology and staples references falls below its threshold; fire once per confirmed regime, resetting after a sustained change among normal, intermediate-risk, and bearish bands. Risk exits do not change the active selection or automatically generate a new entry; retain a pending exit marker to suppress the duplicate exit when that instrument later rotates out. Process risk exits before rotation. Market-cycle labels, early-risk warnings, Bollinger signals, candle colors, and dashboard metrics do not filter the long or rotation signals.
