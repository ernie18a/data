On each bar, form a signed mean-reversion score from enabled, available components, using configurable weights and without renormalizing missing components:
- Statistical core: fit a lagged-price mean-reversion model. When its half-life, Hurst exponent, optional unit-root and variance-ratio tests, and session-gap guard pass, vote long for a sufficiently negative equilibrium z-score and short for a sufficiently positive one; scale strength by its absolute z-score, with a cap. Failure silences this component, not the entire signal.
- Futures basis: compare actual futures price with spot compounded at the risk-free rate to expiry, subtract a Kalman-estimated futures-to-spot return coefficient times the spot price change, and standardize the residual over a rolling window. Vote against an extreme residual, with capped strength proportional to its magnitude. Reset the filter at detected session boundaries.
- Options skew: standardize the rolling ratio of out-of-the-money put to call prices. Vote long for an extreme high ratio and short for an extreme low ratio, with capped proportional strength.
- Microstructure: vote against extreme standardized deviations from rolling and session-anchored VWAP. Blend their strengths when directions agree, retain a weighted contribution when only one votes, and cancel conflicting votes. Increase strength for a volume spike or order-flow imbalance opposing the proposed direction, and reduce it for unusually high volume toxicity.
- Choose either put-call ratio or gamma exposure. For the ratio, vote long when it is above its upper threshold and rising, and short when below its lower threshold and rising; scale by distance beyond the threshold. For gamma, compute signed call-minus-put open interest times option gamma at nearby strikes, optionally weighted by relative option volume. Select the strike with greatest absolute exposure; vote short if it is call-heavy and above price, or long if put-heavy and below price, with strength proportional to its ATR distance.
- For constituent residuals, estimate each stock's rolling beta to the index and standardize its residual return. Vote in the direction shared by a sufficient number of extreme residuals, provided the opposite side does not also meet that count; scale strength by the number agreeing.
Multiply the sum by a bounded product of enabled modifiers: a volatility-index and ADX regime factor; a constituent-correlation factor; a volatility-index minus realized-volatility factor; a cross-asset stress factor based on the sum of absolute volatility-normalized returns when enough feeds are available; and an expiry-day factor that boosts or penalizes agreement with the direction toward the strike fixed at the detected session start. Block the high-volatility spike or strong-trend regime when that guard is enabled.
Choose the score's sign as direction. In weighted mode, select the longest horizon whose score threshold, half-life condition, and corresponding variance-ratio condition pass, falling back to shorter horizons. In confluence mode, require the configured number of component votes agreeing with the score and assign horizon from that count. In strict mode, require every active statistical, basis, skew, and microstructure vote to agree or be neutral, with a nonzero score, and select the longest horizon. If enabled, require the absolute distance from price to the fitted equilibrium, expressed relative to price, to exceed round-trip cost times the horizon-dependent edge requirement. Suppress signals during configured opening, closing, lunch, and session-warmup periods. A positive surviving direction signals long and a call side; a negative direction signals short and a put side. Otherwise output neutral. The indicator specifies no explicit position-exit decision.
