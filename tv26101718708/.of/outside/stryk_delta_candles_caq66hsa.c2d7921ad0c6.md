On each completed higher-timeframe candle, calculate estimated delta by summing lower-timeframe volume signed positive for an up candle and negative for a down candle; for an unchanged candle, use its close versus the preceding close, or zero if unchanged. If intrabar data is unavailable, classify each chart bar by its close versus open instead. Compare the completed candle’s close with its open using a configurable flat-price tolerance.

Identify bearish and bullish signals as follows: a flip requires delta to change respectively from positive to negative or negative to positive versus the preceding candle, while price is respectively up or flat, or down or flat. A divergence requires price to rise beyond the tolerance with negative delta, or fall beyond it with positive delta; suppress it when the corresponding enabled flip occurs. Absorption requires flat price and absolute delta at least a configurable multiple of the average absolute delta over the recent completed-candle window, including the signal candle. Normally positive delta is bearish absorption and negative delta is bullish; an option reverses those directions. Each signal type can be disabled.

For a higher-conviction short signal, require an enabled bearish signal and the external market-breadth instrument’s high to reach its positive extreme threshold during the same higher-timeframe candle. For a higher-conviction long signal, require an enabled bullish signal and its low to reach the negative extreme threshold during that candle. These classifications require the breadth feature and its signal enhancement to be enabled; missing breadth data cannot qualify.
