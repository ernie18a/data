Long-only entry and exit framework on the traded stock, using a benchmark index as outside data. Each bar, compute a score as the count of seven true criteria. (1) Close is above the stock's daily long-term EMA. (2) The medium-term EMA is above the long-term EMA. (3) The long-term EMA is higher than it was a fixed number of bars ago. (4) The weekly close, taken from the last completed weekly bar with no lookahead, is above the weekly long-term EMA. (5) Relative strength is above parity: (stock close / stock daily long-term EMA) divided by (benchmark daily close / benchmark daily long-term EMA) is greater than 1. The benchmark is a separate index symbol fetched on the daily timeframe with no lookahead, for example S&P 500 or a user-chosen symbol. (6) Daily RSI lies within a healthy band, at or above a lower bound and at or below an upper bound. (7) Volume trend is rising: the short moving average of volume now is greater than the same average measured a fixed number of bars earlier, with both values valid. A qualified state means score >= a minimum score threshold. Entry (go long): on a bar where all of the following hold, open or add a long position. (a) A long-term-EMA retest has just started. A bar counts as near-EMA when its low is at or below the long-term EMA plus a small percentage tolerance and its close is above the long-term EMA. The retest is new when the previous three bars were not near-EMA, which prevents repeated signals in chop. (b) The score is qualified. (c) The volume filter passes: either the filter is disabled, or the current bar's volume exceeds its longer-term volume moving average times a multiplier. Exit/weakness (close or review a held long): when the score falls below the minimum threshold after being at or above it on the previous bar, treat it as an exit/reduce signal for the long. There is no short logic. A transition from below the threshold to at or above it (score strengthening) is only a watchlist state with no trade. No stops or targets are defined.
