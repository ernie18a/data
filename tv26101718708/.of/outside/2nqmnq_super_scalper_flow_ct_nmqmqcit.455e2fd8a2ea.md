Evaluate each bar for independent long and short signals on the charted futures contract. Use chart price, volume and footprint data together with ES futures, the dollar index, SPY, QQQ, market TICK, volatility futures, short-term volatility and the technology-sector ETF. Treat all lookbacks, thresholds, weights, buffers and minimum target distances as configurable parameters.

Build a directional score from price trend and structure, higher-timeframe moving-average alignment, momentum, VWAP, volume profile, order-flow delta and its persistence, divergences, sweeps, candlestick reversals and exhaustion. Add market breadth from TICK, volatility-futures direction and relative level, technology-sector leadership, futures performance relative to SPY, and divergence between the futures and ES. Standardize the score with an exponentially weighted mean and variance. Use dollar-index swing divergence as additional reversal confirmation. Adjust thresholds and confidence for session, trend regime, trend strength, volatility and relative volume.

Evaluate these main-layer setups separately in both directions: a break of the latest opposing swing after an established swing sequence; divergence between the futures and ES swing extremes, with a liquidity and trend-strength reliability filter in the Asian session; price versus cumulative-delta divergence; regular and hidden price versus RSI divergence; rejection of the latest high-volume candle's boundary; a trend-aligned hold of its midpoint; reversal after a recent high-volume, large-body capitulation candle; high-volume, narrow-range absorption with flat delta; and unusually one-sided aggressive order flow. For each setup, calculate its own weighted confidence from the applicable directional score, reversal or trend context, proximity to support or resistance, range and band position, volume, candle, higher-timeframe bias and market regime. RSI and aggressor setups also reject signals strongly opposed by the higher-timeframe trend; aggressor signals require extra confidence when structure does not confirm them. Issue a trade only when its setup-specific confidence passes and a valid stop and sufficiently distant target exist.

For countertrend signals, first require enabled direction, adequate relative volume and volatility, a downtrend or consolidation for longs or an uptrend or consolidation for shorts, and no strongly opposing higher-timeframe bias. Also require sufficient trend strength or a nonpersistent market. Calculate directional exhaustion and confidence from mean stretch, volatility-band extremes, order-flow and momentum reversal, structure, cross-market divergence, volatility, and mean-reversion regime; penalize strong market velocity and opposing hidden RSI divergence. Compare confidence with its exponentially weighted mean plus standard deviation. In priority order, allow at most one trade per direction per bar: an overextension fade with its own reversal-confidence threshold; regular RSI or delta divergence; a rejected liquidity sweep; an aggressive-flow flip; narrow-range high-volume absorption; or a retest of a stored high-volume zone after price moved away. Each latter setup also needs its own confidence, including setup-specific confirmation, to exceed its own exponentially weighted mean plus standard deviation.

For each accepted trade, enter at the signal close. Choose the setup's target from the applicable nearby VWAP, moving averages, swing, pivot, session, volume-profile or high-volume-zone levels, offset before the level by a crowding-dependent buffer. Include QQQ round-number levels converted to futures prices by the current futures-to-QQQ ratio when selecting applicable support and resistance. Place stops beyond the relevant swing, zone boundary or signal extreme with a buffer based on level crowding and recent wick size; sweep fades use the more extreme of the sweep and swing and also account for the setup wick. Reject trades without both levels or with less than the required profit distance. Track each ticket independently and exit when price touches its target or stop; a stop cannot trigger on the entry bar, and a simultaneous target and stop touch is recorded as a target hit.
