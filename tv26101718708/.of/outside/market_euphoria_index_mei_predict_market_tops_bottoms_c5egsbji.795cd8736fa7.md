Monthly macro regime framework for an equity index (e.g. S&P 500), driven by outside data: the index's own price, the 10Y-2Y Treasury spread, VIX, US CPI year-over-year, the Fed funds rate, and the 10Y Treasury yield. All inputs are sampled at monthly resolution. Build a 0-100 euphoria score as a weighted average of six sub-scores, each clipped or bucketed to 0-100, then smooth the result with a short exponential moving average. (1) Price extension: the percent the index sits above its 5-year simple moving average, scaled linearly and clipped. Higher extension scores higher. (2) Yield-curve clock: while the spread is negative, score moderately high, rising with the depth of inversion up to a cap. After un-inversion, score by months since the spread was last negative. The score peaks in the first months after un-inversion and steps down in stages as time passes, to a low value once the inversion is long past. (3) Complacency: the 12-month average of VIX, scored inversely and linearly, so a persistently low average scores high. (4) Inflation re-acceleration: the 6-month change in CPI YoY, bucketed so larger rises score higher and falling inflation scores low. (5) Real-rate stress: the 10Y yield minus CPI YoY, bucketed so higher real rates score higher. The top bucket requires a high real rate that also rose substantially over the prior 12 months. Negative or very negative real rates score low. (6) Fed cycle: bucketed on the Fed funds level and its 12-month change. A high, flat rate (plateau) scores highest. A very high rate or a moderately high and non-falling rate also scores high. Mid-to-low rates score progressively lower, and a sharp 12-month cut at low rates gives the lowest score. Weight the sub-scores unequally, with price extension the heaviest, the yield-curve clock next, VIX next, and the other three smallest and equal. Normalize by the total weight. Signals come from the smoothed score crossing four fixed zone thresholds. Crossing upward through the upper 'euphoria warning' level is a late-cycle caution: tighten stops and reduce long exposure. Crossing upward through the higher 'extreme euphoria' level is a strong top-risk signal: exit or heavily reduce longs, and a short bias is possible. Crossing downward through the lower 'fear' level marks an opportunity zone: begin or add to long exposure. Crossing downward through the lower 'extreme fear' level marks a likely major bottom: the strongest long or accumulation signal. Between the fear and warning levels the regime is neutral, with no new directional action. The framework is for major cyclical turning points only, not short-term timing.
