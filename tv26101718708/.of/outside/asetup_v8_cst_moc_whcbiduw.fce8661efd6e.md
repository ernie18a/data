On each bar, calculate bullish and bearish scores by awarding one point for each aligned condition: short-, medium-, and long-term averages stacked in the trade direction; price on the matching side of volume-weighted average price (VWAP) with VWAP sloping that way; higher-timeframe averages stacked in that direction (or the higher-timeframe filter disabled); RSI within the corresponding trend range; MACD line on the matching side of its signal line with the histogram expanding that way; and qualifying high, optionally expanding, volume. Also count trend agreement from average stacking, price relative to VWAP, and higher-timeframe alignment; when strict trend filtering is enabled, at least two of these three must agree.

A bullish pattern is a bullish engulfing candle, hammer, strong bullish close above the recent swing high, strong bullish reversal after touching support, or a volume-confirmed recovery above a recent low swept within the prior few bars. A bearish pattern is the inverse: bearish engulfing, shooting star, strong bearish close below the recent swing low, strong bearish rejection at resistance, or a volume-confirmed fall below a recent high swept within the prior few bars. Support and resistance are drawn from prior and current session extremes, prior closes, session and opening-range extremes, recent pivots, VWAP, and the medium-term average; proximity to a level is required for bounce or rejection patterns, not for every entry.

Buy a call on a bullish pattern when the bullish score is full for the premium tier or in the qualifying lower tier, trend agreement passes, the bar is outside the blocked trading windows and the end-of-day window, and the call cooldown has elapsed. Buy a put under the corresponding bearish conditions and put cooldown. The lower tier applies only when the premium tier does not. During the end-of-day window, buy a call or put when the corresponding score reaches the qualifying threshold, its pattern and trend filter pass, volume qualifies, its separate cooldown has elapsed, and price has room before the prior day's high for calls or prior day's low for puts. End-of-day signals are suppressed when a regular signal fires in the same direction.

At entry, set the stop using either an volatility-based distance or a nearby recent-bar/average reference. Set a partial target and a farther full-exit target from the entry risk distance. When price reaches the partial target, move the stop to breakeven. Exit a call if its full target or stop is reached, bearish trend alignment and a bearish pattern occur together, or its time limit expires; exit a put on the mirrored conditions. A reversal warning is also indicated when the opposite score and pattern qualify and price or average alignment supports the reversal.
