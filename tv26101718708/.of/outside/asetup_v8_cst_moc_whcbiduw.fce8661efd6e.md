Evaluate each bar using the underlying instrument’s price and volume. Calculate separate bullish and bearish scores, awarding one point each for: directional ordering of fast, middle, and slow EMAs; close on the directional side of VWAP with VWAP moving in that direction; matching higher-timeframe EMA ordering, or the higher-timeframe filter being disabled; RSI within the corresponding directional range; MACD on the directional side of its signal with the histogram moving further in that direction; and volume above its average by a configured multiplier, optionally requiring consecutive increases. For the optional trend filter, require a configured majority of directional EMA ordering, close versus VWAP, and higher-timeframe agreement; disabling the higher-timeframe filter counts as agreement.

A bullish or bearish pattern is any enabled directional engulfing candle, long-wick reversal candle, strong-bodied close beyond the preceding swing range, strong-bodied bounce or rejection near a relevant level, or confirmed sweep and reclaim of a prior range extreme. For a bounce or rejection, compare the candle’s low or high with prior day and week levels, current-day extremes, premarket and opening-range extremes, the latest confirmed swing, the middle EMA, and VWAP; require a directional candle closing beyond its midpoint. For a sweep pattern, record the breached prior extreme and accept a directional close back across it within the allowed bars with qualified volume; clear the pending sweep after confirmation or expiry. Apply the corresponding mirrored conditions for bearish patterns.

Outside the designated end-of-day window, issue a call-buy or put-buy signal when its enabled score tier, matching pattern, optional trend filter, direction-specific cooldown, and allowed-time filter all pass. During the end-of-day window, use its separate enabled tier and shared cooldown, require at least the configured score, matching pattern, trend filter and qualified volume, and require the close to remain a configured margin below the previous-day high for calls or above the previous-day low for puts. The end-of-day rule does not use the ordinary blocked-time filter. Update cooldowns when signals fire, even if that option side is already active; calls and puts can be active independently.

On a buy signal for an inactive side, record the underlying close as entry. Set the stop using either a configured ATR distance or a buffered recent extreme combined with the middle EMA, then set two targets at configured multiples of initial risk. When the first target is touched, mark it reached and move the stop to entry; this does not itself close the position. Sell the call when the underlying reaches the second target or stop, a bearish EMA stack, close below VWAP and bearish pattern coincide, or an enabled time limit expires. Mirror these conditions for the put. Process the first target and stop update before checking full exits, including on the entry bar.
