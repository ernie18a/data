Use the traded instrument’s price and volatility history, days to option expiry, and a manual event-risk flag. Classify trend direction from the slope of a long-term moving average; use a trend-strength measure to distinguish ranging from trending conditions. Rank recent average true range against its history, compare fast and slow volatility to detect expansion, and measure whether price is in the middle or beyond the extremes of its recent volatility bands.

Apply this priority order:
1. Defensive: if trend strength breaks upward through its no-trend boundary, volatility’s historical rank jumps sharply, or price breaks out beyond a band extreme, open no new positions and manage existing ones. Close or roll the tested option leg; allow the untested leg to decay or trail its stop.
2. Full-size range premium sale: if trend strength indicates no trend, volatility is in a suitable middle-to-high but non-extreme range and is not expanding sharply, price is between the inner band levels, expiry is sufficiently distant, and no event risk is flagged, sell both calls and puts as a strangle or iron condor.
3. Full-size directional credit spread: if a trend exists, volatility is in a suitable middle-to-high but non-extreme range and is not expanding sharply, expiry is sufficiently distant, and there is no combination of event risk with rich volatility, sell puts in an uptrend or calls in a downtrend, with a farther-out long option as a hedge. If trend strength indicates a trend but the moving-average slope is neutral, sell both sides as a tighter condor.
4. Otherwise, use reduced size with wider, two-sided option strikes; do not treat these conditions as a no-trade signal.

For two-sided positions, place short call and put strikes on opposite sides of spot at a regime-dependent multiple of current average true range, with long wings farther out. For directional positions, place the short strike in the trend-aligned option class at a regime-dependent ATR distance and buy a farther-out hedge. Round strikes to the instrument’s standard strike increment. Use wider strike distances in reduced-size conditions. Do not open full-size positions once expiry is within the full-size cutoff; the resulting fallback is reduced size unless a higher-priority defensive condition applies. Close all positions when expiry reaches the forced-close window, regardless of profit or loss.

For existing option positions, take profit when premium has decayed to a regime-dependent fraction of its entry value; use a more conservative target for directional or reduced-size regimes than for range premium sales. Exit a leg if its premium rises to the specified multiple of entry. Once a specified portion of profit is captured, trail the remaining position’s stop to breakeven.
