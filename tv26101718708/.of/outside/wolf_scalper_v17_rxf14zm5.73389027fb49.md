On each bar, compare the traded instrument with two configured external instruments on the same timeframe. At each confirmed pivot high of the traded instrument, record each external instrument's high at the pivot bar. Bearish divergence occurs when the traded instrument makes a higher pivot high than its preceding pivot high while either external instrument has a lower high at the corresponding pivot bars. Bullish divergence is the mirror image using lower pivot lows and higher external lows. A divergence contributes to its direction's score for a configured number of bars after confirmation, provided divergence detection is enabled.

For each direction, require a confirmed setup: a liquidity sweep, an inverted price gap, or an order block. A bullish sweep penetrates the latest confirmed pivot low, closes back above it with a sufficiently large lower wick, closes above its open, has a sufficiently large body if displacement is required, and is no farther than a configured ATR distance above that pivot. A bearish sweep mirrors these conditions. A bullish inverted gap is a retained bearish three-candle gap that price has closed above; a bearish inverted gap is a retained bullish gap that price has closed below. Require the current bar to overlap the inverted gap, close in the setup direction, and satisfy the displacement filter. Construct gaps from nonoverlapping highs and lows separated by one candle, subject to a minimum size and a configured retention limit; remove an inverted gap when price crosses its far boundary. A bullish order block is the preceding candle's body when a confirmed bullish displacement candle closes above the preceding high; a bearish block is the mirror image. Retain a configured number per direction and remove a block when price closes through its configured mitigation level. A block setup requires overlap, a close in its direction, and the displacement filter. Gap and block detection depend on their respective display switches. The pattern selector gates sweeps and inverted gaps but does not gate order blocks; selecting divergence alone does not make divergence a confirmed setup.

Score each direction by adding one point for each of: its confirmed setup; recent same-direction external divergence; a recent same-direction liquidity sweep; a matching persistent market-structure direction; overlap with a same-direction ordinary gap, inverted gap, or order block; a same-direction close-sequence confirmation firing on this bar; price in the favorable extreme of the rolling high-low range; and price on the favorable side of the recorded New York session open. Market structure switches bullish when close exceeds the latest confirmed pivot high and bearish when close falls below the latest confirmed pivot low. Close-sequence confirmation fires on a confirmed bar after consecutive closes in one direction, subject to an alternating-direction rule and a minimum interval; it depends on its display switch. The favorable range extreme is discount for longs and premium for shorts.

On a confirmed bar, issue a long or short setup when its confirmed setup and score meet the configured grade threshold, its direction's signal cooldown has elapsed, and the optional ATR-based chop filter passes. The lower grade requires a score below the higher-grade threshold. The higher grade additionally requires the optional active-session and volume filters and, when configured to block rather than warn, prohibits longs in premium and shorts in discount. Signal display switches control whether the corresponding grade is displayed and update the per-direction cooldown; alerts use the same grade conditions. Early directional indications can also arise from a current pivot penetration, inverted-gap overlap, or newly confirmed external divergence, subject to the selected pattern mode and chop filter. Risk levels shown for a higher-grade signal use the signal close as entry, an ATR-based stop, and targets expressed as multiples of the stop distance; the displayed levels are cleared when the stop or first target is touched.
