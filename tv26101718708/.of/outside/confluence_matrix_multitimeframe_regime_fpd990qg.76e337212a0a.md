Score direction by combining trend (moving-average alignment, normalized distance from the slow average, and regression slope quality), trend quality, momentum (oscillator level and change), range position and swing breakouts, volume flow, and contrarian deviation from a moving average. Weight these factors according to regime: emphasize trend and momentum in trends, mean reversion in ranges, and reduce confidence when volatility is elevated. Optionally adjust each factor's weight using its past follow-through versus the instrument's unconditional directional follow-through. Blend the timeframe scores into fast, medium, and slow horizon scores, giving greater influence to the timeframes suited to each horizon. Add a directional tilt from value location, positioning, a correlation-qualified related-market leader, and the implied-versus-realized volatility premium. Reduce confidence when order flow is toxic, flow is self-exciting, implied volatility is stressed, or a recent statistical change point signals a regime shift. Classify each horizon as bullish when its blended directional score is positive beyond a neutral deadband, bearish when it is negative beyond that deadband, and mixed otherwise. Treat bullish as long bias, bearish as short bias, and mixed as no clear position; the indicator provides no explicit position-exit rule.
