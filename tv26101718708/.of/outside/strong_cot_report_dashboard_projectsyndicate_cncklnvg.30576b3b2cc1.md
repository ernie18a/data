For each traded market, use its weekly CFTC positioning data to calculate non-commercial and commercial net positions, each net position's location within its rolling historical range, non-commercial net position as a share of open interest, weekly changes in net position and open interest, and the history of the net-position share. If the non-commercial index is high and the commercial index is low, mark a potential top; if the non-commercial index is low and the commercial index is high, mark a potential bottom. If the commercial index is unavailable, substitute the complement of the non-commercial index. After a sustained crowded positioning regime, go short at a potential top or long at a potential bottom only when the traded market confirms a reversal. Opposing commercial positioning and a reversal in weekly non-commercial net changes strengthen the setup. Exit a fade when the positioning index returns toward its midpoint or price reaches a mid-range value area; stop beyond the swing that made the extreme. For a continuation trade, go long when non-commercial net positioning is building upward above its historical midpoint, or short when it is building downward below its midpoint, provided weekly changes are consistently aligned, open interest is expanding, the net-position share progresses in the same direction, and the traded market confirms continuation. Exit when the positioning state reaches a potential top or bottom, or when the ongoing price structure decisively breaks. Otherwise, take no trade.
