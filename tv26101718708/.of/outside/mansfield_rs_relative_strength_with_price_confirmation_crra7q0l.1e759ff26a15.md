Compare the traded instrument’s price with a broad-market benchmark by dividing its price by the benchmark’s weekly close, then normalize that ratio against its rolling average. A cross above the neutral level signals bullish bias; a cross below signals bearish bias. Confirm bullishness when the instrument is above a rising price average, and bearishness when it is below a falling average. Agreement between positive relative strength and bullish price confirmation is the strongest long signal; agreement between negative relative strength and bearish confirmation indicates weakness to avoid. A turn in relative-strength momentum upward or downward also signals a bullish or bearish shift.
