During the regular session, compare short-term trend, momentum, VWAP position and RSI with higher-timeframe trend, NYSE advance/decline, up/down volume balance, smoothed TICK and cumulative TICK direction, and the traded index’s relative strength versus the S&P. Award directional support points for bullish or bearish conditions; choose the side with the higher score only when it clears the required score and lead over the opposite side, and its short-term trend agrees with VWAP. Require internals and relative-strength data when enabled. Veto a long when volatility rises sharply, or a short when it falls sharply; downgrade when multiple internals oppose, and never allow a full entry when all three oppose. An optional mode requires the signal to persist through a completed signal-timeframe bar. Treat lower scores as caution, not entry. Before allowing entry, block if the structural stop is too wide, reward relative to risk is inadequate, nearby structure leaves insufficient room, or divergence/exhaustion signals a dangerous entry near a level. Strong, aligned continuation may soften a divergence warning and treat nearby breakable levels as checkpoints. If several extension warnings coincide, defer entry until a pullback. The framework defines risk stops and targets but no independent position-exit signal.
