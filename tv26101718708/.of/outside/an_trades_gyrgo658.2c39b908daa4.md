On each bar, evaluate long and short entries independently. Parameters control the comparison instrument and lookback, pivot and sweep lookbacks, gap tracking limit, delivery and wick thresholds, and whether delivery, comparison divergence, liquidity and wick filters are enabled.

Track confirmed pivot highs and lows using equal left and right lookbacks. A bullish sweep occurs when the low breaks the latest confirmed pivot low and the close recovers above it; a bearish sweep occurs when the high breaks the latest pivot high and the close falls below it. Each sweep remains valid for the configured number of bars, including its bar.

Track bullish gaps between the high two bars ago and the current low, and bearish gaps between the current high and the low two bars ago. Limit each raw gap list by discarding its oldest member when full. Process new and existing gaps on every bar, including their creation bar. When a bar overlaps a bullish gap, convert it to a bearish inverted gap if the close is below its bottom; discard it if the close is above its top; otherwise mark it touched. Mirror these rules for bearish gaps. An inverted gap inherits whether the raw gap had previously been touched. A newly formed inverted gap qualifies on its formation bar. Subsequently, a bullish inverted gap qualifies when the bar overlaps its full range, or only its bottom-to-midpoint half if previously touched; a bearish inverted gap uses its full range, or midpoint-to-top half if previously touched. Check qualification before removing a bullish inverted gap when the close exceeds its top, or a bearish one when the close falls below its bottom.

For delivery confirmation, maintain the most recent open of each bullish and bearish candle whose body is at least the configured fraction of its range. Update these opens before testing the current bar. Bullish confirmation occurs when the close exceeds the stored bearish open, then clears that open; bearish confirmation occurs when the close falls below the stored bullish open, then clears it.

For comparison divergence, bullish divergence requires the traded instrument's low to break its prior lookback low while the comparison instrument's low does not reach or break its own prior lookback low. Bearish divergence requires the traded instrument's high to break its prior lookback high while the comparison instrument's high does not reach or break its own prior lookback high. Use data at the traded chart's timeframe.

Keep the configured number of most recent confirmed pivot highs and lows. Find the closest retained high strictly above the close and low strictly below it. If the liquidity filter is enabled and both exist, block long when the lower level is closer than the upper level, and block short when the upper level is closer than the lower level. If the wick filter is enabled, block long when the lower wick exceeds the configured multiple of average true range, and block short when the upper wick exceeds it.

Signal long when a bullish sweep is valid, a bullish inverted gap qualifies, enabled delivery and comparison filters have bullish confirmation, and neither applicable blocking filter blocks long. Signal short under the mirrored conditions. Both signals can occur on the same bar. The higher-timeframe bias and setup rating do not gate entries. The displayed stop and target levels do not generate exit signals.
