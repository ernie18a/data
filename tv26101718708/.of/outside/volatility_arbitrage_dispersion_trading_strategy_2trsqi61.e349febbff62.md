On each bar, calculate log returns for the traded instrument and a configurable weighted basket of enabled external instruments. Optionally exclude basket symbols matching the traded instrument or its base currency. Include only components with available rolling volatility, normalize their weights, and calculate the basket return as their weighted return sum. Require at least two components and a positive variance-decomposition denominator. From annualized rolling component and basket return volatilities, calculate basket correlation as (basket variance minus the sum of squared weighted component volatilities) divided by (the square of the weighted sum of component volatilities minus that same sum of squares), clamped to the valid correlation range.

Estimate the traded instrument's rolling beta to the basket as return correlation times the ratio of their return standard deviations. Accumulate the instrument's log return minus the prior bar's beta times the basket return, substituting zero for unavailable instrument return or prior beta. Fit a rolling AR(1) regression of this cumulative residual against its one-bar lag. Require its slope to be strictly between zero and one; derive the equilibrium, equilibrium standard deviation, and mean-reversion half-life from the regression. The score is the current cumulative residual's distance from equilibrium divided by its equilibrium standard deviation. Fair-value price is the current close multiplied by the exponential of equilibrium minus the cumulative residual.

Allow entries only when basket correlation meets its minimum, its rolling z-score is at or below the spike threshold or unavailable, the instrument's short-to-long realized-volatility ratio is at or below its limit, and, if enabled, external implied volatility minus annualized short realized volatility in percentage points meets the premium threshold. Also require a valid score, positive total basket weight, and half-life within configured bounds.

A raw long signal occurs when the score crosses upward through the negative entry threshold, or is at or below that threshold if the reversal hook is disabled, and remains above the negative statistical stop. A raw short signal occurs when the score crosses downward through the positive entry threshold, or is at or above it if the hook is disabled, and remains below the positive statistical stop. Optionally reverse the resulting long and short directions. Enter only while flat and after the configured cooldown since the position was observed closed.

At entry, fix an ATR-based stop on the loss side. Set a profit target either at fair-value price if it lies on the profit side, otherwise at a configured risk-reward multiple; directly at that risk-reward target; or omit the target, according to the selected mode. Also fix a time limit as the rounded entry half-life times a configured multiplier, bounded below by one bar and above by a configured cap. Starting after the entry bar, close a trade from a raw long signal when the score reaches or exceeds the negative exit threshold or falls to or below the negative statistical stop; close a trade from a raw short signal when the score reaches or falls below the positive exit threshold or rises to or above the positive statistical stop. These score exits follow the original raw signal even when direction is reversed. Close either trade when its time limit is reached, or when its fixed price stop or target is hit.
