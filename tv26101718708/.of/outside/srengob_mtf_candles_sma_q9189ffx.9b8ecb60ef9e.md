On each bar, identify alternating confirmed swing highs and lows: a candidate bar becomes a swing high when its high exceeds all highs in the subsequent lookback window, or a swing low when its low is below all lows in that window; retain the latest of each. When the close first breaks above the latest swing high, create a bullish order block from the candle with the lowest selected lower boundary between the swing and the breakout. When the close first breaks below the latest swing low, create a bearish block from the candle with the highest selected upper boundary in that interval. Boundaries use candle bodies or full ranges according to configuration. Remove bullish blocks when the candle body falls below their bottom and bearish blocks when the body rises above their top; use the newest remaining block of each type.

Calculate one direction-neutral score, rounding the total. Add configured weights for: close beyond either corresponding swing; close inside either corresponding block; either block's size divided by fast ATR exceeding the strength threshold, otherwise add a smaller baseline; either bullish candle with a sufficiently large upper wick or bearish candle with a sufficiently large lower wick, measured against both body and range; GC1! futures volume at the chart timeframe exceeding its moving average by either configured multiplier or above-average margin, or rising over the configured consecutive bars; price sweeping either block's outer boundary and closing back across it; close above its SMA with a bullish swing break or below it with a bearish swing break; and the bar's hour within the configured active session. Also add a momentum weight when the absolute close change over the configured lookback exceeds an ATR multiple, subtract a choppiness penalty when candle range divided by ATR is below its threshold, and add a reversal weight when consecutive candles have opposite directions.

Add a momentum contribution using the first applicable condition: the largest contribution for an impulse in either direction, a smaller contribution for an early signal in either direction, another contribution for sufficiently large absolute ATR-normalized momentum, or a divergence contribution for either divergence. Add a separate bonus for high relative chart-instrument volume on a directional candle. A bullish divergence means fast percentage rate of change exceeds slow rate and is rising while close falls; bearish is the reverse. An early directional signal requires positive or negative acceleration in the same direction as the latest close change, plus either corresponding divergence, elevated relative volume on a matching candle, or a close sufficiently near the directional end of the range with above-average volume. An impulse requires either that early signal or sufficiently strong directional ATR-normalized momentum, together with the corresponding swing break or divergence. These score contributions are direction-neutral, even when their underlying conditions are directional.

Signal long when the rounded score meets its threshold, close is above the latest swing high, inside the newest bullish block, and above the close SMA. Signal short when the score meets its threshold, close is below the latest swing low, inside the newest bearish block, and below the close SMA. Otherwise wait. No exit rule is specified.
