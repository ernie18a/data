On confirmed bars within the configured trading window, evaluate entries only after the configured cooldown and subject to direction restrictions. For a long, require the traded instrument's fast close EMA above its slow close EMA, close above an EMA of typical price, and, if enabled, the benchmark close above its EMA on the selected benchmark timeframe. For a short, reverse these comparisons. Also require the optional average dollar-volume and relative-volume filters to pass.

Require a qualifying trigger in the selected entry mode. A long breakout closes above both the ATR-based upper channel around the typical-price EMA and the previous high; a short breakout closes below the lower channel and previous low. A long continuation requires a recent low near either the fast EMA or basis, an optional recent-low hold above a tolerance below the basis, and a current high and close above the previous high, with close above the fast EMA. Reverse these conditions for a short. The continuation also requires the previous close to lie within the previous bar's range.

Require the corresponding directional quality score to reach its threshold. Calculate it as a weighted sum of bounded linear scales of relative volume, ATR-normalized distance beyond the directional channel, directional close rate of change, absolute candle body divided by ATR, and directional fast-to-slow EMA gap divided by ATR, plus a weighted regime score. Classify the regime in priority order as trending when choppiness is low, normalized basis slope is sufficiently large, and closes persist on either side of the basis; expanding when ATR relative to its average is high and choppiness is below its expansion threshold; quiet when choppiness is high and ATR is below its average; otherwise noisy. Choppiness uses the logarithm of summed true range divided by the range of highs and lows over the regime window. Use separately configured scale bounds, weights, regime scores, and thresholds.

Size each entry as the smaller of the equity risk budget divided by the distance from signal close to the initial stop and the allocation budget divided by signal close; optionally round down to whole units. The long initial stop is below the lower of the signal-bar low and basis by an ATR multiple; the short stop is symmetrically above the higher of the high and basis. Enter only for positive size, closing an opposite position first. Use the signal-close-to-seed-stop distance as initial risk around the actual average fill price. Exit at the initial stop or a target at a configured multiple of that risk. On any bar that reaches one initial risk of favorable movement, optionally move that bar's stop to a buffered breakeven level and optionally activate an ATR-based trailing exit. Close after the configured holding period. The breakeven and trailing activation tests are recalculated on each bar rather than latched.
