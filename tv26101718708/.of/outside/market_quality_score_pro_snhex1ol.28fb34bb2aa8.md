Evaluate on each chart bar using daily instrument and benchmark data. Classify the instrument as stock, fund, index, bond, cryptocurrency, currency, or commodity, using an optional manual mode and otherwise its metadata. Select a manual benchmark or an asset- and region-specific benchmark with fallback. Compare stocks, funds, indices, bonds, and non-Bitcoin cryptocurrencies with their benchmark; use absolute price strength for Bitcoin, currencies, and commodities.

Calculate a relative-strength score as the weighted average of available historical percentile ranks of long-term return excluding the most recent month, medium- and shorter-term returns, price relative to its rolling high, and the change in the instrument-to-benchmark price ratio. Use benchmark-excess returns for benchmarked instruments and absolute returns otherwise; use price itself instead of the ratio when no comparison is required. Smooth this score for indices. For stocks, calculate a fundamental score from available earnings growth, revenue growth, and return on equity: cap and normalize each metric and take their weighted average. Prefer quarterly revenue growth to trailing growth, and trailing return on equity to annual return on equity or net income divided by positive equity. Calculate a trend score as the weighted average of normalized, clipped measures of price distance above its long moving average, the short-to-long moving-average spread, and drawdown from a rolling closing high relative to a volatility-adjusted drawdown limit. Renormalize each score over available components.

Measure coverage as the weighted fraction of available strength, fundamental, and trend inputs, with fundamentals included only for stocks. Combine coverage with a graded history-availability score to obtain confidence. Form the total score as the weighted average of usable strength, fundamental, and trend scores, renormalizing over available components; require minimum strength coverage and a valid benchmark when the strength calculation uses one. Smooth the total for indices.

A standard long condition requires full history, minimum buy coverage and confidence, a benchmark when required by the configured strict-benchmark setting, and the total score at or above the buy threshold on both the current and previous bars. If the optional safety filter is enabled, also require price above its long moving average and drawdown within the volatility-adjusted limit; stocks additionally require minimum average turnover, while funds require it only when volume is available. Emit a standard long signal when this condition first becomes true, unless the strong-long condition is also true.

A strong long requires the standard gates, higher minimum strength, trend, coverage and confidence, a tighter drawdown limit, and for stocks a minimum fundamental score. It also requires the total score at or above the strong threshold for both the current and previous bars, higher than several bars ago, positive smoothed score momentum, and a closing price at or above the previous rolling price high. Emit a strong-long signal when this condition first becomes true.

Emit an exit when the total score crosses below the standard buy threshold, provided minimum history and watch-level coverage are available. Separately emit a sell/avoid alert when the total score crosses below the lower failure threshold. No short or options decision is defined.
