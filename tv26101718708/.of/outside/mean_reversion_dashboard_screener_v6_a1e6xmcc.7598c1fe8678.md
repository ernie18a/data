On each bar, calculate a close-price EMA and bands at a configurable multiple of the close-price standard deviation around it. Calculate RSI, ATR, local ADX, and the percentile rank of band width, where band width is (upper band − lower band) / EMA. Obtain the selected benchmark's ADX, close and close-price SMA, and the VIX close on the same timeframe. Block all signals when the optional earnings filter is enabled and the next earnings time falls within its specified window relative to the current time; missing earnings data does not block signals.

For mean reversion, require VIX below its configured limit, benchmark ADX below its limit, local ADX below its limit, and band-width percentile at or above its minimum. Go long when close is below the lower band and RSI is below its oversold threshold, provided the EMA-minus-close reward is positive and its ratio to close minus (low minus an ATR multiple) meets the minimum reward-to-risk ratio. Go short when close is above the upper band and RSI is above its overbought threshold, provided the close-minus-EMA reward is positive and its ratio to (high plus an ATR multiple) minus close meets the minimum. Treat a nonpositive risk as a zero ratio.

When momentum scanning is enabled, go long if the instrument's fast EMA exceeds its slower EMA, which exceeds its intermediate SMA, which exceeds its long SMA; benchmark close exceeds its SMA; VIX is below the separate momentum limit; band-width percentile is below the squeeze limit; short-period average volume is below long-period average volume; current high exceeds the highest high over the breakout lookback ending on the previous bar; and current volume exceeds a configured multiple of its long-period average. Current-bar volume is included in both volume averages.

If multiple signals coincide, prioritize mean-reversion long, then mean-reversion short, then momentum long. Otherwise produce no signal. The calculated mean-reversion stop and target are previews and do not generate exit signals.
