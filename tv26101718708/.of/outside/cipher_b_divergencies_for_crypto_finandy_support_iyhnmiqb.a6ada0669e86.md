On each confirmed chart bar, enter only when flat. Calculate a local trend as a configurable higher-timeframe simple moving average of the traded instrument’s close. Calculate a global trend from Bitcoin perpetual futures: compare its close with its configurable simple moving average on a configurable timeframe. The global long filter passes if the current or previous Bitcoin close is above its corresponding average; the short filter passes if either is below. On an unconfirmed chart bar, request the previous Bitcoin timeframe bar’s values instead of the current bar’s values.

Calculate WaveTrend from the traded instrument’s chosen source: subtract its channel-length EMA, divide by a scaled EMA of the absolute deviation, smooth with an EMA, and smooth that result with an SMA. An ordinary bullish signal occurs when the EMA-smoothed line crosses above the SMA-smoothed line while the latter is oversold; the bearish counterpart crosses below while overbought.

For divergence signals, identify strict five-bar oscillator peaks or troughs, confirmed two bars after their center. Compare the center’s price high or low and oscillator value with the most recent qualifying same-type fractal known two bars before confirmation. A regular bullish divergence has a lower price low and higher oscillator trough; a regular bearish divergence has a higher price high and lower oscillator peak. WaveTrend divergences require the oscillator center to meet either of two configurable oversold or overbought threshold sets. Optionally include regular RSI divergences with their own thresholds and regular stochastic-RSI divergences without thresholds. Enable each oscillator’s divergences using its respective setting; both WaveTrend threshold sets participate whenever WaveTrend divergences are enabled. Hidden divergences do not trigger entries. A special bullish signal also qualifies when an enabled primary WaveTrend or RSI bullish divergence occurs, the previous qualifying WaveTrend trough was below an extreme threshold, the current WaveTrend value has recovered above it by a specified minimum amount, and RSI at that previous trough was below its threshold.

With divergence-only mode disabled, use only the ordinary bullish or bearish signal. With it enabled, use the corresponding enabled regular divergence, including the special bullish signal. Enter long when the bullish signal is valid, the traded close is above its local average, and the global long filter passes. Enter short when the bearish signal is valid, the traded close is below its local average, and the global short filter passes. Both global filters can pass simultaneously.

At entry, fix a percentage stop and target from the signal bar’s close: below and above respectively for a long, above and below for a short. Exit when either price is reached. If the optional local-trend exit is enabled, also close a long whenever the close is below the local average, or a short whenever it is above.
