On each bar, confirm pivot highs and lows for the traded instrument and a correlated instrument using the same configurable number of bars on each side. Update each instrument's most recent confirmed pivot before evaluating signals. A bearish signal requires a newly confirmed traded-instrument high above its most recently recorded high as of twice the pivot lookback ago, and a newly confirmed correlated-instrument high below its most recently recorded high. A bullish signal requires the corresponding traded-instrument low to be lower and the correlated-instrument low to be higher. Both signals are impossible: when a correlated pivot is confirmed, its most recently recorded pivot has already been updated to that same value; otherwise, the pivot value is unavailable.
