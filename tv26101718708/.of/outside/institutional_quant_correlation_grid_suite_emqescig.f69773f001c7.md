On each bar, compare the traded instrument with a selected benchmark on the same timeframe. Over a configurable lookback, calculate the Pearson correlation of their closes. Calculate each instrument's close-to-close return multiplied by its volume divided by its rolling average volume, then calculate the Pearson correlation of those weighted returns. Calculate relative-strength momentum as the percentage deviation of the instrument-to-benchmark close ratio from its rolling average. Calculate the spread score by standardizing the logarithm of that ratio using its rolling mean and standard deviation. Calculate beta as the rolling average of the product of each return's deviation from its own contemporaneous rolling mean, divided by the rolling average of the squared benchmark-return deviation from its contemporaneous rolling mean.

Signal buy when both correlations meet or exceed the correlation threshold, relative-strength momentum is positive, beta exceeds its minimum, and the spread score is below its upper threshold. Signal hold when close correlation meets or exceeds the correlation threshold, the absolute relative-strength momentum is at or below its neutral limit, and the absolute spread score is below its threshold. Signal sell or avoid when relative-strength momentum is negative, close correlation is below its separate minimum, or the spread score reaches or exceeds its upper threshold. Evaluate these signals independently; they can overlap. Do not trigger a condition when required inputs are unavailable.
