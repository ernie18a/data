Calculation: Ribbon = two ALMAs of close with the same length and sigma; the first has a larger offset than the second.
Calculation: Adaptive average of source = previous average plus the squared smoothing coefficient times (source minus previous average), initialized at zero; efficiency is abs(source minus source at the lookback) divided by the sum of absolute bar-to-bar source changes over that lookback, or zero if the sum is zero; smoothing coefficient is the square of the linear interpolation between slow and fast smoothing constants using efficiency.
Calculation: Cloud base = long-lookback adaptive average of close. Cloud fan = adaptive averages of OHLC4 at a sequence of increasing lookbacks ending at the cloud base lookback. Cloud top = cloud base multiplied by one plus a parameter times the mean of (shorter-lookback fan average minus adjacent longer-lookback fan average) divided by the longer-lookback average, across the fan.
Calculation: Regime votes = for each direction, add one vote if the fraction of adjacent cloud fan pairs ordered in that direction reaches an alignment threshold; add one if the rescaled-range Hurst estimate of close exceeds its threshold; add one if the EMA of the change in cloud-base velocity has that direction and the cloud base moves in that direction. Velocity is the cloud-base change divided by ATR, or zero when ATR is zero. Compute Hurst periodically and retain its latest value; its estimate is log(range of cumulative deviations from the lookback mean divided by the population standard deviation) divided by log(lookback), clamped to the unit interval, with a neutral fallback when the ratio is zero.
Calculation: Consensus oscillator = SMA of a weighted average of smoothed stochastic RSI, money flow index of HLC3, and the percentile rank of CCI of close; each component has its own lookback.
Calculation: Regression candles = linear-regression endpoint of each OHLC field over the same lookback, with high raised to at least open and close and low lowered to at most open and close. Adaptive regression candles = each original OHLC field blended with its regression endpoint using a weight obtained by clamping the squared correlation of close with bar index between lower and upper thresholds and linearly mapping it between minimum and maximum blend weights; adjust high and low as for regression candles.
Calculation: Responsive Heikin Ashi candles = close is a weighted average of input OHLC with greater weight on close; estimate volume and (close minus open) times volume with separate scalar recursive filters whose gain is (previous error plus process noise) divided by (previous error plus process noise plus measurement noise), updating error to (1 minus gain) times (previous error plus process noise). Update the variance estimate of the latter series by exponential smoothing of its squared residual. Form a volume factor from the square root of capped volume divided by estimated volume, multiplied by a clamped linear function of the standardized price-volume residual, then blend that factor toward one. Open starts as the weighted input open and close divided by their weight sum and the volume factor; thereafter it moves toward the mean of its previous value and previous Heikin Ashi close at a speed equal to a capped function of responsiveness and the square root of the volume factor. High is the maximum of input high, Heikin Ashi open and close; low is their corresponding minimum. The input is either original OHLC or unadjusted linear-regression OHLC.
Calculation: Selected candle = original OHLC, responsive Heikin Ashi, adaptive regression, responsive Heikin Ashi of regression OHLC, or regression candles, according to the selected candle mode.
Calculation: Adaptive impulse histogram = shorter-lookback ALMA of close minus longer-lookback ALMA of close, minus an ALMA of that difference; the signal ALMA has its own lookback.
Calculation: Rail direction = for a polynomial-regression endpoint source, form raw upper and lower bands by adding and subtracting a factor times the SMA of true range. Replace the lower band with its previous raw value unless the current raw lower is higher or the previous source was below the previous raw lower; replace the upper band with its previous raw value unless the current raw upper is lower or the previous source was above the previous raw upper. Initialize bearish. If the previous rail line equaled the previous raw upper, switch bullish only when the current source exceeds the adjusted upper; otherwise switch bearish only when the current source is below the adjusted lower. The bullish line is the adjusted lower and the bearish line is the adjusted upper. Calculate two independent rails with different factors and periods; the rail with the shorter period also has the smaller factor.
Calculation: Signal lookback = a base polynomial lookback and two rail periods, optionally scaled upward for chart timeframes below a reference duration; optionally extend the polynomial lookback and, by a smaller proportion, the rail periods as EMA-smoothed close efficiency decreases. Quantize each to a positive lookback increment. Optionally replace the polynomial lookback with an optimized lookback: run the same polynomial and two-rail calculation independently at shorter, middle, and longer lookbacks around the scaled base. On each transition into both rails bullish or both bearish, record close, direction and ATR. On later bars score the greatest favorable high or low excursion in ascending ATR tiers; resolve at the highest tier, after a maximum age, or on the next test signal, and retain a bounded history of resolved scores. For each lookback, calculate the score average weighted by squared chronological rank, multiply its power by the number of scores, and use these weights to average the three lookbacks, rounded to an integer; use the middle lookback when total weight is zero.
Calculation: Signal candidates = fit a polynomial of fixed degree by least squares to close over the signal lookback, with equally spaced normalized time coordinates from oldest to newest; evaluate at the newest coordinate. A bullish candidate occurs when both independent rails are bullish and they were not both bullish on the preceding bar; a bearish candidate is defined symmetrically. Remember the direction of every raw candidate, even one that is held or cancelled.
Calculation: Signal release = on a bullish candidate, cancel any bearish holds; if the first ribbon ALMA is below the second and their adverse gap is widening, hold the candidate until a bullish ribbon crossover within a limited number of bars, cancelling it on an opposite candidate. Otherwise release immediately if the preceding raw candidate was not bullish and either bar-quality filtering is disabled or close is at least open and abs(close minus open) divided by max(high minus low, a small positive constant) reaches the body-ratio threshold. If that quality test fails, hold until a qualifying bullish bar within a separate limited number of bars, cancelling on an opposite candidate. A ribbon-hold release does not require bar quality or deduplication. Bearish release reverses the inequalities and uses a bearish ribbon crossunder. Holds expire after their respective limits. Quality and regime gates change signal grade only; both grades retain their direction.
