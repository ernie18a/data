Calculation: Structural pivots = confirmed pivot highs and lows with equal left and right lookbacks; retain a limited number of the most recently confirmed pivots of each type.
Calculation: Higher-timeframe bias = on the last completed higher-timeframe bar, compare the two most recently confirmed pivot highs and the two most recently confirmed pivot lows, using an equal left and right lookback. Bullish bias requires both latest pivots to be higher than their predecessors; bearish bias requires both to be lower; otherwise bias is neutral.
Calculation: Bullish candidate = on confirmation of an asymmetric impulse pivot high in fast mode, or a symmetric pullback pivot low in slow mode, select the most recent retained structural pivot low before that pivot as the origin and the most recent retained structural pivot high before the origin as the break reference. The impulse is the triggering pivot high in fast mode; in slow mode it is the highest retained structural pivot high between the origin and the pullback. The impulse pivot uses the structural left lookback and a separately chosen right lookback; the pullback pivot uses a separate equal left and right lookback.
Calculation: Bearish candidate = on confirmation of an asymmetric impulse pivot low in fast mode, or a symmetric pullback pivot high in slow mode, select the most recent retained structural pivot high before that pivot as the origin and the most recent retained structural pivot low before the origin as the break reference. The impulse is the triggering pivot low in fast mode; in slow mode it is the lowest retained structural pivot low between the origin and the pullback. The impulse pivot uses the structural left lookback and a separately chosen right lookback; the pullback pivot uses a separate equal left and right lookback.
Calculation: Active leg = a qualifying bullish or bearish candidate replaces any active leg of the opposite direction; a candidate in the active direction does not replace its locked origin and impulse. Process higher-timeframe conflict first, bullish candidate second, bearish candidate third, then invalidation and age expiry. Clear a bullish leg when close is below its origin minus an ATR buffer, or enabled higher-timeframe bias is bearish; clear a bearish leg when close is above its origin plus an ATR buffer, or enabled higher-timeframe bias is bullish. Clear either leg when bars since its impulse pivot exceed a maximum age.
Calculation: Golden zone = the interval between two chosen Fibonacci retracement ratios measured from the active leg's impulse toward its origin; the deeper ratio is greater than the shallower ratio. A touch occurs when the bar's low is at or below the upper boundary AND its high is at or above the lower boundary; count only the first touch per active leg.
Long: Higher-timeframe bias is bullish.
Short: Higher-timeframe bias is bearish.
Long: A bullish candidate exists AND its impulse is above its break reference AND impulse minus origin is at least a displacement multiple of current ATR AND (fast mode is enabled OR the pullback low is above the origin AND below the impulse) AND (higher-timeframe filtering is disabled or unavailable OR higher-timeframe bias is bullish) AND the active leg is not bullish.
Short: A bearish candidate exists AND its impulse is below its break reference AND origin minus impulse is at least a displacement multiple of current ATR AND (fast mode is enabled OR the pullback high is below the origin AND above the impulse) AND (higher-timeframe filtering is disabled or unavailable OR higher-timeframe bias is bearish) AND the active leg is not bearish.
Long: The active leg is bullish AND the golden zone is touched for the first time.
Short: The active leg is bearish AND the golden zone is touched for the first time.
Exit: long, the active leg is bullish AND close is below its origin minus an ATR buffer.
Exit: short, the active leg is bearish AND close is above its origin plus an ATR buffer.
