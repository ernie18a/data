Calculation: Confirmed bullish divergence = a newly confirmed RSI pivot low has a higher RSI than the preceding confirmed RSI pivot low, while the low at the new pivot is lower than the low at the preceding pivot; bearish divergence reverses both comparisons. Evaluate on the confirmation bar.
Calculation: Support and resistance candidates = the latest confirmed higher-timeframe pivot high and low; higher-timeframe shorter and longer EMAs; selected best-trade, best-long, best-short and best-support/resistance moving averages; Fibonacci retracements and optional extensions of the latest confirmed pivot high–low range; displaced chart-timeframe Ichimoku cloud boundaries; chart-timeframe shorter and longer EMAs and shorter adaptive moving average; optional Bollinger or Donchian boundaries; session VWAP; qualifying chart-timeframe pivot levels; and active trend lines, channel boundaries and channel midlines. Retain positive candidates within a configured ATR distance of close; classify each by whether it is above or below close and assign its source-specific weight. Higher-timeframe calculations use the last completed higher-timeframe bar. Fibonacci levels use the latest independently confirmed pivot high and low, irrespective of their order.
Calculation: Best moving averages = evaluate a configured set of short-to-long SMAs and EMAs and two adaptive moving averages. For each, simulate a long at each close crossing above it and a short at each close crossing below it, closing the opposite simulated position at that close. Periodically select the average with the largest cumulative closed long profit as best-long, the largest cumulative closed short profit as best-short, and the largest combined closed profit plus open profit as best-trade. Select best-support/resistance by the highest sum of support and resistance non-break percentages: a support touch occurs when the previous close is above the current average and the low reaches its tolerance zone, and is broken if the current close is below it; reverse these conditions for resistance. Each adaptive average starts at its SMA and recursively adds smoothing factor times (close minus previous adaptive average), where efficiency is absolute close change over its lookback divided by the sum of absolute differences between the current close and each preceding close in that lookback, and smoothing factor is the square of the linear interpolation between configured fast and slow EMA smoothing constants using that efficiency.
Calculation: Qualifying chart-timeframe pivot levels = maintain separate bounded, newest-first pools of confirmed pivot lows and highs; discard a new pivot within a configured ATR distance of an existing same-side pivot. Initialize each level with one touch and its pivot-bar volume. Increment touches when low or close is near a support, or high or close is near a resistance, subject to a minimum separation between touches. Increment violations on each close beyond the level by a configured ATR distance. Its weight is touches minus violations plus a volume bonus determined by pivot volume relative to its current moving average. Include levels with at least the configured minimum touches and positive weight.
Calculation: Active trend lines and channels = for each of long, intermediate and short pivot lookbacks, retain recent confirmed pivot lows and highs. Form ascending lines from pairs of higher lows and descending lines from pairs of lower highs. Reject lines with more than the permitted number of close or wick violations from the older pivot through the current bar, or with current close too far on the wrong side. Rank remaining lines by a weighted sum of separated ATR-proximity touches, negative violations and age, and retain the highest-ranked line for each direction and lookback when a new pivot is confirmed. Mark a line broken after the configured consecutive closes beyond its ATR buffer or one wick beyond its larger ATR buffer; replace it only after the configured delay. A parallel channel is offset from its line by the greatest opposite-side high or low since the older pivot and is valid if it has enough separated touches; invalidate it when close breaks its buffered outer boundary. Channel support proximity compares low with an ascending line or descending channel boundary; resistance proximity compares high with a descending line or ascending channel boundary. Trend-line and channel weights depend on pivot lookback and line touch count.
Calculation: Ranked support and resistance = merge nearby same-side candidates in source order, summing weights and retaining the nearest-to-close price and the far edge of each merged group; merge nearby opposite-side groups into the higher-weight side, with ties favoring support. Rank each side by descending weight after excluding levels beyond a configured ATR distance. This ranking is recalculated only on the last chart bar; on historical bars its initialized levels are unavailable.
Calculation: Directional reward/risk = for long, use the highest-ranked resistance minus an ATR target buffer as target and the far edge of the highest-ranked support minus an ATR instant-stop buffer as stop; for short, reverse sides and signs. If the first target is too close to close and a second target exists, use the second target. Divide directional target distance from close by directional stop distance from close, only when the latter is positive.
Calculation: Directional score = clamp to the score range the sum of trend component times an ADX-dependent trend multiplier, support/resistance component times an inverse ADX-dependent multiplier, momentum component, higher-timeframe component and reward/risk component. The long trend component awards configured weights for close above best-long average, shorter EMA above longer EMA, rising shorter adaptive average, MACD above its signal, and close above the displaced Ichimoku cloud; the short component reverses comparisons and uses best-short. Cap each component at its configured limit. The long support/resistance component awards weights for no nearby higher-timeframe resistance, close above a lower Fibonacci retracement, close above the cloud, proximity to the newest pivot-low level, close above the main ascending trend line, channel-support proximity, a descending trend-line break, that break combined with bullish divergence, and an upward Bollinger squeeze release; subtract weights for channel-resistance proximity and close below the newest pivot-low level by its ATR buffer. Reverse these conditions for short, using the higher Fibonacci retracement and newest pivot-high level. Clamp this component between zero and its cap. Momentum awards weights for RSI below the overbought threshold and bullish divergence for long, or RSI above the oversold threshold and bearish divergence for short. Higher-timeframe scoring awards weights for shorter EMA above longer EMA, RSI below its upper threshold, chart close above the undisplaced higher-timeframe cloud and higher-timeframe volume above its volume average by a configured factor, and subtracts a weight for volume below its average by another factor; reverse trend, RSI and cloud comparisons for short. Reward/risk contributes a capped piecewise linear function of directional reward/risk, or zero when unavailable. Use last completed higher-timeframe bars for all higher-timeframe inputs. Optionally subtract the sum of weights of eligible opposing candidates within a configured ATR distance, or set the score to zero when any such candidate exists; eligible sources can be restricted to Fibonacci, pivot and higher-timeframe levels. Use the score at chart-bar close.
Calculation: Bounce bonus = award a configured bonus when low reaches the newest pivot-low support's ATR zone and close is above it; for short use high near the newest pivot-high resistance and close below it. Add a bonus for a same-side rejection wick or engulfing candle at that bounce and another when the corresponding reward/risk meets its threshold. Add a divergence-sequence bonus based on the ratio of the current same-side consecutive divergence count to the historical mean length of completed same-side sequences. End a sequence on an opposite divergence or an RSI midpoint cross.
Calculation: Signal trail = on the first strong or normal signal of a side while its trail is absent, select the highest-weight candidate on the protective side of close, extend its reference to the farthest neighboring candidate within an ATR merge distance, and initialize the instant and close trails at separate ATR buffers beyond that reference; if no candidate exists, use an ATR-offset fallback reference. Save initial risk from entry close to instant trail. Once profit exceeds initial risk, examine the strongest candidates in rank order and move the reference to the first more protective level whose buffered instant stop remains farther than initial risk from close. Ratchet both buffered trails only toward profit. Independently ratchet the highest high minus an ATR multiple for long, or lowest low plus an ATR multiple for short, and combine its separately buffered values with the respective trails using the more protective value. Reset a side's trail after its instant stop is crossed.
Calculation: Position trail = initialize from the highest-weight protective candidate relative to entry price, using the same merged-reference, fallback, risk, ratcheting and ATR-following rules as the signal trail. When price approaches the strongest opposing ranked level and closes without breaking it, additionally move the reference to the most protective eligible level among the top-ranked protective levels. Maintain separate instant and close-buffer trails.
