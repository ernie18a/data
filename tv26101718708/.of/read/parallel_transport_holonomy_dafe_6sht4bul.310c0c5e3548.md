Calculation: Normalized price-volume vectors = For each fast, medium, and slow lookback, divide the sum of one-bar close changes by that lookback and the current exponentially weighted standard deviation of one-bar close changes; calculate the volume component analogously. Initialize the exponentially weighted means to zero and variances to a positive parameter, update the mean before the variance, and use a decreasing update weight with a maximum effective history parameter. Use zero for unavailable previous close and the current volume for unavailable previous volume. The fast lookback is shorter than the medium lookback, which is shorter than the slow lookback. The vector angle is atan2(volume component, price component).
Calculation: Transport deviation = Calculate one-bar fractional close and volume changes, using zero when the denominator is zero and flooring the previous volume at a positive parameter. Calculate their exponentially weighted second moments and cross moment with a weight determined by a rolling rescaled-range Hurst exponent. Form the price-volume covariance matrix from these moments, floor its diagonal and determinant at a positive parameter, and calculate the connection as one half of (inverse matrix element 11 times the change in covariance plus inverse element 12 times the change in volume variance minus inverse element 12 times the change in price variance), scaled by a clamped linear function of the Hurst exponent. For each vector, subtract its previous angle plus the connection times its scale-specific coefficient from its current angle, wrapping the result to the interval from minus pi to pi. Transport deviation is the weighted sum of the three wrapped differences. Initialize the moments to zero, use the current angle and matrix elements when their previous values are unavailable, and initialize the Hurst exponent to its neutral value.
Calculation: Smoothed transport = Recursive exponential average of transport deviation, initialized to zero.
Calculation: Pressure = After a warmup lookback, clamp the sum of the lower-wick fraction of the bar range, less the upper-wick fraction, each scaled up when its own rolling wick-length z-score exceeds a threshold; the close-location imbalance scaled by volume; a positive coefficient when close exceeds open and a negative coefficient otherwise; and a coefficient times transport deviation. Before warmup it is zero. Define a wick-length z-score as zero when its rolling standard deviation is zero.
Calculation: Holonomy spike = Sum transport deviation over a rolling lookback; calculate the absolute sum's exponentially weighted mean and variance, updating the mean before the variance, with the same decreasing update weight as the vector normalization. Initialize the mean to zero and the variance to a positive parameter. Divide the deviation from the updated mean by the standard deviation, clamp it, and clamp the resulting scaled value to a nonnegative normalized range. A spike occurs when this normalized value exceeds a threshold and the number of bars since the previous spike exceeds a cooldown. Initialize the elapsed-bar counter to zero and increment it before testing each bar.
Long: Smoothed transport > 0 OR (smoothed transport = 0 AND pressure > 0)
Short: Smoothed transport < 0 OR (smoothed transport = 0 AND pressure <= 0)
Long: Fast vector price component > 0 AND bar index modulo the vector-display interval = 0
Short: Fast vector price component <= 0 AND bar index modulo the vector-display interval = 0
Long: Holonomy spike = true AND rolling sum of transport deviation > 0 AND wrapped difference between fast and slow vector angles > 0
Short: Holonomy spike = true AND rolling sum of transport deviation < 0 AND wrapped difference between fast and slow vector angles < 0
