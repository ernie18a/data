Calculation: Standardized move = (close change − its rolling mean) / its rolling standard deviation, computed separately for gold, the dollar, the yield, and oil on each used timeframe. Use the last completed lower-timeframe bar and last completed same- or higher-timeframe bar.
Calculation: Market-to-gold direction = sign of the rolling Pearson correlation between that market’s and gold’s completed close changes, using completed higher-timeframe bars; use the longer available timeframe’s correlation, falling back to the shorter higher timeframe when insufficient pairs exist. If correlation is unavailable or weak, use the usual inverse direction for the dollar and yield; oil has no direction and is excluded.
Calculation: Aligned market move = standardized market move × its market-to-gold direction.
Calculation: Macro side and push = when the dollar and yield are both active and point in opposite directions, side is neutral; otherwise, side is the direction of the active dollar, yield, or oil moves. Include active dollar and yield moves and active oil only if it agrees with the side; push is their weighted mean.
Calculation: Open room = push − gold’s standardized move, retained only when its sign agrees with the macro side; otherwise zero.
Calculation: Context side = direction of the first active aligned dollar, yield, or eligible oil move, in that order, on each of the short and longer context timeframes; neutral if none is active or dollar and yield are both active in opposite directions.
Calculation: Unstable = the state turns on when the shorter-higher-timeframe dollar/gold correlation reaches the instability threshold and turns off when it falls below that threshold by the exit band.
Long: macro side is up AND dollar/yield are not opposed AND open room is at least the open-room threshold AND neither context side is down AND unstable is false
Short: macro side is down AND dollar/yield are not opposed AND open room is at least the open-room threshold in magnitude AND neither context side is up AND unstable is false
