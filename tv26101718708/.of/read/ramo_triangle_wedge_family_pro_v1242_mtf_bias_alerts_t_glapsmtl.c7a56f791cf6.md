Calculation: Pivot memory = On each completed chart bar, confirm pivot highs and lows using three ordered lookbacks: short, medium, and long, with equal left and right lengths. Process them in that order. Maintain separate newest-first high and low lists, each limited to a parameterized count. Merge a new pivot into the first existing pivot within parameterized bar and ATR distances, retaining the existing pivot bar, the more extreme price, and the larger pivot scale and pivot-bar ATR; otherwise insert it chronologically.
Calculation: Candidate boundaries = For every ordered pair of stored high pivots and every ordered pair of stored low pivots separated by at least a parameterized anchor gap, draw straight upper and lower lines. The start is the earlier old anchor, the geometry end is the later new anchor, and duration is current bar index minus start. Normalize each line slope by current ATR. Width is upper minus lower; contraction is one minus current width divided by start width; apex is the intersection of the lines; progress is elapsed duration divided by start-to-apex duration.
Calculation: Candidate touches and containment = Count stored pivots from the start onward whose distance from their corresponding line is at most the greater of a parameterized multiple of pivot-bar ATR and a parameterized percentage of the line price. Touch fit is one minus the mean normalized touch error, bounded to zero through one. Containment is the fraction of equally spaced sample bars from start through geometry end whose high and low lie within the respective lines plus or minus the greater of a parameterized ATR tolerance and price-percentage tolerance; count only samples where the upper line exceeds the lower line. For a horizontal zone, count same-side pivots near the mean anchor price using a separate ATR tolerance and the line price-percentage tolerance, skipping pivots too close in time to the last accepted pivot; calculate their time spread and newest age as fractions of pattern duration.
Calculation: Valid candidate = Duration is within its bounds; both anchor pairs meet the minimum gap; the four anchors include an alternating high-low-high or low-high-low sequence; lower slope exceeds upper slope; start width is at least a parameterized multiple of current ATR; current width exceeds a smaller parameterized multiple of current ATR; contraction exceeds its minimum; apex is ahead of the current bar; progress is within its bounds; each boundary has at least two touches and their combined touches reach a parameterized minimum; containment reaches its minimum; and at least one anchor has medium-or-long pivot scale or total touches reach a higher threshold. An ascending or descending triangle additionally requires its horizontal zone to meet minimum touch count and spread and maximum newest age.
Calculation: Pattern classification = For selection, classify a triangle in order as ascending when the upper normalized slope is within the flat threshold and lower slope is at least the minimum rising slope, descending when upper slope is at most the negative minimum slope and lower slope is within the flat threshold, or symmetric when upper slope is at most the negative minimum and lower slope is at least the minimum. Independently classify its displayed type with mutually exclusive flat and directional slopes: symmetric first, then ascending, then descending; a valid triangle requires both classifications. A rising wedge has both normalized slopes above the flat threshold, a minimum lower-minus-upper slope gap, and a bounded lower-to-upper slope ratio. A falling wedge has both slopes below the negative flat threshold, the same minimum slope gap, and a bounded absolute upper-to-lower slope ratio. A wedge also requires contraction to reach the greater of the general and wedge-specific minima.
Calculation: Candidate selection score = Use parameterized weighted sums of touch quality, containment, contraction quality, slope quality, apex-progress quality, and duration quality. Touch quality combines bounded total-touch count, touch balance, and mean boundary fit. Contraction quality rises linearly from the relevant minimum; progress quality decreases linearly with distance from a preferred progress; duration quality rises linearly between duration bounds. Triangle slope quality combines flatness and directional steepness for ascending or descending selection classifications, or both directional steepnesses and their symmetry for symmetric selection. Wedge slope quality combines minimum absolute slope, slope gap, and slope ratio. Bound each component to its permitted range. For displayed symmetric triangles, subtract a bounded penalty per stored pivot that breaches its boundary by more than the greater of an ATR tolerance and a price-percentage tolerance. Require selection score to reach its minimum. The displayed score uses the displayed triangle classification instead of the selection classification, subtracts a late-progress penalty, adds a bounded horizontal-zone touch bonus for directional triangles, and is bounded to its permitted range; wedges use their selection score less the late-progress penalty.
Calculation: Selected pattern = Scan candidates in newest-first pivot-pair order. Within triangles and wedges separately, replace the current best with a valid candidate if its duration exceeds the best duration by a parameterized ratio and its selection score is within a parameterized slack of the best, or if the best does not similarly dominate it and its score is higher. Select the best triangle when one exists; otherwise select the best wedge. A pattern identity consists of its displayed type and start bar.
Calculation: Parent repair = When enabled on an eligible chart timeframe, calculate the same selected pattern on a higher timeframe using its last completed bar. If the chart pattern is ascending or descending, the parent is symmetric, the chart close is within the parent boundaries plus or minus a chart-ATR tolerance, their midpoints are within that tolerance, and the parent displayed score is within a parameterized slack of the chart score, convert the parent's slopes to chart-bar units and anchor its current boundary values at the current chart bar. Replace the chart pattern's flat boundary with the corresponding parent boundary. If the repaired upper boundary exceeds the lower boundary and their apex remains ahead, classify the result as symmetric, use the earlier chart or converted parent start, recalculate width, contraction and progress, and clear its internal swings.
Calculation: Internal swings = For each boundary of a symmetric pattern, take the newest stored same-side pivot since pattern start whose distance from the boundary exceeds a parameterized multiple of the pivot touch tolerance; it is absent if no pivot qualifies.
Calculation: Breakout levels = Upper level is the current upper boundary plus the greater of a parameterized percentage of its absolute price, a parameterized multiple of current ATR, and a minimum price increment. Lower level is the current lower boundary minus the equivalent buffer. For a symmetric pattern, raise the upper level to at least its internal high plus the upper buffer when that swing exists, and lower the lower level to at most its internal low minus the lower buffer when that swing exists. Treat the minimum price increment as a parameter because it is not present in the backtest fields.
Calculation: Breakout state = On completed chart bars, reset direction, breakout bar, breakout boundary, retest, and failure whenever pattern identity changes or no valid pattern exists. For a new identity, record the first allowed breakout only if the opposite boundary has not been broken: ascending and falling-wedge patterns allow an upper breakout; descending and rising-wedge patterns allow a lower breakout; symmetric patterns allow either. Each requires progress at least the breakout minimum and close strictly beyond its breakout level. Save the unbuffered boundary as the breakout boundary. From the next bar through a parameterized maximum age, set the retest flag when an upward breakout has low at or below boundary plus retest tolerance and close at or above boundary minus tolerance, or a downward breakout has high at or above boundary minus tolerance and close at or below boundary plus tolerance. Retest tolerance is the greater of a parameterized percentage of absolute breakout boundary and a parameterized multiple of current ATR. In the same age window set failure when an upward breakout closes below boundary minus failure tolerance or a downward breakout closes above boundary plus failure tolerance; failure tolerance is the greater of a parameterized multiple of current ATR and a separate percentage of absolute breakout boundary. Retest and failure persist until reset.
Calculation: Entry risk = For a long reference boundary, proposed entry is boundary plus an ATR buffer and proposed stop is boundary minus a separate ATR buffer; reverse these signs for a short. Risk passes when the stop is on the appropriate side of entry and the entry-to-stop distance is no greater than both a parameterized percentage of entry and a parameterized multiple of current ATR.
Calculation: Directional bias = On the previous completed chart bar and the last completed bars of two successively higher timeframes, calculate RSI, relative volume as volume divided by its volume SMA, and candle direction as the sign of close minus open. On each timeframe, RSI contribution is zero between upper and lower RSI thresholds, otherwise the signed distance of RSI from its midpoint scaled and bounded to unit magnitude. Volume contribution is zero below the relative-volume threshold or for a flat candle; otherwise it is candle direction times the bounded scaled excess above that threshold. Combine RSI and volume contributions with parameterized weights, then combine the three timeframe scores with increasing weights for the higher timeframes.
Long: Selected pattern is ascending OR selected pattern is a falling wedge OR selected pattern is symmetric AND recorded breakout direction is long OR selected pattern is symmetric AND no breakout direction is recorded AND directional bias is at least its positive threshold.
Short: Selected pattern is descending OR selected pattern is a rising wedge OR selected pattern is symmetric AND recorded breakout direction is short OR selected pattern is symmetric AND no breakout direction is recorded AND directional bias is at most its negative threshold.
Long: Selected pattern is ascending, symmetric, or falling wedge AND progress is at least the breakout minimum AND close is above the upper breakout level AND the preceding bar's corresponding allowed-breakout condition is false.
Short: Selected pattern is descending, symmetric, or rising wedge AND progress is at least the breakout minimum AND close is below the lower breakout level AND the preceding bar's corresponding allowed-breakout condition is false.
Long: Selected pattern is ascending AND no breakout direction is recorded AND close is not below the lower breakout level AND low is at or below the lower boundary plus a parameterized ATR proximity AND close is at or above that boundary AND entry risk passes for that boundary.
Short: Selected pattern is descending AND no breakout direction is recorded AND close is not above the upper breakout level AND high is at or above the upper boundary minus a parameterized ATR proximity AND close is at or below that boundary AND entry risk passes for that boundary.
Long: Recorded breakout direction is long AND retest is true AND failure is false AND the selected pattern has no opposite-side breakout AND entry risk passes for the recorded breakout boundary.
Short: Recorded breakout direction is short AND retest is true AND failure is false AND the selected pattern has no opposite-side breakout AND entry risk passes for the recorded breakout boundary.
