Calculation: Confirmed swings = Chart-timeframe pivot highs and lows confirmed by the same number of bars on each side. Ignore bars confirming both a high and a low. Keep alternating swings, replacing consecutive swings of the same type only when the newer swing is more extreme. Retain swings within a lookback. Each swing carries ATR at its origin; its context is its price minus the close a lookback before its origin.
Calculation: Reversal pattern = A double top or bottom uses three alternating swings; a triple top or bottom or head-and-shoulders pattern uses five. The first swing's signed context must exceed an ATR threshold, and the span must fall within its allowed range. For tops use sign +1 and for bottoms sign -1. A double has matching signed outer prices within an ATR tolerance and an intervening signed price at least an ATR distance below both. A triple has three matching signed outer prices and both intervening signed prices at least an ATR distance below their adjacent outer prices. Head-and-shoulders requires the middle outer swing to exceed both shoulders by an ATR distance, shoulders to match within an ATR tolerance, the head to exceed the line through the intervening swings by an ATR distance, and the two half-spans to have an allowed ratio; it takes precedence over a triple. Also consider a double across intervening swings of the same type if none overtops the endpoints by the break tolerance or makes a central matching contact; use the most extreme opposite swing between the endpoints as the intervening swing. Use the last swing's ATR for these tests.
Calculation: Fitted pattern = Fit separate least-squares lines through the high and low swings of each recent alternating swing sequence. The upper line must exceed the lower line at every swing; each swing must lie within an ATR contact tolerance of its corresponding line, and consecutive contacts on the same line must be separated by a minimum number of bars. Every close between the first and last swing must stay within the lines plus an ATR break tolerance, and the fraction of bars whose wicks exceed the lines plus an ATR contact tolerance must not exceed a limit. The swing count and span must be within their allowed ranges. A converging pattern has a smaller final width, an upper slope below the lower slope, and a future intersection within an allowed distance. A parallel pattern has sufficiently similar slopes and widths. Use the last swing's ATR for these tests.
Calculation: Flag or pennant = A fitted pattern follows a pole whose close-to-close movement exceeds an ATR threshold and whose net movement divided by total absolute close-to-close travel exceeds a threshold. Search allowed pole lengths from shortest to longest and use the first qualifying pole. The pattern span and retracement must be within limits relative to the pole. A flag has parallel boundaries that are both flat or both slope against the pole; a pennant has converging boundaries with a falling upper and rising lower line and the required number of swings. Its direction is the pole direction.
Calculation: Pattern boundaries = For fitted patterns use their fitted lines. For a top use the highest endpoint price as the upper boundary and its neckline as the lower boundary; for a bottom use its neckline as the upper boundary and the lowest endpoint price as the lower boundary. A double neckline is the intervening swing price; a triple neckline is the lower intervening price for a top or the higher for a bottom. A head-and-shoulders neckline uses the slope through the intervening swings and the first intervening swing's price as its intercept.
Long: A confirmed bottom or inverse head-and-shoulders reversal pattern is detected.
Short: A confirmed top or head-and-shoulders reversal pattern is detected.
Long: A bullish flag or pennant is detected.
Short: A bearish flag or pennant is detected.
Long: A nonexpired, valid pattern's close exceeds its upper boundary plus an ATR break tolerance AND its expected direction is upward or unrestricted.
Short: A nonexpired, valid pattern's close falls below its lower boundary minus an ATR break tolerance AND its expected direction is downward or unrestricted.
Long: Close exceeds the latest confirmed swing high plus an ATR break tolerance AND that swing high has not already been broken.
Short: Close falls below the latest confirmed swing low minus an ATR break tolerance AND that swing low has not already been broken.
