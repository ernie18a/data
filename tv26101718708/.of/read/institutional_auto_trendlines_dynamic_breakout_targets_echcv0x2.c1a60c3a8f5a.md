Calculation: Adaptive volatility = a weighted combination of short- and long-period ATR and an EMA of close × sqrt(log(high / low)² / (4 × log(2))); use zero for the last component when low is not positive.
Calculation: Higher-timeframe bias = for each of two higher timeframes, using its last completed bar, classify it bullish when close > shorter EMA > longer EMA, bearish when close < shorter EMA < longer EMA, and neutral otherwise. Count bullish and bearish timeframes separately.
Calculation: Directional filter = optionally require the trend test, the momentum test, both, or neither. The long trend test is close > short EMA AND short EMA >= longer EMA; the short test reverses both comparisons. The long momentum test is an SMA of RSI >= its midpoint AND MACD histogram >= zero; the short test reverses both comparisons. Optionally require the count of higher-timeframe biases agreeing with the direction >= the opposing count. Optionally require volume / SMA(volume) >= a threshold; pass the volume test when the ratio is unavailable.
Calculation: Trendlines = maintain support and resistance lines separately. At each discovery interval, for each of a short, medium, and long lookback, test pairs among the most recent confirmed pivot lows or highs, respectively. Both anchors must lie within the lookback, their separation must exceed a fraction of it, and the absolute slope divided by current adaptive volatility and its arctangent must be within their respective permitted ranges. Project each candidate line through its anchors. Score recent same-side pivots within the lookback as touches when their wick, or optionally the relevant body edge, is within a volatility-scaled tolerance of the line; group touches separated by at least a minimum number of bars. Use historical adaptive volatility at each checked bar, or optionally current adaptive volatility. Sample bars at evenly spaced lookback intervals: a violation is a support close below the line minus a volatility-scaled tolerance or a resistance close above the line plus that tolerance; a respect hit is a candle crossing the line with its relevant body edge on the nonviolating side. Require minimum touch clusters and fit and maximum violation rate. Fit is one minus mean touch distance divided by mean touch tolerance, clamped to the unit interval. Raw quality is a weighted sum of normalized touch-cluster count, fit, inverse violation rate, respect hits and failures, fraction of touches closing on the nonviolating side, mean relevant wick fraction, touch span, touch recency, and confluence. Confluence is the clamped inverse of the smallest adaptive-volatility-normalized distance from the current line to a short, medium, or long EMA or session VWAP. Multiply quality by an optional higher-timeframe bias factor favoring agreement and penalizing strong opposition, then clamp it to the quality scale. For each side and lookback retain the highest-quality valid candidate if its quality reaches the creation threshold. A same-side line is a duplicate when current and projected price differences and normalized slope difference are within their thresholds; replace the first duplicate only when the candidate has greater raw quality, preserving its creation time and signal history. Otherwise add the candidate with its newest touch time and empty signal history.
Calculation: Line maintenance and ranking = effective quality is raw quality plus current confluence boost minus capped penalties for time since creation and last touch, clamped to the quality scale. Update last-touch time whenever the current relevant wick or optional body edge is within the touch tolerance. At discovery intervals remove lines exceeding maximum anchor or creation age, falling below the effective-quality removal floor, or optionally materially broken after a minimum creation age; if the line count exceeds its limit, remove the lowest-ranked lines. Rank each line within its side by effective quality plus weighted touch recency, cluster count, and respect score minus weighted adaptive-volatility-normalized distance from close; strictly higher scores rank ahead. A signal-eligible line has rank within the top limit, raw and effective quality above their signal thresholds, and more than the cooldown number of bars since its last signal.
Calculation: Break state = for each line, record the bar and direction of its latest breakout; clear the direction after a retest or failed break. A break is in its retest window only after its breakout bar, within the configured number of bars, and while its recorded direction remains set. Every breakout, retest, rejection, or failed break updates that line's last-signal bar. Evaluate breakout, then retest, then rejection, then failed break on each bar; breakout and rejection eligibility uses the signal-eligible state computed before these events.
Calculation: Target levels = on a selected breakout, retest, or rejection, set target entry to that signal bar's close and freeze the signal's line price. Set volatility band to the lesser of target-period ATR × a multiplier and entry × a price-percentage cap. ATR risk is the band × a distance multiplier. Structural risk is entry minus (line price minus band × a buffer multiplier) for a long, or (line price plus band × the buffer multiplier) minus entry for a short. Choose ATR risk, structural risk, or their maximum; risk is the maximum of the chosen risk and the band. Long stop = entry − risk and long target = entry + risk × reward/risk; short stop = entry + risk and short target = entry − risk × reward/risk. A new selected signal may replace an active target according to the replacement setting; otherwise retain it until a target or stop is hit. Evaluate the target before the stop, including on the signal bar.
Long: A signal-eligible resistance line AND close > current line + adaptive volatility × breakout buffer AND previous close <= previous line + current adaptive volatility × breakout buffer AND optional min(open, close) > current line + a fraction of that buffer AND long directional filter passes.
Short: A signal-eligible support line AND close < current line − adaptive volatility × breakout buffer AND previous close >= previous line − current adaptive volatility × breakout buffer AND optional max(open, close) < current line − a fraction of that buffer AND short directional filter passes.
Long: A resistance line has a recorded long break in its retest window AND bars since its last signal > cooldown AND effective quality >= signal threshold AND low <= current line + adaptive volatility × retest distance AND close > current line AND long directional filter passes.
Short: A support line has a recorded short break in its retest window AND bars since its last signal > cooldown AND effective quality >= signal threshold AND high >= current line − adaptive volatility × retest distance AND close < current line AND short directional filter passes.
Long: A signal-eligible support line AND low <= current line + adaptive volatility × retest distance AND close > current line + adaptive volatility × rejection buffer AND close > open AND long directional filter passes.
Short: A signal-eligible resistance line AND high >= current line − adaptive volatility × retest distance AND close < current line − adaptive volatility × rejection buffer AND close < open AND short directional filter passes.
Long: A line has a recorded short break in its retest window AND close > current line + a fraction of adaptive volatility × breakout buffer.
Short: A line has a recorded long break in its retest window AND close < current line − a fraction of adaptive volatility × breakout buffer.
Exit: long, an active long target exists AND (high >= target OR close <= stop, or optionally low <= stop).
Exit: short, an active short target exists AND (low <= target OR close >= stop, or optionally high >= stop).
