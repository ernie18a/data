Calculation: Confirmed pivots = pivot highs and lows confirmed after equal left and right lookbacks. Maintain separate newest-first high and low lists: on a change of pivot type, prepend the pivot; on consecutive pivots of the same type, prepend only a more extreme pivot. When the type changes and both lists contain more than three pivots, prepend the latest pivot of the preceding type to an alternating pivot history.
Calculation: Adaptive tolerance = a base tolerance multiplied by one plus a scaling parameter times the clamped z-score of ATR relative to its longer-lookback SMA and standard deviation.
Calculation: Candidate boundary = for three chronological same-type pivots, require the middle pivot's absolute deviation from linear interpolation between the outer pivots, divided by the absolute first pivot price with a positive floor, to be less than adaptive tolerance. The boundary starts at the first pivot and ends at the newest pivot's time at the projection of a line from the first pivot through the midpoint of the other two pivots. On each pivot-type change, test the latest three opposite-type pivots and the latest two completed current-type pivots together with the latest current-type pivot. Retain each successful high or low boundary until replaced or more than one pivot-type change has elapsed.
Calculation: Active channel = a pair of retained high and low boundaries formed on pivot-type changes after sufficient alternating pivot history exists. Their creation pivot counts differ by at most one; at least two pivot-type changes have elapsed since the preceding channel was created; the earlier boundary start is later than the preceding channel's later endpoint; the overlap of their time spans and the shorter span are each at least a specified fraction of the longer span; the absolute boundary separation at the later endpoint is less than at the earlier start; and the current close lies between the projected boundaries, which have not crossed relative to their ordering at the earlier start. Reset both retained boundaries after creating a channel. Project each active boundary forward each bar; upper and lower are the maximum and minimum of the projections. Remove a channel after its first confirmed breakout or when its age from the earlier boundary start exceeds a specified multiple of the updated high-boundary span.
Calculation: Outside count = consecutive bars on which the close is outside an active channel, resetting to zero when the close is inside or on either boundary. Outside bars on opposite sides still count as consecutive.
Calculation: Strong breakout = the close is outside the channel AND the current candle body's z-score relative to the SMA and standard deviation of body lengths is at least a threshold AND either the lower body quartile is above the upper boundary OR the upper body quartile is below the lower boundary. Use zero for the z-score when its standard deviation is zero.
Calculation: Bull probability = for the latest active channel, calculate its duration from its earlier boundary start. Maintain a bounded history of confirmed breakout durations and directions. If each direction has sufficient observations and nonzero sample standard deviation, calculate the standard-normal CDF of the current duration's z-score within each direction and divide the bullish CDF by their sum; otherwise use the historical bullish fraction. When history exists and total channel volume is positive, blend that probability with a parameter-weighted volume probability; with no history, use the volume probability alone. Volume probability is one half plus one half times (volume on bars with close above open minus volume on bars with close below open) divided by total volume since channel start, clamped to probability bounds; use one half when total volume is zero. Clamp the final probability to the same bounds.
Calculation: Breakout levels = on each confirmed breakout, record the close as entry and the channel width as upper minus lower with a positive floor. Append the current duration to the bounded breakout-duration history before calculating the mean. Duration factor is current duration divided by that mean, clamped to lower and upper bounds. For a long or short respectively, targets are entry plus or minus width times duration factor times three increasing target multipliers; add or subtract an additional absolute clamped ATR z-score times ATR to the furthest target. The stop is entry minus or plus width times a stop multiplier. A new breakout replaces the prior levels and resets their hit flags.
Long: An active channel's close > upper boundary AND (outside count >= the confirmation count OR strong breakout).
Short: An active channel's close < lower boundary AND (outside count >= the confirmation count OR strong breakout).
Long: Latest active channel width > 0 AND bull probability > the bullish bias threshold.
Short: Latest active channel width > 0 AND bull probability < the bearish bias threshold.
Long: Latest active channel count > 0 AND bull probability > the neutral probability threshold.
Short: Latest active channel count > 0 AND bull probability <= the neutral probability threshold.
Exit: long, an unhit target's high >= its breakout level AND previous high < that level OR the unhit stop's low <= its breakout level AND previous low > that level.
Exit: short, an unhit target's low <= its breakout level AND previous low > that level OR the unhit stop's high >= its breakout level AND previous high < that level.
