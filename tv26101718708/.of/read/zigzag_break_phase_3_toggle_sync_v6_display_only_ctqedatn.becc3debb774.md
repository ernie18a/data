Calculation: Structural state = On each new chart bar, compare the preceding bar’s close with the high and low of a stored reference bar, initialized to the first preceding bar; initialize direction bullish if that bar closed at or above its open, otherwise bearish. If the close exceeds the reference high or falls below the reference low, replace the reference bar with the preceding bar and set direction bullish or bearish respectively. When this changes direction, confirm a high for a bearish reversal or a low for a bullish reversal: take the most extreme chart-bar high or low between the preceding confirmed vertex and the current bar, excluding both, within a limited lookback; for the first vertex, start after the previous reference bar. Mark the new high or low unbroken. If a confirmed high is no higher than the preceding high after an upward break, or a confirmed low is no lower than the preceding low after a downward break, reset break direction to neutral. An upward break occurs once per confirmed high when the preceding chart-bar close exceeds it; a downward break occurs once per confirmed low when the preceding chart-bar close falls below it. A break sets break direction to its direction.
Calculation: Vertex sequence = An upward break after neutral or downward break direction starts a bullish sequence; a downward break after neutral or upward break direction starts a bearish sequence. Subsequent confirmed vertices alternate between the sequence’s high and low sides, starting with a high in a bullish sequence and a low in a bearish sequence. A new sequence replaces the preceding sequence.
Calculation: MA event = Compare the chart-timeframe close SMA with the same-length close SMA on a higher timeframe using its still-forming bar. The chart SMA is rising if it exceeds its value a lookback earlier. Convergence is chart SMA above higher-timeframe SMA and not rising, or chart SMA at or below higher-timeframe SMA and rising. Divergence is chart SMA above higher-timeframe SMA and rising, or chart SMA at or below higher-timeframe SMA and not rising. A convergence or divergence event occurs when its condition changes from false to true. A crossover or crossunder occurs when the chart SMA moves strictly above or below the higher-timeframe SMA from at or below or at or above it, respectively.
Calculation: Step sequence = Choose three ordered events from crossover, convergence, divergence, or off; the final event may also be a confirmed vertex or a new structural sequence. A non-off first event starts or restarts the sequence. An off later event is immediately satisfied, and multiple steps may complete on the same bar. The first event assigns direction: crossover direction, direction toward the higher-timeframe SMA for convergence, or chart SMA direction for divergence. Later crossovers satisfy their step in either direction without changing the assigned direction. Later convergence and divergence events also preserve direction. A vertex step requires a confirmed low for bullish direction or a confirmed high for bearish direction; a structural-sequence step requires a new sequence in the assigned direction. Completion resets the sequence. The sequence cannot start when its first step is off.
Long: The chart-timeframe close SMA, or a higher- or lower-timeframe close SMA using its still-forming higher-timeframe bar or last available lower-timeframe bar, is greater than its value a lookback earlier.
Short: The chart-timeframe close SMA, or a higher- or lower-timeframe close SMA using its still-forming higher-timeframe bar or last available lower-timeframe bar, is less than or equal to its value a lookback earlier.
Long: An upward structural break occurs.
Short: A downward structural break occurs.
Long: A bullish vertex sequence starts or a subsequent vertex in that sequence is confirmed while break direction is bullish.
Short: A bearish vertex sequence starts or a subsequent vertex in that sequence is confirmed while break direction is bearish.
Long: Chart close exceeds the most recently confirmed, as-yet-unbroken high-side vertex of a bullish sequence.
Short: Chart close falls below the most recently confirmed, as-yet-unbroken low-side vertex of a bearish sequence.
Long: A step sequence reaches any enabled step with bullish direction.
Short: A step sequence reaches any enabled step with bearish direction.
