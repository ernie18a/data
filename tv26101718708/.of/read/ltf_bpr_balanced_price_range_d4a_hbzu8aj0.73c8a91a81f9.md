Calculation: Lower-timeframe gap candidates = Use completed lower-timeframe bars. For each chart bar, first discard stored gaps whose final bar opened more than a lookback duration before the latest lower-timeframe bar's open. Then process three-bar sequences spanning the previous and current chart bars, followed by sequences wholly within the current chart bar; process each group in chronological order. Let bars 2, 1, and 0 be the oldest through newest bars in a sequence. A bullish candidate requires high[1] > high[2], low[0] > high[2], and low[0] - high[2] > a multiplier times the lower-timeframe ATR calculated on the last completed lower-timeframe bar of the current chart bar; use zero if ATR is unavailable. Its upper boundary is low[0] and lower boundary is high[2]. A bearish candidate requires low[1] < low[2], high[0] < low[2], and low[2] - high[0] > the same ATR threshold. Its upper boundary is low[2] and lower boundary is high[0]. If volume-imbalance inclusion is enabled, replace the bullish upper boundary with open[0] when close[0] > open[0] > close[1], or with close[0] when close[0] < open[0] and close[0] > close[1]; replace its lower boundary with close[2] when open[2] < close[2] < open[1]. Replace the bearish upper boundary with close[2] when open[2] > close[2] > open[1]; replace its lower boundary with open[0] when open[0] > close[0] and open[0] < close[1], or with close[0] when open[0] < close[0] < close[1]. Store a candidate only when its upper boundary exceeds its lower boundary.
Calculation: Balanced price range formation = On storing a candidate, search stored opposite-direction gaps in reverse storage order. Stop when the candidate's final-bar open is more than the lookback duration after the stored gap's final-bar open. The first gap with min(candidate upper boundary, stored upper boundary) > max(candidate lower boundary, stored lower boundary) forms a range in the candidate's direction.
Long: A bullish balanced price range forms.
Short: A bearish balanced price range forms.
