Calculation: Rails = the highest high and lowest low over a lookback ending on the previous bar; the rails are valid when the upper rail exceeds the lower rail.
Calculation: Trap candidate = an upper candidate when rails are valid AND high exceeds the upper rail by more than a minimum ATR multiple AND close is below the upper rail AND above the rails’ midpoint; a lower candidate when rails are valid AND low is below the lower rail by more than the same ATR multiple AND close is above the lower rail AND below the midpoint. If both occur, select the candidate with the larger probe depth in ATR units, selecting the upper candidate on a tie.
Calculation: Active trap = when no trap is active, store the selected candidate’s side, bar, rail as its edge, probe depth in ATR units, and distance between the edge and probe extreme as its zone height. Maintain at most one trap; expire it when its age exceeds the failure window, allowing a new candidate on that bar. Clear it after a qualified return. The trap bar has age zero.
Calculation: Return = for an upper trap, close exceeds the stored edge plus the current ATR times a buffer; for a lower trap, close is below the stored edge minus the current ATR times the same buffer. Count consecutive return closes, resetting the count to zero whenever return is false.
Calculation: Failure score = a weighted sum, clamped to its score range, of probe quality, recovery, follow-through, relative volume, acceptance, and persistence. Normalize each component to the same range: probe quality rises linearly from zero at the minimum probe depth to its maximum at an ideal depth, then falls linearly to zero at a larger depth; recovery and follow-through rise linearly from zero to maximum between their respective lower and upper thresholds using signed distance beyond the edge divided by stored zone height and current ATR, respectively; relative volume uses current volume divided by its moving average; persistence uses the consecutive return count divided by the required count. Clamp each normalized component. Acceptance is zero without a return; otherwise it is a clamped weighted sum of persistence, follow-through, and close location, where close location is (close - low) / (high - low) for an upper trap and its complement for a lower trap. Use positive minimum denominators for zero ranges and ATR, and use neutral relative volume when its average is unavailable or nonpositive.
Long: A new lower trap candidate is selected when no trap is active.
Short: A new upper trap candidate is selected when no trap is active.
Long: An upper trap is active AND return is true AND the consecutive return count is below its required count OR an upper trap is active AND return is true AND the failure score is below its required threshold.
Short: A lower trap is active AND return is true AND the consecutive return count is below its required count OR a lower trap is active AND return is true AND the failure score is below its required threshold.
Long: An upper trap is active AND return is true AND the consecutive return count meets or exceeds its required count AND the failure score meets or exceeds its required threshold.
Short: A lower trap is active AND return is true AND the consecutive return count meets or exceeds its required count AND the failure score meets or exceeds its required threshold.
