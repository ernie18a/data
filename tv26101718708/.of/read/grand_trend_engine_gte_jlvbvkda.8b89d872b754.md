Calculation: Rail state = On each completed bar, let core be the linear-regression endpoint of (high + low) / 2, and let offset be ATR multiplied by a configurable factor. Initialize direction to bullish when close >= core, otherwise bearish; initialize rail to core minus offset when bullish, otherwise core plus offset. On subsequent bars, if direction was bearish and close > previous rail, change direction to bullish and set rail to core minus offset; if direction was bullish and close < previous rail, change direction to bearish and set rail to core plus offset. Otherwise retain direction and set rail to the greater of previous rail and core minus offset when bullish, or the lesser of previous rail and core plus offset when bearish.
Calculation: Timeframe votes = Compute the rail state independently on each of four configured timeframes. For a higher timeframe use its last completed bar; for a lower timeframe use its last completed intrabar within the chart bar. Count bullish and bearish states separately. The majority direction is the direction with more votes, breaking a tie using the chart-timeframe rail direction.
Calculation: Confirmed swing = A high or low pivot with equal configurable numbers of bars on each side, available only after the right-hand bars have completed. Use the latest confirmed swing high or low as applicable.
Calculation: Bullish order block = The interval from the previous low to previous open on the latest bar where previous close < previous open AND close > previous high AND absolute close minus open > ATR multiplied by a configurable factor. Bearish order block = The interval from previous open to previous high on the latest bar where previous close > previous open AND close < previous low AND absolute close minus open > ATR multiplied by the same factor. Retain each interval until replaced by a newer interval of its direction.
Calculation: Pivot sequence = Maintain confirmed swing highs and lows in chronological order. Append a pivot when its type differs from the last pivot; when its type matches, replace the last pivot only if the new high is higher or the new low is lower. If both types are confirmed on the same bar, process the high first. Maintain separate sequences for ordinary highs and lows and for Heikin-Ashi highs and lows.
Calculation: Formation geometry = Over a configurable lookback, regress Heikin-Ashi highs and lows separately and take each regression's first and last fitted values. Upper and lower slopes are last minus first; left and right widths are upper minus lower fitted values; compression is 100 times (1 minus right width divided by left width) when left width > 0, otherwise zero. A symmetric triangle requires sufficient history, upper slope < 0, lower slope > 0, and compression > its threshold. An ascending triangle requires sufficient history, absolute upper slope < ATR times a flatness factor, lower slope > 0, and compression > its threshold. A descending triangle reverses the slopes. An upward or downward impulse requires the corresponding percentage change in Heikin-Ashi close over the formation lookback to exceed an impulse threshold, with the earlier close > 0. A bullish or bearish flag requires sufficient history, the corresponding impulse, absolute values of both slopes < ATR times a flag factor, and compression < a flag threshold. A bullish or bearish pennant requires the corresponding impulse and a symmetric triangle. Sufficient history means the chart bar index exceeds the formation lookback plus a configurable warm-up.
Calculation: Other classic patterns = A double top requires sufficient history, absolute current Heikin-Ashi high minus its lookback maximum < ATR times a tolerance, and Heikin-Ashi close < its SMA; a double bottom uses the lookback minimum of Heikin-Ashi low and close > its SMA. A W requires the latest five Heikin-Ashi pivots to be low, high, low, high, low, the absolute difference of the first and third prices < ATR times a tolerance, and the fourth price > the second times a near-equality factor below 1; an M reverses pivot types, uses the same pivot tolerance, and requires the fourth price < the second times a near-equality factor above 1. An inverse head-and-shoulders requires sufficient history, the lookback minimum Heikin-Ashi low to be below each of two earlier Heikin-Ashi lows sampled at longer and shorter fractions of the lookback by more than ATR, and the absolute difference between those sampled lows < ATR times a shoulder tolerance. A head-and-shoulders reverses lows and inequalities using the lookback maximum high and sampled highs.
Calculation: Formation context = Heikin-Ashi close is (open + high + low + close) / 4; Heikin-Ashi open starts at (open + close) / 2 and thereafter equals the mean of the preceding Heikin-Ashi open and close; Heikin-Ashi high and low are the maximum and minimum of high, low, Heikin-Ashi open and close as applicable. Context score for each direction is a capped, scaled positive directional Aroon difference plus a tiered score for volume divided by its SMA. Fibonacci score uses the latest confirmed Heikin-Ashi swing high and low: assign the highest score when Heikin-Ashi close is in the middle retracement zone of a swing in that direction, a lower score in its deeper retracement zone, and a base score otherwise.
Calculation: Selected classic formation = Select the first matching pattern in this order: ascending triangle, descending triangle, symmetric triangle, either pennant, either flag, W or double bottom, M or double top, inverse head-and-shoulders, head-and-shoulders. If both directions of a paired pattern match, select bullish. Its direction is bullish, bearish, or mixed according to the selected pattern. Its quality is capped at a maximum and equals shape score plus the selected direction's Fibonacci and context scores; for a symmetric triangle use the larger Fibonacci score and the larger context score. Triangle shape score is a capped nonnegative sum of absolute compression and scaled absolute upper and lower slopes divided by ATR; other shapes use a fixed base score. A bullish classic qualifier means any bullish classic pattern matches AND the selected formation's quality meets its threshold; a bearish qualifier is defined analogously, even if the selected formation has another direction.
Calculation: Harmonic formation = Use the latest five ordinary-price pivots X, A, B, C, D. Direction is bullish for low-high-low-high-low and bearish for high-low-high-low-high. Let XA, AB, BC, CD and XC be absolute price differences between the named pivots. Test, in priority order, Gartley, Bat, Butterfly, Crab, Cypher, and ABCD using configurable inclusive ratio bands: Gartley requires AB/XA near its target, BC/AB in its band and |D-X|/XA near its target; Bat requires AB/XA and BC/AB in their bands and |D-X|/XA near its target; Butterfly requires AB/XA near its target and |D-X|/XA in its band; Crab requires AB/XA in its band and |D-X|/XA near its target; Cypher requires AB/XA and |C-X|/XA in their bands and |D-X|/XC near its target; ABCD requires BC/AB and CD/AB in their bands. A near test uses an absolute tolerance. Ratios with zero denominators are unavailable. The first matching pattern supplies its fixed base score; harmonic quality is that score plus the directional formation context score, capped at a maximum. A bullish or bearish harmonic qualifier requires a matching pattern in that direction and quality >= its threshold.
Calculation: Formation synchronization = A direction is synchronized when both its classic qualifier and its harmonic qualifier have occurred within a configurable number of chart bars, counting the current bar. The displayed formation direction follows this priority: bullish synchronization alone, bearish synchronization alone, no direction if both synchronize, bullish classic qualifier alone, bearish classic qualifier alone, bullish harmonic qualifier alone, bearish harmonic qualifier alone, otherwise no direction.
