Calculation: Source bars = aggregate the input bars to each configured source timeframe. A source timeframe may be higher, lower, or equal to the chart timeframe; use its last completed bar, with the source event published one source bar after its confirmation. For a lower source timeframe, use the last available source result in each chart bar. Evaluate each source layer independently.
Calculation: Directional notation = use sign d = +1 for demand and -1 for supply. For a price interval, proximal is its upper boundary when d = +1 and its lower boundary when d = -1; distal is the opposite boundary. A directional close above a level means d × close > d × level. A directional break beyond a level with buffer means d × close > d × level + buffer.
Calculation: Base = the configured number of consecutive source bars immediately preceding a departure. Its upper and lower boundaries are their highest high and lowest low. Average body ratio is the mean of abs(close - open) / max(high - low, minimum price increment). Average overlap is the mean, over adjacent base bars, of their shared price range divided by max(the smaller bar range, minimum price increment). Close drift is the absolute difference between the last and first base closes. A compressed base has average body ratio at most its mode-adjusted limit AND base range at most its mode-adjusted ATR multiple, using ATR from the last base bar. For the standard layer, a loose base requires compression; a balanced or custom base also requires overlap at least its minimum OR drift at most its ATR limit; a strict base requires both. For the session layer, every base bar must be in the configured weekday session, and a strict base additionally requires minimum overlap.
Calculation: Zone boundaries = select the most recent base candle opposite to d, or the most recent base candle if none exists. Depending on the configured boundary method, use the complete base range, the selected candle's full range, its body, or its distal wick and body through its proximal edge.
Calculation: Departure candidate = a valid base AND a source bar whose candle direction equals d, whose body is at least the mode-adjusted departure ATR multiple, whose body/range is at least the mode-adjusted directional minimum, and whose close breaks beyond the base's proximal extreme by the configured ATR buffer. Apply the configured zone-width limit if enabled. In the session layer, the departure must also occur in the configured weekday session, close directionally beyond the zone's proximal boundary, and satisfy the mandatory zone-width limit. ATR and the buffer are measured on the departure bar. For loose mode the body, range, departure, and directional limits are relaxed; for strict mode they are tightened according to their configured limits.
Calculation: Structure level = for a standard candidate, choose the latest confirmed pivot in direction d, the highest high or lowest low respectively over the configured rolling lookback ending immediately before the base, or the more extreme of those two; if the pivot is unavailable, use the rolling level. For a session candidate, use the latest confirmed pivot in direction d, with equal left and right strengths; a missing pivot cannot confirm. Freeze the level and candidate attributes at the latest candidate of that direction.
Calculation: First structure break = the latest candidate is no older than the configured confirmation window AND the current source close breaks directionally beyond its frozen structure level by the current ATR buffer AND either the candidate is on the current bar OR the preceding source bar did not satisfy this break condition. For session candidates, the permitted window is the larger of the confirmation and post-session windows when post-session confirmation is enabled, otherwise the confirmation window; confirmation must occur in session OR, when enabled, within the post-session bar limit measured from the candidate.
Calculation: Qualification features = at the candidate, retracement is the distance from the pre-base lookback's highest high to the base low for demand, or from the pre-base lookback's lowest low to the base high for supply, at least its configured price or ATR minimum. A sweep is a base low below the latest confirmed shorter-strength pivot low followed by the last base close above it for demand, or a base high above the latest confirmed shorter-strength pivot high followed by the last base close below it for supply. A bullish imbalance is current low > high two bars earlier; a bearish imbalance is current high < low two bars earlier. Standard qualification accepts an imbalance occurring from the candidate through confirmation; session qualification uses only the candidate bar. Resting liquidity is the pre-base rolling high > close for demand or rolling low < close for supply; standard qualification evaluates it on confirmation and session qualification freezes it at the candidate. Strong departure means candidate body is at least its configured strong-departure ATR threshold.
Calculation: Qualification score = add configured points for a first structure break, valid base, retracement, departure strength, sweep, imbalance, and resting liquidity. Base points have a higher value when both overlap and drift pass for the standard layer, or when overlap passes for the session layer; otherwise use the lower base value. Departure points use the strong or ordinary value. Omit sweep points in the session layer and cap its score at the configured maximum. Strict standard qualification additionally requires retracement AND (sweep OR imbalance); strict session qualification requires retracement AND imbalance. Custom qualification applies each enabled retracement, imbalance, and, for the standard layer, sweep requirement. Loose and balanced modes have no additional mandatory features. Compare the score with the threshold for the selected mode.
Calculation: Confirmed breaker = on a source bar, detect the first close beyond the latest confirmed pivot high plus an ATR buffer, or below the latest confirmed pivot low minus that buffer. On an upward break, store the most recent bearish candle within the configured search window as a demand block; on a downward break, store the most recent bullish candle as a supply block. Use its full range or body as configured, optionally expanding it to include the immediately preceding candle when that candle has the same direction. Replace the previous block of the same type. A block expires after its configured maximum age. A bullish breaker occurs when a later source close exceeds the live supply block's upper boundary plus the current ATR buffer; a bearish breaker occurs when a later source close falls below the live demand block's lower boundary minus that buffer. Consume the block on the break. For the session layer, structural breaks and the selected opposing candle must be in session, blocks reset on a new session-local calendar day, and a breaker must occur in session OR, if post-session confirmation is enabled, within the configured number of bars after block creation.
Long: A standard-layer demand candidate has a first structure break AND its qualification score meets the selected threshold AND its selected mode's mandatory features pass AND its origin is within the configured history window.
Short: A standard-layer supply candidate has a first structure break AND its qualification score meets the selected threshold AND its selected mode's mandatory features pass AND its origin is within the configured history window.
Long: A session-layer demand candidate has a first structure break AND its qualification score meets the selected threshold AND its selected mode's mandatory features pass AND confirmation-bar low >= its distal boundary AND chart close > its proximal boundary AND its origin is within the configured retention window.
Short: A session-layer supply candidate has a first structure break AND its qualification score meets the selected threshold AND its selected mode's mandatory features pass AND confirmation-bar high <= its distal boundary AND chart close < its proximal boundary AND its origin is within the configured retention window.
Long: A standard-layer bullish breaker is confirmed AND its origin is within the configured history window.
Short: A standard-layer bearish breaker is confirmed AND its origin is within the configured history window.
Long: A session-layer bullish breaker is confirmed AND its block width is at most the configured maximum AND chart close > its upper boundary AND its origin is within the configured retention window.
Short: A session-layer bearish breaker is confirmed AND its block width is at most the configured maximum AND chart close < its lower boundary AND its origin is within the configured retention window.
