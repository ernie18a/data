Calculation: Liquidity pools = Maintain separate upper and lower pools. On confirmation of a pivot high or low, create a member at the pivot price if its enabled sources include ordinary swings, an equal swing, a major swing, or a repeated rejection. An equal swing has a remembered same-side pivot within an ATR-scaled price tolerance and at least the pivot-right confirmation length away. A repeated rejection has a remembered same-side pivot within that tolerance and a separate minimum bar distance, and the pivot candle's corresponding wick divided by its range meets a minimum ratio. A major swing has pivot-to-opposite-extreme distance over the pivot window divided by current ATR at or above a threshold. Remember a bounded number of confirmed pivots per side. At the start of each new period, add the preceding completed high and low from each enabled reference timeframe that is higher than or equal to the chart timeframe; the longer reference timeframe receives greater member weight than the shorter one. Pivot members receive greater weight when classified as major. Merge each member into the nearest same-side forming, active, or approaching pool whose centroid is within an ATR-scaled clustering distance; otherwise create a pool. Maintain weighted-average centroid and formation ATR, minimum and maximum member prices, member count, source set, earliest origin and confirmation bars, maximum normalized swing prominence, average normalized pivot relative volume, and independently separated pivot-touch count. A single-member zone is centered on its centroid with ATR-scaled half-width; a multi-member zone extends from its minimum to maximum member price plus ATR-scaled padding, capped at an ATR-scaled maximum width around its midpoint. When storage exceeds its limit, remove the pool with the earliest confirmation bar.
Calculation: Liquidity strength = A weighted sum of saturating exponential functions of member count and independent touch count, maximum pivot prominence normalized by ATR and capped to a unit interval, source-timeframe importance, and average pivot volume divided by its volume moving average and capped to a unit interval. The longer completed reference timeframe has greater importance than the shorter, and either has greater importance than a swing-only pool.
Calculation: Pool lifecycle = On each confirmed chart bar, update every pool with positive ATR. For a forming, active, or approaching upper pool, a sweep occurs when high is at or above its upper zone boundary plus an ATR-scaled buffer; for a lower pool, when low is at or below its lower boundary minus the buffer. Otherwise the pool is forming below the liquidity-strength threshold, approaching when its distance from close to the zone divided by ATR is at or below the proximity threshold, and active otherwise. On a sweep, initialize the consecutive beyond-boundary close count to one if close is strictly beyond the outer boundary, otherwise zero; accept immediately if that count reaches the required count, otherwise mark sweeping. On subsequent bars of a sweeping pool, increment that count for a close strictly beyond the outer boundary and reset it otherwise; accept when it reaches the required count. Otherwise reject if, within the rejection window, close is strictly beyond the opposite boundary on the original side; retire it when the window expires. Retire accepted and rejected pools after the resolved-state holding period, then remove retired pools after their retirement bar. Remove pools whose age since confirmation exceeds the maximum age.
Calculation: Pool gravity = Clamp liquidity strength multiplied by distance, freshness, trend, and regime modifiers to the score range. Distance is zero inside the zone and otherwise the distance from close to its nearest boundary divided by ATR; its modifier is an affine function of inverse quadratic accessibility using an ATR-distance scale. Freshness decays exponentially with bars since confirmation using a source-dependent half-life increased by liquidity strength; use the longer reference timeframe's half-life before the shorter one's, then the clustered-structure half-life, then the isolated-swing half-life. Trend bias is the difference between shorter and longer close EMAs divided by ATR and a scale, capped to a signed unit interval; its directional modifier uses the pool side and a bounded chart-timeframe-dependent influence. Classify regime from ADX and ATR relative to its moving average as trend expansion, trend, compression, or range. Its bounded modifier favors trend-aligned pools in trend regimes, equal-swing or repeated-rejection pools in range, and discounts pools in compression.
Calculation: Ranked pull = For each side, select active or approaching pools within the maximum ATR-normalized zone distance whose outer boundary remains on that side of close. Rank them by descending pool gravity, breaking ties by pool storage order. Sum the highest-ranked scores using successively smaller rank weights; a missing rank contributes zero.
Calculation: Net gravity = The difference between upper and lower ranked pull divided by their sum, scaled to the score range; use zero when their sum is zero.
Long: An eligible upper pool has a rank within the displayed rank limit.
Short: An eligible lower pool has a rank within the displayed rank limit.
Long: Net gravity is at or above zero AND an eligible primary upper pool exists.
Short: Net gravity is below zero AND an eligible primary lower pool exists.
Long: Net gravity is above a positive directional threshold.
Short: Net gravity is below the negative directional threshold.
Long: Net gravity crosses above zero OR crosses above the positive strong-gravity threshold.
Short: Net gravity crosses below zero OR crosses below the negative strong-gravity threshold.
Long: An upper-pool sweep occurs OR an upper pool is accepted OR a lower pool is rejected.
Short: A lower-pool sweep occurs OR a lower pool is accepted OR an upper pool is rejected.
