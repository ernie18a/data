Calculation: Confirmed flag = On each closed bar with no active flag, examine candidate pole tops from the most recent to the oldest within the permitted flag-length range and select the first that satisfies all conditions. The pole low is the earliest lowest low in the maximum pole-length lookback before the top; it is positive, and its distance from the top is at least the minimum pole length. Every high from the pole low through the bar before the top is below the top high. The top-to-low gain is at least the minimum pole gain. Every high after the top through the current bar is at most the top high, and the lowest low of those bars is within the maximum flag depth below the top. If positive volume has appeared in the data so far, average pole volume, including both endpoints, must be positive; the maximum pole volume must be at least a threshold multiple of the average volume over the shorter baseline lookback immediately before the pole low when that full lookback exists and its average is positive; and average flag volume must be at most a threshold multiple of average pole volume. Skip all volume conditions until positive volume has appeared. A confirmed flag remains active until it breaks out, is invalidated, or expires. On each subsequent closed bar, invalidate it if the low is below the depth limit; otherwise mark it broken out if the high exceeds the pole top; otherwise expire it if its age from the pole top exceeds the maximum flag length. If both price limits are crossed on the same bar, count only the limit closer to the open, choosing the upper limit on a tie. Check breakout and invalidation before expiration. After an active flag ends, search for a new flag on the same bar.
Long: An active confirmed flag breaks out.
