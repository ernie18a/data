Calculation: Structural state = Process chart bars in order. Initialize the reference high and low, continuation level, reversal level and location to the first processed bar's high, low, high, low and index; initialize direction to neutral. On each bar, set the upward expansion flag if high exceeds the reference high, then replace both references with this bar's high and low. Next set the downward expansion flag if low is below the resulting reference low, again replacing both references. Confirm pivot highs and lows with equal left and right lookbacks; retain the latest confirmed pivot until price exceeds it. Maintain the current directional extreme and its latest bar index. When sweeping is enabled, a low at or below a structural level with close at or above it replaces that level with low; a high at or above a structural level with close at or below it replaces that level with high. A sweep takes precedence over a break of the same level.
Calculation: Structural transitions = In neutral state, first test a sweep of the reversal low, then a sweep of the continuation high; otherwise close at or below the reversal low changes direction to down, while close at or above the continuation high changes it to up. After either break, clear the continuation level, set the directional extreme to the current low or high, and set the location to the current bar; for a down break set the reversal level to the old continuation high, and for an up break retain the reversal low. In a down state, update the extreme on low <= extreme. If the continuation level is absent, set it to the extreme and its location when upward expansion occurs and both current and previous candles close above their opens. In an up state, use the symmetric high extreme, downward expansion, and two candles closing below their opens. In adjusted-point mode, on bars whose index is divisible by the pivot lookback, while a continuation level exists, replace the down-state reversal level with the latest unbroken pivot high if lower, or the up-state reversal level with the latest unbroken pivot low if higher; also set the location and extreme index to that pivot's index. In a down state, process a continuation-low sweep or close <= continuation level first; on a break, clear that level and set the reversal level and location to the highest high and its index from the bar after the old location through the current bar, including the old location if it is adjacent. Then process a reversal-high sweep or close >= reversal level; on a break, change direction to up, set the reversal level to the continuation level if present, otherwise to the lowest low over the same location-to-current range, clear the continuation level, and reset the extreme and location to the current high and bar. In an up state apply the symmetric rules: continuation-high break sets the reversal level to the range's lowest low; reversal-low break changes direction to down and uses the continuation level if present, otherwise the range's highest high. When equal extremes occur in a range, select the oldest bar. Process the continuation and reversal tests sequentially on the same bar. Record the latest transition type as continuation or reversal and retain it until another transition.
Calculation: Order blocks = At each structural break, select the lowest-low bar for a bullish block or highest-high bar for a bearish block over the structural location-to-current range, with the same endpoint and tie rules as structural transitions; if the immediately preceding bar has a more extreme low or high, select it instead. A bullish block spans the selected low to either the selected high or min(selected high, selected low + scaled ATR); a bearish block spans either the selected low or max(selected low, selected high - scaled ATR) to the selected high. The scaled ATR is ATR multiplied by a configurable scale. Create a bullish block on an upward continuation, upward reversal, or initial downward break; create a bearish block on a downward continuation, downward reversal, or initial upward break. Reset that side's interaction flag on creation. Track blocks newest first. On confirmed bars, a bullish block becomes a breaker when its lower boundary is breached by min(open, close), low, or low against its midpoint, according to the selected close, wick, or average mitigation method; a bearish block uses max(open, close), high, or high against its midpoint and its upper boundary. Remove mitigated blocks unless breakers are enabled. Remove bullish breakers when the corresponding upper boundary or midpoint is exceeded, and bearish breakers when the corresponding lower boundary or midpoint is breached. If overlap filtering is enabled, remove overlapping blocks according to the selected preference for recent or old blocks, including comparisons across directions. These mitigation and overlap updates apply only when order-block display and a positive display count are enabled. Test interaction before those updates: the newest non-breaker bullish block sets its interaction flag when low < its upper boundary, and the newest non-breaker bearish block sets its flag when high > its lower boundary. The flag persists until another block of that side is created.
Calculation: Fair value gaps = A bullish gap is formed when low > high two bars earlier AND the preceding close > preceding low + preceding ATR times a configurable threshold; its upper boundary is current low and lower boundary is high two bars earlier. A bearish gap is formed when low two bars earlier > high AND the preceding close < preceding high - preceding ATR times the threshold; its upper boundary is low two bars earlier and lower boundary is current high. Add the gap on the next bar. Track gaps newest first. Mitigate, convert to breakers, and remove them with the same close, wick, or average boundary tests as order blocks; remove mitigated gaps in gap mode, or retain them as breakers until the opposite boundary is breached in breaker mode. If overlap filtering is enabled, remove older overlapping same-side gaps and overlapping opposite-side gaps according to the source's newest-first comparisons.
Long: Direction = up AND latest transition type = reversal AND bar is confirmed.
Short: Direction = down AND latest transition type = reversal AND bar is confirmed.
Long: An upward structural continuation break occurs.
Short: A downward structural continuation break occurs.
Long: A structural low is swept.
Short: A structural high is swept.
Long: The bullish order-block interaction flag changes from false to true.
Short: The bearish order-block interaction flag changes from false to true.
Long: A bullish fair value gap is formed.
Short: A bearish fair value gap is formed.
Long: A non-breaker bullish fair value gap has not previously been raided AND low < its upper boundary AND close > its upper boundary.
Short: A non-breaker bearish fair value gap has not previously been raided AND high > its lower boundary AND close < its lower boundary.
