Calculation: Effective lookbacks = optionally scale each designated lookback inversely with chart-bar duration relative to a reference duration, round, and apply its minimum.
Calculation: KAMA slope = (KAMA of close minus its value at the slope lookback) / close.
Calculation: Residual z-score = z-score of close minus KAMA, using a rolling residual mean and standard deviation.
Calculation: Wavelet slope = (close smoothed by repeated short SMAs minus that smoothed value at the slope lookback) / close; each smoothing level applies the same SMA to the preceding level.
Calculation: Kalman slope = (scalar Kalman-filtered close minus its value at the slope lookback) / close. Initialize the state to close and its error variance to an initial value; on each confirmed bar add process noise to error variance, calculate gain = predicted variance / (predicted variance + measurement noise), update state by gain × (close − state), and update variance by (1 − gain) × predicted variance.
Calculation: Hamilton state probabilities = a three-state Gaussian hidden Markov filter on log close returns. Estimate rolling return mean and standard deviation; set bull and bear observation means to the mean plus and minus a standard-deviation multiple, bounded respectively above zero and below zero, and their shared standard deviation to a low-volatility multiple of return standard deviation. Set the range mean to zero and its standard deviation to a larger multiple. Initialize state probabilities approximately equally; on each confirmed bar apply a transition matrix with equal diagonal persistence and equal off-diagonal probabilities, multiply each resulting prior by its Gaussian return likelihood, and normalize. Hold the previous probabilities if the likelihood sum is zero. Disable updates below a chart-timeframe cutoff.
Calculation: Model probabilities = map a direction d in {−1, 0, 1} and strength s in [0, 1] to 0.5 + d × probability-amplitude × s. For the KAMA model, d is the sign of KAMA slope and s is its absolute value times a scale, capped at 1. For the mean-reversion model, d is positive below the negative z-score threshold, negative above the positive threshold, and otherwise zero; s is absolute residual z-score divided by a scale, capped at 1. For the wavelet and Kalman models, d is positive above their positive slope threshold, negative below its negative, and otherwise zero; s is absolute slope times a scale, capped at 1. A disabled wavelet or Kalman model has probability 0.5. The Hamilton probability is 0.5 plus probability-amplitude × (bull probability − bear probability), bounded to a probability range; it is 0.5 when Hamilton is inactive.
Calculation: Ensemble probability = on confirmed bars, begin with equal weights for the five models. After a prediction evaluation horizon, score each stored model probability against whether current close exceeds prediction-bar close using binary log loss; multiply its weight by exp(−learning rate × loss), apply a minimum weight, then normalize all weights. Take the weighted average of current model probabilities, or 0.5 when the ensemble is disabled. Optionally damp its distance from 0.5 when Hawkes intensity divided by its rolling event-rate baseline exceeds a warning threshold: an event is an absolute log return above a multiple of rolling return standard deviation; intensity recursively decays exponentially and receives an excitation increment on each event. The damping fraction is the excess intensity ratio divided by the warning threshold, capped at a maximum. Optionally shrink the remaining distance from 0.5 using historical calibration: bin predictions by absolute distance from 0.5, exclude a central neutral band, and after a separate evaluation horizon count whether the predicted direction matched close above prediction-bar close. For a sufficiently populated bin whose hit rate is below its probability midpoint, multiply the distance from 0.5 by the hit rate divided by that midpoint, subject to a minimum factor. Compute the current shrinkage before updating matured calibration counts.
Calculation: Return t-statistic = rolling mean of log close returns divided by their rolling standard deviation divided by the square root of the lookback; use zero when the standard deviation is zero. Realized volatility is the rolling standard deviation of log returns; its low-volatility boundary is a lower rolling percentile over a longer lookback.
Long: KAMA slope > 0 OR residual z-score < negative mean-reversion threshold OR enabled wavelet slope > positive wavelet threshold OR enabled Kalman slope > positive Kalman threshold OR active Hamilton bull probability > bear probability AND bull probability > range probability OR realized volatility < low-volatility boundary AND return t-statistic > positive regime threshold OR ensemble probability > upper probability-bar threshold OR ensemble probability > upper neutral boundary AND ensemble probability >= bullish verdict threshold.
Short: KAMA slope < 0 OR residual z-score > positive mean-reversion threshold OR enabled wavelet slope < negative wavelet threshold OR enabled Kalman slope < negative Kalman threshold OR active Hamilton bear probability > range probability AND NOT (bull probability > bear probability AND bull probability > range probability) OR realized volatility < low-volatility boundary AND return t-statistic < negative regime threshold OR ensemble probability < lower probability-bar threshold OR ensemble probability < lower neutral boundary AND ensemble probability <= bearish verdict threshold.
