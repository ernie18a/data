Calculation: Conventions = Evaluate on each completed chart bar. Use independent configurable parameters for numerical thresholds, lookbacks, weights and switches; preserve the stated ordering of related parameters. A crossing occurs when the current strict inequality holds and the previous bar had the opposite or equal inequality. Direction d is +1 for long and -1 for short. A directional price comparison d*a > d*b means a > b for long and a < b for short. News and manual political risk are unavailable from the specified data, so treat them as disabled.
Calculation: Trend state = On each selected higher or lower timeframe, compare that timeframe's fast EMA of close with its slow EMA and close with its fast EMA: bullish if fast > slow AND close > fast; bearish if fast < slow AND close < fast; otherwise neutral. Use either the last completed timeframe bar or the bar still forming, according to a switch; for a lower timeframe use the last available constituent bar. Count bullish and bearish states separately. Use a shorter fast EMA lookback than the slow EMA lookback, including on the chart timeframe.
Calculation: Momentum = Linear-regression endpoint of close minus the average of (the midpoint of the lookback's highest high and lowest low) and the SMA of close, over the same lookback. Its signal is a shorter EMA of momentum. Rising or falling means greater or less than its previous value. Directional momentum means momentum is above its signal and rising for long, or below its signal and falling for short.
Calculation: Squeeze = The Bollinger bands around the SMA of close are both inside the corresponding ATR-based channel around that same SMA. Recent squeeze means squeeze occurred within a lookback, including the current bar. Squeeze release means the previous bar was in squeeze and the current bar is not. Directional squeeze means recent squeeze OR squeeze release, AND directional momentum.
Calculation: Directional flux = EMA of the change in close divided by ATR, or zero when ATR is not positive. Its signal is a shorter EMA of flux. Bullish flux requires flux > signal AND flux > zero; bearish flux requires flux < signal AND flux < zero.
Calculation: Swings and structure = Confirm pivot highs and lows using equal numbers of bars on both sides; retain the latest confirmed pivot and its bar index for each side. An upward structure break occurs when close > the latest pivot high and that pivot has not already been broken; a downward break occurs when close < the latest pivot low and that pivot has not already been broken. If both occur, choose the upward break when the prior structure bias is neutral or bearish, otherwise choose the downward break. A break against neutral or opposite prior bias is a change of character; a break in the existing bias is a continuation break. Set bias to the break direction and remember the broken pivot index.
Calculation: Volume states = Strong volume means volume > SMA(volume) times a strong-volume multiplier; soft volume means volume > the same SMA times a smaller multiplier.
Calculation: Liquidity sweep = A downside sweep occurs when low < latest pivot low minus an ATR buffer AND open > that pivot AND close > that pivot, OR low < the lowest low of a preceding-bar lookback AND close > that low AND soft volume. An upside sweep reverses these comparisons using the latest pivot high and preceding-bar highest high.
Calculation: Breakout and retest = The range high and low are the highest high and lowest low over a lookback excluding the current bar. An upward breakout requires close > range high AND soft volume AND (recent squeeze OR previous close <= current range high); reverse comparisons for a downward breakout. Each breakout replaces the stored same-direction breakout bar and level. A long retest requires a later bar within the allowed retest lookback with low <= the stored upward breakout level AND close > it; reverse using high and close for a short retest. Clear the pending retest after its first occurrence or expiry.
Calculation: Order blocks = On an upward structure break, store the high and low of the latest bearish candle, including the current bar if bearish, as the bullish block; on a downward break store the latest bullish candle's high and low as the bearish block. A bullish block retest requires an active bullish block AND low <= its high AND close >= its low; a bearish block retest requires an active bearish block AND high >= its low AND close <= its high. Deactivate the bullish block after evaluating the retest if close < its low, and the bearish block if close > its high. A new same-direction structure break replaces its block.
Calculation: Fibonacci range and confluence = On an upward structure break, set direction bullish, the range high to current high and range low to the latest pivot low; subsequently raise the range high whenever high exceeds it. On a downward break, set direction bearish, range low to current low and range high to the latest pivot high; subsequently lower the range low whenever low falls below it. The range is valid only when both endpoints exist and high > low. For a bullish range calculate retracement levels as range high minus range size times each retracement fraction; for a bearish range calculate range low plus range size times each fraction. The entry zone lies between a smaller and a larger retracement fraction and is touched when bar high >= its lower boundary AND bar low <= its upper boundary. Bullish confluence requires the Fibonacci filter enabled AND bullish range AND zone touched AND (downside sweep OR bullish block retest OR rising momentum OR close > the midpoint retracement). Bearish confluence reverses direction, using upside sweep, bearish block retest, falling momentum and close < midpoint retracement.
Calculation: Candle and divergence = A bullish pin bar has a lower wick greater than a body-size multiplier AND close > open; a bearish pin bar uses the upper wick and close < open. A bullish engulfing candle has close > open AND previous close < previous open AND close >= previous open AND open <= previous close; reverse all inequalities for bearish engulfing. A directional candle is its pin bar OR engulfing candle. Bullish divergence occurs on confirmation of a pivot low lower than the preceding confirmed pivot low while RSI at the new pivot is higher than RSI at the preceding pivot; bearish divergence uses a higher pivot high and lower RSI.
Calculation: Trend quality = Clamp to the percentage range the average of four components: absolute close change over a lookback divided by the sum of absolute one-bar close changes over that lookback, capped at the percentage maximum and zero if the denominator is zero; ADX divided by a scale parameter and capped at the percentage maximum; the greater of the percentages of bars with rising and falling momentum over that lookback; and the percentage maximum minus a multiplier times the absolute deviation of ATR divided by its longer SMA from one, floored at zero. Use a neutral ATR ratio when its SMA is not positive.
Calculation: Adaptive trail = Multiply ATR by a bounded factor equal to a base factor plus a multiplier times the complement of trend quality as a fraction of its maximum. Initialize the previous trail to close minus that loss when unavailable. If current and previous close are both above the previous trail, the trail is the greater of the previous trail and close minus loss; if both are below, it is the lesser of the previous trail and close plus loss; otherwise it is close minus loss when close > previous trail, and close plus loss otherwise.
Calculation: Directional trend = For long, chart fast EMA > slow EMA AND close > fast EMA AND close > adaptive trail AND ADX >= trend threshold AND bullish timeframe count >= trend-count threshold; reverse the directional comparisons and use bearish count for short. A late long requires long trend AND close > trail plus an ATR-distance threshold AND RSI > an upper threshold AND trend quality < the strong-quality threshold; reverse for a late short using a lower RSI threshold. Long exhaustion requires long trend AND (RSI > a higher upper threshold OR (trend quality < previous trend quality AND trend quality < weak-quality threshold AND momentum falling)) AND close > fast EMA plus an ATR-distance threshold AND volume is not strong; reverse for short using a lower RSI threshold and rising momentum.
Calculation: Directional score = Clamp the sum to the percentage range. Add configurable weights for each true condition: directional chart EMA state; close on the directional side of the adaptive trail; ADX at or above its trend threshold; strong volume, otherwise a smaller weight for soft volume; directional timeframe count times a per-timeframe weight; directional squeeze; directional flux; opposite-side liquidity sweep; directional divergence; directional candle; directional breakout; directional retest; change of character, otherwise a smaller weight for a continuation break, otherwise a still smaller weight for matching structure bias; Fibonacci confluence, otherwise a smaller weight for a matching valid Fibonacci range with close on the retracement side of its midpoint; block retest, otherwise a smaller weight for an active matching block with close beyond its directional outer boundary; and trend quality at or above its strong threshold, otherwise a smaller weight when at or above its weak threshold. Subtract a weight when the valid matching Fibonacci range has close beyond its midpoint in the trend direction AND trend quality is below its strong threshold. The weak-quality threshold is below the strong-quality threshold.
Calculation: Early setup = For long, (directional squeeze AND bullish flux) OR (downside sweep AND (momentum rising OR bullish candle)) OR (bullish divergence AND momentum rising) OR (momentum crosses above its signal AND RSI < an upper threshold). For short reverse all directional conditions and use RSI > a lower threshold. An enabled early setup additionally requires directional momentum AND directional flux AND soft volume AND opposing timeframe count <= a maximum AND NOT (opposing directional trend OR opposing timeframe count >= a context threshold).
Calculation: Re-entry setup = A direction is due if no previous signal in that direction exists or the elapsed chart bars since that signal >= a configured duration converted to chart bars, rounded up. A long pullback requires pullback entries enabled AND long trend AND long due AND low <= fast EMA plus an ATR buffer AND close > fast EMA AND close > open AND momentum rising AND long score >= early-entry threshold AND bearish timeframe count <= its maximum AND long is not late. A long continuation requires long trend AND long due AND close > the highest high of a preceding-bar lookback AND soft volume AND long score >= entry threshold AND bullish timeframe count >= entry-count threshold AND long is not late AND trend quality >= weak threshold. Reverse the price, momentum and timeframe directions for short.
Calculation: Valid setup = The safe long setup is long retest OR bullish block retest OR bullish Fibonacci confluence OR long pullback OR (downside sweep AND bullish candle AND bearish timeframe count <= its maximum). The aggressive long setup is upward breakout OR upward structure break OR enabled early long setup OR long continuation. Choose safe, aggressive, or their OR according to the entry-mode switch. Define the short setup symmetrically.
Calculation: Entry levels = On an entry store the close as entry price and calculate an ATR stop below a long entry or above a short entry. If swing stops are enabled, also calculate a swing stop from the latest correctly positioned pivot and a block stop from the correctly positioned active matching block boundary, each offset outward by an ATR buffer; substitute the ATR stop for either unavailable or incorrectly positioned candidate. Select the highest candidate for long or lowest for short. Otherwise use the ATR stop. Store first target as entry plus the entry-to-stop risk times a configurable reward multiple for long, or minus it for short; the multiple may depend on whether entry-bar trend quality is strong, intermediate or weak. On each bar, the effective stop is entry price only when moving to breakeven is enabled AND that bar's high reaches the stored first target for long, or its low reaches the target for short; otherwise it is the stored stop. This breakeven substitution is not persistent.
Long: Bullish Fibonacci confluence OR bullish block retest.
Short: Bearish Fibonacci confluence OR bearish block retest.
Long: Position side != long AND long is not late AND ((long score >= entry threshold AND long score >= short score plus lead threshold AND bullish timeframe count >= entry-count threshold AND valid long setup) OR (enabled early long setup AND long score >= early-entry threshold AND long score >= short score plus the nonnegative difference between lead threshold and a lead reduction AND bearish timeframe count <= its maximum) OR long pullback OR long continuation).
Short: Position side != short AND short is not late AND ((short score >= entry threshold AND short score >= long score plus lead threshold AND bearish timeframe count >= entry-count threshold AND valid short setup) OR (enabled early short setup AND short score >= early-entry threshold AND short score >= long score plus the nonnegative difference between lead threshold and a lead reduction AND bullish timeframe count <= its maximum) OR short pullback OR short continuation).
Exit: long, no long entry signal on the current bar AND (close < adaptive trail OR close < slow EMA OR close <= effective stop OR short score > long score plus an exit-score margin OR long exhaustion).
Exit: short, no short entry signal on the current bar AND (close > adaptive trail OR close > slow EMA OR close >= effective stop OR long score > short score plus an exit-score margin OR short exhaustion).
