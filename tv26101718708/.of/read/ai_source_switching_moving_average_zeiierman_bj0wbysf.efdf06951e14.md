Calculation: Feature vector for each of open, high, low, and close = (trend, mean reversion, momentum, volatility, range position, slope). Trend is the shorter EMA minus the longer EMA, divided by feature ATR, clamped to a symmetric limit and divided by that limit; use zero if ATR is zero. Mean reversion is the negative deviation from the source SMA divided by source standard deviation, clamped and normalized in the same way; use zero if standard deviation is zero. Momentum is source divided by its earlier value minus one, divided by a momentum scale, then clamped and normalized. Volatility is source standard deviation scaled to its rolling minimum and maximum and mapped to [-1,1], using the midpoint when the extremes coincide. Range position is the source's position between the current low and high mapped to [-1,1] and clamped, or zero when high equals low. Slope is source minus its earlier value divided by feature ATR, then clamped and normalized, or zero if ATR is zero.
Calculation: Historical banks = on each confirmed bar after the warmup, store each valid source feature vector from a learning horizon ago with the current close minus the close at that horizon classified by sign and by whether its magnitude exceeds one or two multiples of the horizon bar's feature ATR times a learning sensitivity. Classes are zero or signed weak, medium, and strong integers. Keep a bounded newest-first bank per source and a combined bank of four times that capacity; insert sources in open, high, low, close order.
Calculation: Feature weights = optionally compute Fisher scores for each feature from the combined bank's positive and negative classes as squared difference of class means divided by the sum of class variances plus a numerical stabilizer. When the bank and both classes meet their minimum sizes, divide each score by the maximum score, multiply by a scale and apply a floor; if the maximum is zero, use a normalized score of one. Otherwise use unit raw weights. Starting from unit weights, move each weight toward its raw weight by an adaptation fraction on every confirmed bar; use unit weights when optimization is disabled.
Calculation: Analog statistics for each source = scan its newest-first bank at a fixed row spacing, exclude zero classes, and retain up to the configured number of smallest distances, where distance is the sum over features of feature weight times log(1 + absolute feature difference). Weight each retained class by 1/(1 + distance). Analog is the weighted mean class, or zero without neighbors. Agreement is the fraction of total neighbor weight with the sign of the analog when its magnitude exceeds a direction threshold, otherwise zero. Tightness is one minus mean neighbor distance divided by a scaled sum of feature weights plus a stabilizer, clamped to [0,1]; use zero mean distance without neighbors.
Calculation: Neural score = optionally train a linear model with one coefficient per close feature and a bias. Initialize feature coefficients to a small positive value and bias and Adam moments to zero. On each confirmed bar after warmup, if the horizon-old close features are valid and the outcome is nonzero, train against the sign of the outcome using the Huber-loss gradient and Adam with bias correction. Evaluate the updated model on each current source feature vector; use zero when neural training is disabled.
Calculation: Source rank = clamp to [0,1] a weighted sum of absolute analog normalized by the maximum class magnitude, agreement, tightness, sigmoid of the neural score after clamping its input, and a bonus when the full neighbor count is available. Until every source bank exceeds its readiness minimum, assign all sources the same rank. Select the highest-ranked source, breaking ties in open, high, low, close order. The adaptive source is an EMA of the selected source's current value.
Calculation: Adaptive Supertrend = average analog, agreement, and tightness across the four sources; clamp their weighted sum, using the absolute average analog, to [0,1] as model drive. Multiply the base ATR multiplier by 1 + adaptivity times (1 - drive). Subtract and add that product times Supertrend ATR from the adaptive source to obtain raw lower and upper bands. Initialize the trailing lower and upper bands to their raw bands; thereafter set the lower band to the maximum of its raw value and its prior value when prior close is above the prior lower band, otherwise to its raw value. Set the upper band to the minimum of its raw value and its prior value when prior close is below the prior upper band, otherwise to its raw value. Initialize direction bullish; change bearish to bullish when close exceeds the prior upper band, and bullish to bearish when close is below the prior lower band; otherwise retain direction.
Long: Adaptive Supertrend direction = bullish.
Short: Adaptive Supertrend direction = bearish.
