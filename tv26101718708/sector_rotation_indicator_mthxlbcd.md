## Description

[b]Overview

[/b]Quantitative sector rotation system designed to select the ETF or market stock with the greatest relative strength using technical indicators from the OPERATIONAL CENTER. It aims to maintain exposure to the leading asset during favorable market phases and reduce risk when the stock market regime loses strength. 


[b]Operational Center[/b]

[b]1. SYSTEMATIC RISK[/b]

The objective is to detect market conditions that may be associated with elevated macroeconomic, geopolitical, or global systemic risk. The row of “Riesgo Sistematico” component incorporates an adapted implementation of concepts and calculation logic from the Bubble Risk Model (BRM) originally developed by EdgeTools. It is not an exact replica of the original indicator and is used only as one component within this broader indicator; the author's credits are provided at the bottom of the page. 

This indicator introduces its own additional condition "EXTREME RISK" category, a label that only triggers when the S&P 500 price is below its EMA80, the EMA20/EMA60 score of the consumer staples index (IXR) exceeds that of the technology index (IXT), and the Bubble Risk Model indicates High Risk (its line turns red).

Combines 4 components calculated on the SPX:
•	Year-over-Year (YoY) return relative to its historical percentile.
•	Persistence of positive days (63 sessions) relative to its historical percentile.
•	Distance of the price to its 200 SMA in ATR units.
•	Movement efficiency (return/volatility) over 63 sessions.

The Operational Center features the following categories: 🟣 Extreme Risk (dark red/purple). 🔴 High Risk (fragile model state, red). ⚪ Neutral Risk (gray). 🟢 Low Risk (healthy state, green).

Trading is permitted in low-risk, neutral-risk, and high-risk zones. The exit signal is EXTREME RISK indicated by the Operational Center, which will display a label at the top of the chart with the signal Extreme Risk Close for the last Long position. Extreme risk anticipates an imminent recoil, unless the 80-period EMA is recovered.

[b]2. MARKET CYCLE / TREND[/b]

[i]2.1 Market Cycle[/i]
Classifies the market phase using the position of the SP500 relative to the 21 EMA, 90 EMA, and 200 EMA. It identifies boom, expansion, crisis, and recession phases based on the index's trend structure.
🟢 Peak / Boom (EMA 1-21): The SP500 is above the 21 EMA, showing immediate strength and an uptrend.
🔵 Expansion (EMA 22-90): The price is between the 90 EMA and 21 EMA, indicating a consolidation or moderate growth phase.
🟠 Crisis (EMA 91-200): The price is between the 200 EMA and 90 EMA, reflecting a loss of strength and trend deterioration.
🔴 Recession (EMA >200): The SP500 is below the 200 EMA, indicating structural market weakness.
- Clarification: Only invest when the Market Cycle is in PEAK or EXPANSION.

[i]2.2 Stock Market Trend[/i]
Shows the dominant market direction through the SP500 position within the calculated cycle. Serves as a context filter to determine whether the environment is bullish, neutral, or bearish.
🟢 Bullish: When the cycle is between EMA 1-21. The SP500 is above the 21 EMA, indicating strength and a positive trend.
🟡 Neutral-Bullish: When the cycle is between EMA 22-55. The price maintains a positive structure but loses momentum against the 21 EMA.
🟠 Neutral-Bearish: When the cycle is between EMA 56-90. The price approaches the 90 EMA, showing trend deterioration.
🔴 Bearish: When the cycle is above EMA 91.

[b]3. METRICS (SPX)[/b]

- Header: Market health % is weighted (VIX 40%, RSI 30%, Amplitude 30%). The color uses:🟢 ≤35 (Good health), 36-64⚪ (Neutral), and 🔴 ≥65 High Risk. Low is Better
- VIX: Expected volatility of the SPX. ≤10 Euphoria · ≤20 Calm · ≤30 Neutral · <44 Fear · ≥44 Panic.
- RSI: Average RSI(14) of SPX. Risk increases with oversold conditions and moderately with overbought conditions, with the latter capped at 50.
- Breadth: Higher is better.  Breadth Score (0-100) = 33.3% A/D Line + 33.3% New Highs/Lows + 16.7% S5TH (% > SMA200) + 16.7% S5FI (% > SMA50), with each component normalized to 0-100. Categories; Healthy ≥ 60 | Neutral 40-59 | Weak < 40

[b]4. DOMINANCE[/b]

Represents the relative strength of the selected ETF through the weighted score of 7 indicators. The combined weighting avoids relying on a single signal and combines relative leadership, momentum, trend, and risk.

[b]5. DOMINANCE STRENGTH[/b]

Translates the score value into an actionable operational reading: strong dominance, moderate advantage, or equilibrium. Facilitates visual interpretation of the signal intensity generated by the model.
🟢 Strong Dominance (Above 5%): The fast moving average is widely separated from the slow one. Strong and accelerated bullish momentum exists.
🟡 Moderate Advantage (Between 1% and 5%): The fast moving average leads the slow one by a narrow margin. This means there is a bullish trend, but contained, calm, or in a maturing phase without excessive euphoria. In this phase, a value closer to 1% indicates that assets are more evenly matched.
⚪ Equilibrium / Potential Rotation (Between -1% and 1%): Moving averages are virtually touching. Assets are technically tied, and a change in leadership could occur soon.
🔴 Bearish Dominance (Below -1%): The fast moving average is below the slow one, indicating clear structural weakness. Dominance should have rotated.

[b]6. TREND CHANGE[/b]

Indicates when the system rotates its active position to another ETF because that asset has taken the lead in the score.

[i]Factors that determine the rotation:[/i]

- Score (EMA 20 / EMA 60) → The percentage spread between a fast EMA (20) and a slow EMA (60) of each ETF. A higher score means stronger and more sustained trend momentum.
- Market Regime Filter (S&P 500 vs. SMA 200) → Defines whether the minimum score buffer is required (bullish market) or not (bearish market).
- Minimum Score Buffer → When comparing three or more ETFs, a new leader must exceed the active ETF's score by a minimum margin before the system rotates.
- Anti-Rotation Filter → Limits switches during high-risk periods, blocking rotations toward more volatile ETFs.

[b]7. CANDLE STATE[/b]

- Objective: Facilitate chart reading by classifying and color-coding candles as bullish (cyan) or bearish (fuchsia).
- Mathematical Basis: Composite Kernel Regression model consisting of Epanechnikov, Logistic, and Wave Kernel models.
- Key Feature: Precise identification of trend direction (bullish/bearish) without needing to add structural or complementary indicators.

[b]8. AVOIDING FALSE SIGNALS
[/b]
[i]8.1 Long Filter (EMA 90-200)[/i]

Extreme risk closes long positions in highly complex macroeconomic situations.

Furthermore, the system utilizes a regime filter based on the SP500: when the color band turns blue or red, it signals to close the position; it is most likely that the price will move from below the 80 EMA to below the 200 EMA in a short period.

- Blue Zone: When the SP500 is below the 80 EMA.
- Red Zone: When the SP500 loses the 200 EMA, indicating a higher-risk bearish regime.

[i]8.2 Close Filter (Bollinger)[/i]

This indicator shows +1P when a candle is completely outside the Bollinger Band, providing an additional signal that may help avoid acting prematurely.

The Systematic Risk line can also provide additional context when it is far from its moving average and remains in the green zone, indicating that the current risk conditions remain favorable.

[b]9. Upper Chart Panel[/b]

The main chart displays trades executed by the strategy:
- Long Signals → Entries into the selected ETF.
- Close Signals → Closures of previous positions.
- Early Risk signal (risk anticipation) and Extreme Risk (risk confirmation).
- Risk RSI (exceptional situations, average RSI below 23).
- Evolution of ETF rotation.

[b]10. Lower Chart Panel[/b]

- Bands: The blue band represents the EMA below 80 periods and the red band represents the SPX EMA below 200 periods.
- The lines represent ETF strength.
- Important: Extra line representing systematic risk with three colors: green line (low risk), gray line (neutral), and red zone (high risk).

[b]Originality and how to use[/b]

Unlike a conventional rotation indicator, this one combines, in a single panel, the macro reading of the market (regime, cycle, systematic risk, VIX, RSI and market breadth) with the specific rotation between ETFs and market stocks. This way, every rotation signal is filtered by the current risk context rather than generated in isolation.

[i]How to use[/i]

1. Timeframe: The user can choose any timeframe they prefer. This does not affect the indicator's functionality.
2. Recommended chart: ideally, always use it on the charts of the assets you are comparing (ETF A, ETF B…), since the Long/Close signals, the Bollinger Bands and the candle coloring are drawn on the chart's asset.

[i]Structure: macro + specific[/i]

- Macro part: market regime (SPX versus its moving averages), market cycle (Boom, Expansion, Crisis, Recession), trend, systematic risk with its labels (Extreme Risk, Early Risk, Risk RSI) and SPX metrics (VIX, average RSI and market breadth) with a composite score.
- Specific part: strength and dominance between the ETFs (EMA20/EMA60), absolute momentum filter, Long/Close signals, Bollinger Bands (+1P/-1P) and candle state via Kernel Regression.
Both parts are integrated in the "Operations Center" table, which brings together the macro context and the specific rotation signal in a single view.

[i]3. Very Important: [/i]

- If Extreme Risk / Close A appears, the indicator is signaling that A should be closed due to risk.
- That close does not change the DOMINANCE row: it will keep showing A (as the dominant asset, is correct).
- The cell will only change when the system selects another ETF, for example B.
- The user must manually change the assets they wish to compare from the settings. The default selected assets are SP:IXT and SP:IXR. Changing these assets does not alter how the indicator works; the choice of assets is left entirely to the user's discretion. The "Extreme Risk" label/signal is not affected by the user's asset selection (ETF A–E): it is always calculated from fixed reference data (SPX, IXT and IXR).
- Configurable Alert Conditions: Extreme Risk, Early Risk, Risk RSI, Price below EMA 200, Price above EMA 200, Fast EMA above Slow EMA, Fast EMA below Slow EMA (with left click). This is what turns our indicator into an operational tool.

[i]4. Limitations[/i]

Since the macroeconomic regime is linked exclusively to the S&P 500, it may not be representative of alternative assets that are uncorrelated with it (such as commodities or crypto-assets…). The asset comparison regime is unaffected by this, so it will continue to function correctly.

f_calc_tail_risk_score(float c1, float c2, float c3, float c4) =>
    arr = array.new_float(4)
    array.set(arr, 0, nz(c1, 50.0))
    array.set(arr, 1, nz(c2, 50.0))
    array.set(arr, 2, nz(c3, 50.0))
    array.set(arr, 3, nz(c4, 50.0))
    array.sort(arr, order.ascending)

    float_idx = 0.7 * 3.0
    lower_idx = math.floor(float_idx)
    upper_idx = math.min(3, lower_idx + 1)
    weight = float_idx - lower_idx
    lower_val = array.get(arr, int(lower_idx))
    upper_val = array.get(arr, int(upper_idx))
    quantile_score = lower_val * (1 - weight) + upper_val * weight

    max_comp = array.max(arr)
    tail_penalty = max_comp > 85 ? (max_comp - 85) * 0.3 : 0.0

    (100.0 - math.min(100.0, quantile_score + tail_penalty)) / 100.0

f_risk_bundle(short_len, long_len, vol_period, critical_thr, base_maturity, transition_resist, use_adapt, normal_smooth, extreme_smooth, early_smooth) =>
    close_252 = nz(close[252], close)
    yoy_return_raw = (close - close_252) / close_252 * 100
    yoy_pct_short = ta.percentrank(yoy_return_raw, short_len)
    yoy_pct_long = ta.percentrank(yoy_return_raw, long_len)

    daily_return = close - nz(close[1], close)
    positive_days = math.sum(daily_return > 0 ? 1.0 : 0.0, 63)
    persistence_raw = positive_days / 63.0 * 100.0
    pers_pct_short = ta.percentrank(persistence_raw, short_len)
    pers_pct_long = ta.percentrank(persistence_raw, long_len)

    ma200_val = ta.sma(close, 200)
    atr200_val = ta.atr(200)
    ma_dist_raw = atr200_val > 0 ? (close - ma200_val) / atr200_val : 0.0
    ma_pct_short = ta.percentrank(ma_dist_raw, short_len)
    ma_pct_long = ta.percentrank(ma_dist_raw, long_len)

    close_63 = nz(close[63], close)
    ret_63_raw = (close - close_63) / close_63 * 100
    atr_63_val = ta.atr(63)
    price_level_63 = ta.sma(close, 63)
    norm_atr_raw = price_level_63 > 0 ? atr_63_val / price_level_63 * 100 : 1.0
    efficiency_raw = norm_atr_raw > 0 ? ret_63_raw / norm_atr_raw : 0.0
    eff_pct_short = ta.percentrank(efficiency_raw, short_len)
    eff_pct_long = ta.percentrank(efficiency_raw, long_len)

    comp1_raw = math.max(yoy_pct_short, yoy_pct_long)
    comp2_raw = math.max(pers_pct_short, pers_pct_long)
    comp3_raw = math.max(ma_pct_short, ma_pct_long)
    comp4_raw = math.max(eff_pct_short, eff_pct_long)

    atr_14_val = ta.atr(14)
    price_14_val = ta.sma(close, 14)
    norm_atr_regime = price_14_val > 0 ? atr_14_val / price_14_val * 100 : 1.0
    vol_percentile = ta.percentrank(norm_atr_regime, vol_period)
    vol_maturity_scalar = vol_percentile < 25 ? 1.5 : vol_percentile > 75 ? 0.6 : 1.0
    regime_scaled_maturity = base_maturity * vol_maturity_scalar

    raw_score = f_calc_tail_risk_score(comp1_raw, comp2_raw, comp3_raw, comp4_raw)

    var float smoothed_score = na
    is_in_extreme = raw_score < 0.3 or raw_score > 0.8
    smooth_period = use_adapt ? (is_in_extreme ? extreme_smooth : normal_smooth) : normal_smooth
    alpha = 2.0 / (smooth_period + 1.0)
    smoothed_score := na(smoothed_score) ? raw_score : smoothed_score + alpha * (raw_score - smoothed_score)

    var float early_smoothed = na
    early_period = use_adapt ? (is_in_extreme ? extreme_smooth : early_smooth) : early_smooth
    early_alpha = 2.0 / (early_period + 1.0)
    early_smoothed := na(early_smoothed) ? raw_score : early_smoothed + early_alpha * (raw_score - early_smoothed)

    var int state_code = 2
    var int bars_in_state = 0
    var float state_confidence = 0.0

    proposed_code = smoothed_score < critical_thr ? 0 : smoothed_score < critical_thr + 0.2 ? 1 : 2
    state_maturity = math.min(1.0, bars_in_state / regime_scaled_maturity)

    score_supports_state = (state_code == 0 and smoothed_score < critical_thr + 0.05) or
                           (state_code == 1 and smoothed_score >= critical_thr - 0.05 and smoothed_score < critical_thr + 0.25) or
                           (state_code == 2 and smoothed_score >= critical_thr + 0.15)

    state_confidence := score_supports_state ? math.min(1.0, state_confidence + 0.05) : math.max(0.0, state_confidence - 0.1)

    transition_allowed = state_maturity < 0.5 or state_confidence < transition_resist or
                         (proposed_code != state_code and
                          ((proposed_code == 0 and smoothed_score < critical_thr - 0.05) or
                           (proposed_code == 2 and smoothed_score > critical_thr + 0.25)))

    if transition_allowed and proposed_code != state_code
        state_code := proposed_code
        bars_in_state := 1
        state_confidence := 0.3
    else
        bars_in_state := bars_in_state + 1

    [state_code, smoothed_score, early_smoothed]

f_vix_base_risk(float vix) =>
    float r = 95.0
    if vix <= 10.0
        r := 10.0
    else if vix <= 15.0
        r := 10.0 + (vix - 10.0) / 5.0 * 15.0
    else if vix <= 25.0
        r := 25.0 + (vix - 15.0) / 10.0 * 25.0
    else if vix <= 37.0
        r := 50.0 + (vix - 25.0) / 12.0 * 25.0
    else if vix <= 50.0
        r := 75.0 + (vix - 37.0) / 13.0 * 20.0
    r

f_vix_risk_score(float vix) =>
    math.max(0.0, math.min(100.0, f_vix_base_risk(vix)))

f_rsi_risk_score(float rsi) =>
    float oversold   = 100.0 - rsi
    float overbought = 50.0 * math.max(0.0, math.min(1.0, (rsi - 65.0) / 25.0))
    math.max(0.0, math.min(100.0, math.max(oversold, overbought)))

f_breadth_risk_score(float b_score, float vix) =>
    float base = 100.0 - b_score
    float s    = f_vix_stress_weight(vix)
    base + s * math.max(0.0, 50.0 - base)

        w   += 0.4
    if not na(rsi)
        acc += f_rsi_risk_score(rsi) * 0.3
        w   += 0.3
    if not na(b_score)
        acc += f_breadth_risk_score(b_score, vix) * 0.3
        w   += 0.3
    w > 0 ? math.max(0.0, math.min(100.0, acc / w)) : na

f_kr_daily(int h, int mid_len) =>
    var float[] w_ep = array.new_float()
    var float[] w_lo = array.new_float()
    var float[] w_wa = array.new_float()
    if array.size(w_ep) == 0
        for i = 0 to h
            float u  = (i * i) / (h * h)
            float uw = i / h
            array.push(w_ep, u <= 1.0 ? 0.75 * (1.0 - u) : 0.0)
            array.push(w_lo, 1.0 / (math.exp(u) + 2.0 + math.exp(-u)))
            array.push(w_wa, math.cos(uw * math.pi / 2.0) * (1.0 - uw))

    float s_ep  = 0.0
    float s_lo  = 0.0
    float s_wa  = 0.0
    float sw_ep = 0.0
    float sw_lo = 0.0
    float sw_wa = 0.0
    for i = 0 to h
        float c   = close[i]
        float w1  = array.get(w_ep, i)
        float w2  = array.get(w_lo, i)
        float w3  = array.get(w_wa, i)
        s_ep  += c * w1
        s_lo  += c * w2
        s_wa  += c * w3
        sw_ep += w1
        sw_lo += w2
        sw_wa += w3

    float ep = sw_ep > 0 ? s_ep / sw_ep : close
    float lo = sw_lo > 0 ? s_lo / sw_lo : close
    float wa = sw_wa > 0 ? s_wa / sw_wa : close

    float ep_dev = ep != 0 ? (close - ep) / ep : 0.0
    float lo_dev = lo != 0 ? (close - lo) / lo : 0.0
    float wa_dev = wa != 0 ? (close - wa) / wa : 0.0

    float av  = (ep_dev + lo_dev + wa_dev) / 3.0
    float mid = ta.sma(av, mid_len)
    [av, mid]

f_kr_dyn(int n, int stride, int mid_len, bool sub) =>
    var float[] w_ep = array.new_float()
    var float[] w_lo = array.new_float()
    var float[] w_wa = array.new_float()
    var int last_n = -1
    if n != last_n
        array.clear(w_ep)
        array.clear(w_lo)
        array.clear(w_wa)
        for i = 0 to n
            float u  = (i * i) / (n * n)
            float uw = i / n
            array.push(w_ep, u <= 1.0 ? 0.75 * (1.0 - u) : 0.0)
            array.push(w_lo, 1.0 / (math.exp(u) + 2.0 + math.exp(-u)))
            array.push(w_wa, math.cos(uw * math.pi / 2.0) * (1.0 - uw))
        last_n := n

    float s_ep  = 0.0
    float s_lo  = 0.0
    float s_wa  = 0.0
    float sw_ep = 0.0
    float sw_lo = 0.0
    float sw_wa = 0.0
    for i = 0 to n
        float c = close[i * stride]
        if not na(c)
            float w1 = array.get(w_ep, i)
            float w2 = array.get(w_lo, i)
            float w3 = array.get(w_wa, i)
            s_ep  += c * w1
            s_lo  += c * w2
            s_wa  += c * w3
            sw_ep += w1
            sw_lo += w2
            sw_wa += w3

    float ep = sw_ep > 0 ? s_ep / sw_ep : close
    float lo = sw_lo > 0 ? s_lo / sw_lo : close
    float wa = sw_wa > 0 ? s_wa / sw_wa : close

    float ep_dev = ep != 0 ? (close - ep) / ep : 0.0
    float lo_dev = lo != 0 ? (close - lo) / lo : 0.0
    float wa_dev = wa != 0 ? (close - wa) / wa : 0.0

    float av  = (ep_dev + lo_dev + wa_dev) / 3.0
    float mid_sma = ta.sma(av, math.min(mid_len, 4000))
    float mid_ema = f_ema_dyn(av, mid_len)
    float mid     = (sub and (mid_len > 4000 or na(mid_sma))) ? mid_ema : mid_sma
    [av, mid]
