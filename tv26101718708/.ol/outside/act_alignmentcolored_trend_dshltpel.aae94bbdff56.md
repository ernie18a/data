Use five successively higher timeframes. Set bullish bias when price is above both highest-timeframe opens, and bearish bias when below both. If those timeframes disagree, use the middle timeframe only when its bar breaks and closes beyond the prior bar in one direction; this is a countertrend bias. Otherwise there is no direction. With aligned highest-timeframe bias, stand aside if both highest-timeframe bars are inside without a live break, or if their directional breaks are failing. Otherwise mark the bias watchable; upgrade it to active when any state or direction timeframe breaks a prior extreme in the bias direction and the trigger timeframe also breaks its prior extreme that way. With conflicting highest timeframes, allow only a watchable countertrend signal when the middle timeframe breaks and closes beyond its prior extreme. Emit long for a bullish watchable or active bias and short for a bearish one; when no qualifying direction remains, turn the signal off. Related-market breakouts affect confirmation and grading only, not direction or whether a signal is on. No options decisions are defined.
