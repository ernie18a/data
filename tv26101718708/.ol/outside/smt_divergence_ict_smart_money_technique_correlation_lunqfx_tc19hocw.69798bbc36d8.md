On each confirmed swing high, compare the traded instrument’s move from its prior swing high with the correlated instrument’s move over the same interval. For a positively correlated pair, compare highs; for a negatively correlated pair, compare the correlated instrument’s lows. Mark a bearish reversal cue if one instrument makes a higher extreme and the other does not, provided the correlated instrument’s move is at least a minimum meaningful size relative to its average range. On each confirmed swing low, apply the corresponding rule to lows for a positive pair or highs for a negative pair; mark a bullish reversal cue if one instrument makes a lower extreme and the other does not, subject to the same move-size filter. By default, emit cues only when the absolute rolling correlation meets a minimum threshold, and compare pivots only when the correlation’s sign has stayed the same between them. These cues indicate potential short or long reversals, respectively; they do not define entries or exits.
