On intraday charts, classify the prior-session CPR width against its recent history: narrow favors a trend day, wide favors a range day, and intermediate width is neutral. Set direction bullish when price is above the CPR and bearish when below; inside the CPR has no direction. Treat options as expensive when implied volatility is high relative to its history or implied volatility materially exceeds realized volatility. If data are unavailable or warming, give no actionable verdict. Otherwise: a range day, or a neutral day without direction, favors selling premium only when options are expensive; otherwise stay out. On a narrow, trend-favoring day with direction, buy a call for bullish direction or a put for bearish direction only when options are not expensive, expiry is not too near, and the first directional pivot target is at least one implied-volatility expected move from the session open. If that target-distance condition fails, favor selling premium; if options are expensive, wait; if expiry is too near, stay out. For other directional cases, show only a directional lean, not a trade. On higher timeframes, require an intraday chart. Override every verdict with stay out during a high-volatility tail-risk regime. Gap, stretched-price, and theta-risk checks annotate the safety of a suggested option buy but do not change the verdict.
