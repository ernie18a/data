Use regular-session prices. Define the current week’s high and low from the first trading day as it develops, then hold those levels for the rest of the week; use their midpoint and the prior week’s range as context. A level touch occurs when a bar overlaps its configurable proximity band. Treat the touch as active until price leaves a wider release band; refresh setup freshness only on a new touch. Apply configurable freshness windows, family permissions, and separate first-weekly-day permissions. Require signals during the regular session.

Long reaction entries require a fresh touch of the weekly low and no prior accepted signal from that touch. Enter on either a close crossing above the 50-period simple moving average while above the weekly low, or a bullish close above the weekly low while the close is above that average. The latter continuation path also requires price to reclaim the weekly low, defend it on the touch bar, or bounce above it while the touch remains fresh. Temporarily block continuation while the close is at or above the weekly midpoint; clear that block if price closes back below the midpoint before the setup expires. The moving-average reversal path is exempt from this midpoint block.

Short reaction entries mirror this logic at the weekly high: require a fresh high touch, then either a close crossing below the 50-period simple moving average while below the weekly high, or a bearish close below the weekly high while below that average. For continuation, require a cross below, rejection at the touch, or fresh bearish bounce below the high. Temporarily block continuation while the close is at or below the midpoint, clearing the block on a close back above it before expiry. The moving-average reversal path is exempt.

For flip entries, confirm a break above the weekly high after the timeframe-dependent required consecutive closes above it; invalidate the flip on a close back at or below the high. A long pullback entry requires a fresh return to the flipped level that closes bullish above it, or a fresh bullish bounce above it, with price above the moving average. A long momentum entry requires a fresh confirmed flip and a bullish candle that closes above the flipped level and above the prior close. Mirror these rules below the weekly low for short flips: confirm consecutive closes below, invalidate on a close back at or above the low, and require a fresh bearish rejection or bounce below it for pullback entries; momentum requires a bearish candle below the level and below the prior close. Use the same configurable freshness and touch lifecycle rules for flip setups.

Accept at most one signal per direction on a bar, preferring momentum over flip pullback over reaction. A configurable direction-wide cooldown suppresses further same-direction signals. Accepted long entries anchor structural risk to the weekly low for reaction entries and the weekly high for flipped-level entries; accepted shorts anchor to the weekly high for reactions and the weekly low for flips. Calculate the displayed stop using the selected structural ATR, fixed buffer, signal-bar invalidation, wider-of-structural-and-signal-bar, or legacy close-based ATR method. The indicator also computes projected targets from the current weekly extreme, a risk multiple, prior-week structure, or an EMA trail; these are projections, not exit signals, and the code contains no actual exit trigger.
