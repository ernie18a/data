Estimate a mean-reverting equilibrium and stationary scale from recent prices, then measure the current price’s standardized distance from equilibrium. Permit the statistical signal only when mean-reversion speed and half-life are valid, the Hurst measure indicates non-persistent behavior, the unit-root test rejects a random walk, the variance-ratio test indicates mean reversion, and the lookback contains no disqualifying session gap. This signal points long when price is sufficiently far below equilibrium and short when sufficiently far above it; otherwise it is neutral.

Build additional directional signals from available external or market data: an unusually high or low futures-versus-fair-value basis points toward reversion; extreme option skew points according to the script’s skew rule; price unusually far above or below rolling and session-anchored VWAP points toward reversion, with volume, opposing order flow, and toxicity adjusting its strength; a dominant call-heavy gamma wall above price or put-heavy wall below price points toward reversion; and a sufficient majority of constituent stocks with extreme same-sign index-adjusted residuals points in that sign’s direction. A rising put-call ratio at an extreme high points long, while at an extreme low it points short. Missing optional data contributes no signal.

Combine active directional signals using their configured weights and strengths. Apply multipliers for the volatility/trend regime, constituent correlation, implied-versus-realized volatility, cross-asset stress, and expiry-day pin alignment, then clamp the combined multiplier. The prevailing composite sign sets direction. In weighted mode, assign the longest horizon whose composite strength and corresponding reversion-horizon test pass; otherwise try successively shorter horizons. In confluence mode, require the configured minimum number of active layers agreeing with the prevailing direction and map the count to a horizon. In strict mode, require all enabled core layers to agree or be neutral. Suppress a candidate if its expected move to equilibrium does not exceed the horizon-adjusted round-trip cost requirement.

Finally, emit long or short only when the time-of-day and session-warmup filters pass and the market is not in the hard-blocked spike regime; otherwise emit neutral. For a long signal, recommend a call; for a short signal, recommend a put. The selected horizon determines the option’s target delta and strike offset.
