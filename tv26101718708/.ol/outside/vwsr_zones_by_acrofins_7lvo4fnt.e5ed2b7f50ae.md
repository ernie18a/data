On confirmed bars, identify swing highs and lows and form resistance and support zones. Weight each pivot by its trading volume and the price reaction after it forms; merge nearby zones of the same type, reinforce them on new touches, reduce their weight with age, and discard stale, weak, or lowest-ranked zones when limits are exceeded. A bullish break occurs when price closes beyond a resistance zone’s upper edge after the preceding close was at or below that edge; a bearish break is the inverse at a support zone’s lower edge. Require any enabled close-distance, volume-floor, volume-cap, and candle-range filters to pass. If several zones qualify in one direction, select the highest-weight zone; if opposing breaks qualify together, keep only the higher-weight direction. After a break, a bullish retest occurs when price returns to the broken resistance (now support) within the configured waiting window, touches it, then closes back above it with the required upward reaction; bearish retests apply the inverse at broken support (now resistance). Apply the optional mitigation rule to reject repeat retests. Treat enabled breaks and retests as long or short signals. Open a simulated position at the signal-bar close only when flat, risk management is enabled, and session, opening-delay, expiry-day, entry-cutoff, daily-loss-cap, and optional higher-timeframe trend restrictions allow it. Classify the higher-timeframe trend from the relationship between its close and slow moving average and between its fast and slow moving averages; optionally block signals opposing that trend. Set a stop from volatility, optionally adjusted using the broken zone, and profit targets at configured risk multiples. After the first target is reached, optionally move the stop to entry. Exit when the stop or final target is reached, or at the configured intraday force-close time.
