For each instrument, calculate the pivot, central range, and first resistance/support from the prior trading day's high, low, and close. Classify the central range as narrow or wide using configurable width thresholds. Optionally require price to be above/below its trend EMA for bullish/bearish signals; optionally require above-average volume for narrow-range breakouts. Signal BUY CALL if either (a) the range is narrow and the candle closes above the range top and first resistance, is bullish, and passes enabled filters, or (b) the range is wide and price reaches or crosses below the range bottom but closes back above it on a bullish candle and passes the trend filter. Signal BUY PUT on the mirrored narrow-range breakdown below the range bottom and first support, or a wide-range rejection after reaching/crossing above the range top and closing back below it on a bearish candle; apply the corresponding enabled filters. In a narrow range, issue a CALL WATCH when price closes above the pivot at or below first resistance and passes the bullish trend filter; issue a PUT WATCH when it closes below the pivot at or above first support and passes the bearish trend filter. Give BUY CALL precedence over BUY PUT, then CALL WATCH over PUT WATCH; otherwise remain neutral. For any directional setup, derive a stop from either an ATR distance, a recent swing extreme, or the wider of those stop distances, and set a profit target at a configurable risk multiple. The framework defines no exit signal.
