Use a hysteretic directional bias: normalize the instrument’s distance from its EMA by ATR; enter bullish or bearish bias when it crosses the respective configured entry threshold, and return that bias to neutral when it crosses back through its configured exit threshold. Compare short-horizon realized volatility with long-horizon realized volatility to classify it as expanding, compressing, or normal. Combine bias and volatility into a regime: bullish or bearish bias with normal/expanding volatility gives a directional regime; with compression it gives a fading regime. Neutral bias gives range in normal volatility, breakout risk in compression, and unstable in expansion. India VIX at the extreme-risk level overrides the regime to no trade. Sell a put spread in the bullish regime, a call spread in the bearish regime, an iron condor in either fading regime or range, and a wider condor for breakout risk; do not trade in the no-trade regime or when unstable. Place short strikes on the regime-favored side closer to spot and the other side farther away for directional spreads; use approximately symmetric ATR-based offsets for condors, round strikes to the configured increment, and place protective long wings at the configured width. Size positions from VIX risk tiers, reduce size when directional bias coincides with expanding volatility, and scale it further by the selected drawdown band; zero resulting size means no trade. For an active short put or call, exit that leg when spot breaches its stop, set three-quarters of the entry-to-short-strike distance beyond entry spot toward the threatened side. Close all positions on an extreme VIX reading or a VIX shock combining a high reading with a sufficiently large rise from the prior reading.
