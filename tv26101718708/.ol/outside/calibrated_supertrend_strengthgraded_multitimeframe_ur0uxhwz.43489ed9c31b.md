Maintain a directional bias with an adaptive Supertrend: widen its ATR bands when volatility or choppiness rises and tighten them when conditions calm. A close crossing the prior band creates a raw reversal; confirm it only after a meaningful ATR-scaled breach, sufficient time since the previous flip, and bar close. On a confirmed flip, assess trend regime and alignment, higher-timeframe trend agreement, band clearance, trend efficiency, volume pressure, and optional cross-asset direction. Issue a long or short signal only if conviction and flip-strength filters pass and no enabled veto applies, including a volatility spike, unanimous higher-timeframe opposition, a range or volatile regime, or conflicting cross-asset momentum. Strength and thresholds may adapt from the instrument’s historical flip outcomes. Otherwise retain the existing directional bias; use the active Supertrend band as a trailing exit level, with an opposite confirmed flip reversing the bias.
