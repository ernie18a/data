For the traded currency, calculate its divergence from crude oil by comparing each price series’ normalized position relative to its moving average and volatility. Enter long when the divergence has recently exceeded a positive threshold and is now falling, the currency’s short-term return is positive, crude oil’s longer-term average is rising, and their correlation is not strongly negative. Enter short when the divergence has recently fallen below a negative threshold and is now rising, the currency’s short-term return is negative, crude oil’s longer-term average is falling, and their correlation is not strongly negative. Close a long if any of these occurs: the MACD signal line crosses above the MACD line while a smoothed stochastic is overbought; the divergence is below a negative threshold while crude oil’s short-term return is sufficiently negative; or the currency closes below its previous rolling low while its longer-term correlation with crude oil is strongly negative. Close a short if any of these occurs: the MACD signal line crosses below the MACD line while the smoothed stochastic is oversold and crude oil has rebounded sufficiently from its recent low; the divergence is above a positive threshold while crude oil’s short-term return is sufficiently positive; or the currency closes above its previous rolling high while its longer-term correlation with crude oil is strongly negative.
