On the traded equity index, calculate percentage changes over a common lookback for the index and for three external ratios: a growth-stock index divided by the traded index, a small-cap index divided by it, and a high-yield bond fund divided by a Treasury bond fund. Compare the index change with an equity threshold, and count how many ratio changes are positive or negative. Go long when the index rises beyond the threshold and at least two ratios rise. Go short when the index falls beyond the negative threshold and at least two ratios fall. Treat an index rise beyond the threshold with fewer than two rising ratios as a hollow rally and a short or fade-into-strength signal. Also go short on a sharp fall in the high-yield-to-Treasury ratio, defined as below twice the negative equity threshold, or when the index falls beyond its threshold while the 10-year yield rises beyond its separate threshold. Give the latter condition highest priority, followed by the sharp credit-ratio fall, then confirmed risk-off, then hollow rally, then confirmed risk-on; otherwise remain neutral. For confirmed risk-on, classify rising yields as durable, falling yields as fragile, and other yield moves as unqualified risk-on. The indicator specifies no separate exit rule.
