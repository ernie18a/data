For each market, compare weekly net positioning of the selected trader group with the opposite group. Convert that spread to rolling 0–100 historical percentiles over 13, 26, and 52 reports, then weight them 20%, 30%, and 50%. Low readings are bullish; high readings are bearish. If any required percentile is unavailable, report no data. In order: classify a bullish setup when the weighted reading is at most 25 and the 52-report reading at most 30, or an extreme bullish setup when the three readings are at most 10, 10, and 15. A bullish setup turns bullish when the 13-report reading rises from its prior value and the 26-report reading is unchanged or higher; positive weekly net change makes this LONG BUILD, otherwise BULL TURN. Otherwise, report the bullish setup as BUY. Outside these setups, below a weighted reading of 40 is LONG UNWIND if weekly net change is negative, else LONG BIAS. Symmetrically, classify a bearish setup when the weighted reading is at least 75 and the 52-report reading at least 70, or an extreme bearish setup when the three readings are at least 90, 90, and 85. It turns bearish when the 13-report reading falls from its prior value and the 26-report reading is unchanged or lower; negative weekly net change makes this SHORT BUILD, otherwise BEAR TURN. Otherwise, report the bearish setup as TOP RISK. Above a weighted reading of 60, report SHORT COVER if weekly net change is positive, else SHORT BIAS; readings between the bullish and bearish zones are NEUTRAL. Commercial mode reverses the spread orientation, so its positioning perspective is opposite the non-commercial perspective. These are contextual bias labels, not direct trade entries, exits, or orders.
