Measure the traded instrument’s recent price path using normalized net drift and signed path area, and rank the magnitudes of both against their rolling histories. Classify as trending up or down when drift strength meets the trend threshold, using drift’s sign for direction; strong area supports the trend classification, while sufficient drift alone also qualifies. Otherwise, classify as turning when area changes sign after a strong prior curl, drift strength is weakening from a recent peak, and price is near either end of its recent range. Classify all other conditions as range or chop. For a trend, go long when up and short when down. For a turn, fade the range extreme: short near the upper end and long near the lower end. In range or chop, stand aside. Higher-timeframe agreement, benchmark comparison, volume tilt, and historical follow-through are contextual information and do not change this directional rule.
