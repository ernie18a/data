For each selected market, use weekly COT data to calculate stochastic scores (0–100) for the chosen participant group’s net-position difference versus the other group over 13, 26, and 52 weeks, and for its net position over 52 and 156 weeks. Form a market-bullish composite from those scores with weights 10%, 15%, 25%, 25%, and 25%, respectively; invert each score for Non-Commercial participants (contrarian interpretation), and use scores directly for Commercial participants. If any component is unavailable, make no directional decision. Enter long when the composite crosses up into the top quartile (at least 75); enter short when it crosses down into the bottom quartile (at most 25). While in the top quartile, a fall of more than 2 points from the prior week signals a bullish turn; while in the bottom quartile, a rise of more than 2 points signals a bearish turn. Also classify a composite at least 60 as long-biased and at most 40 as short-biased, with the middle range neutral. Use weekly change in participant net positioning to label a long-biased setup as building when flow is bullish on the market-bias axis and unwinding when bearish; label a short-biased setup as building when flow is bearish and covering when bullish. Non-Commercial flow is the inverse of its net-position change; Commercial flow uses its net-position change directly. The indicator provides no explicit exit rule. For relative market bias, subtract the two markets’ composite scores and divide by two; positive favors the first market, negative the second.
