Using the traded asset and synchronized returns from four configurable benchmark markets, classify local price order, directional efficiency, cross-market common-factor concentration, and the traded asset’s signed loading on that factor. First, if systemic concentration is high and either the factor impulse is strong or the local and factor directions conflict, classify as Event/Systemic Risk and reduce trading aggression. Otherwise, classify as Breakout Long or Short when local structure is ordered, efficiency is high, systemic concentration is high, factor impulse is strong, and local and target-attributed factor directions agree; use their shared direction. If that rule does not apply, classify as Trend Long or Short when structure is ordered, efficiency is high, local impulse has a clear direction, and—when systemic concentration is high—the local and factor directions agree; use the local direction. If neither directional rule applies, classify as Mean Reversion when structure is disordered, systemic concentration is low, and efficiency is low. Otherwise classify as No Trade. While required historical and synchronized cross-market data are unavailable, classify as Loading Data. Mean Reversion, Event/Systemic Risk, No Trade, and Loading Data do not specify a long or short direction.
