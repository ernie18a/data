For each monitored instrument and timeframe, identify bullish factors: (1) price makes a lower low while RSI makes a higher low in oversold territory; (2) price makes a lower low while money flow makes a higher low in its oversold territory; (3) unusually high volume accompanies a long lower wick while RSI is oversold. Identify bearish factors symmetrically: price makes a higher high while RSI or money flow makes a lower high in its overbought territory, or unusually high volume accompanies a long upper wick while RSI is overbought. Compare each divergence with the previous corresponding extreme within a finite lookback. Count each active factor once. If volatility is exceptionally expanded, add one point, but only when at least one directional factor is active. Cap the score at four. When bullish factors exist and no bearish factors exist, assign a long direction; when bearish factors exist and no bullish factors exist, assign a short direction. If factors point both ways, mark direction mixed; if neither side has a factor, remain neutral. The indicator provides no exit rule.
