Use confirmed bar data to evaluate trend continuation setups. Go long on a bullish rejection of the session range low, a bullish rejection from the pullback zone of a rising higher-timeframe trend, a confirmed upward structure break or retest, a defended bullish order block, a rising-trend pullback to the short moving average, an upward post-squeeze momentum release, a bullish gap retest, or a qualified opening-drive impulse. Reverse the conditions for shorts. A failed downside liquidity sweep can trigger a long and a failed upside sweep can trigger a short, subject to stronger reversal confirmation. Require each setup’s directional confidence to clear its setup-specific floor, adequate room to the next relevant target, and a valid structural stop; apply the higher-timeframe opposition filter where used. Build directional confidence from trend, momentum, volatility, location, candle and volume evidence, market structure, and cross-market context: compare the traded market’s recent return with a broad-market ETF, assess technology-sector relative strength, use index-futures swing divergence, market breadth, volatility futures and their term structure, and order-flow delta. These external readings can support or oppose a direction and affect confidence or explicit higher-timeframe gates. Place the target near the next relevant support or resistance, adjusted for nearby level congestion, and the stop beyond the setup’s invalidation level with a volatility and wick buffer. Exit each signal ticket when its target or stop is reached; only allow a stop to trigger after the entry bar.
