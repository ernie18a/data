On a downside sweep of the instrument’s prior range, evaluate a long; on an upside sweep, evaluate a short. Score each side using one point each for aligned higher-timeframe sweep bias, active session, volume spike, confirming sweep in the correlated market, relative strength versus that market, a non-overridden higher-timeframe gap in the same direction, same-direction daily gap, proximity to the corresponding prior-day extreme, supportive volatility-index movement, and at least two same-direction sweeps. Add one point for being near any directional structure and two each for a strong engulfing reversal at structure and an engulfing entry inside a recent same-direction gap. Cap each score at ten. Long or short setup alerts require the matching sweep and a score of at least four. For the displayed directional verdict, choose the higher score only when it leads by at least one point; call it actionable when it is at least six, or at least four with a lead of two or more. A conflict between an opposite gap and sweep forces a wait. At score eight or higher, take the direction when correlated-market confirmation and directional structure are present; otherwise wait for a break of structure if no confirming break level exists. At scores six or seven, indicate scaling in when volume confirms, otherwise mark valid; at four or five, mark marginal. An opposite-direction sweep can be labeled a fade when the dominant score is at least five. After a downside sweep, exit the active long if price closes below the prior-range low minus one ATR; after an upside sweep, exit the active short if price closes above the prior-range high plus one ATR.
