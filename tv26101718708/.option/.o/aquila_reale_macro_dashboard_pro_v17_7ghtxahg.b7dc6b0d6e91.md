Sum these external-market scores to form the bias (daily bias drives the trade signal). Classify the daily scenario in priority order: oil above 100 with the traded asset down over 0.5% means stagflation; oil above 100 with it up over 0.5% means active hedge; oil below 100 with it up over 0.5% means cautious risk-off if the 10-year yield is above 4.3%, otherwise reflation; oil below 100 with it down over 0.5% means risk-on; all other cases are mixed. Signal rules: stagflation → wait; active hedge or reflation → long, strong if bias is at least +3, otherwise ordinary; cautious risk-off → cautious long; risk-on → no long; mixed → strong long at bias ≥+8, ordinary long at ≥+3, stay out/short bias at ≤−8, no long at ≤−3, otherwise wait. Separately, label timeframe alignment long if all three timeframe biases are ≥+3, short if all are ≤−3, neutral if all lie between −3 and +3, and conflict otherwise. There is no explicit exit rule.
