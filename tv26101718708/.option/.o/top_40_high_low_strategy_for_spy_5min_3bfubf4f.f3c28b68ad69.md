For the traded instrument, calculate a breadth index from a fixed basket of related stocks: compare each stock’s current high and low with its respective prior rolling extremes, count stocks making a new high or low, and express new highs as a percentage of stocks making either. Smooth that percentage, then require the breadth index to be rising for a long setup or falling for a short setup. Enter long when that breadth direction agrees with a rising MACD histogram and the traded instrument’s regression slope is positive, above a trend-strength threshold, and accelerating. Enter short when breadth direction agrees with a falling MACD histogram and the regression slope is negative, beyond the threshold, and accelerating downward. Avoid entries near the breadth index’s extremes and during the configured intraday pause. While a position is profitable, an optional early exit closes a long if breadth reverses down after an extreme high, or a short if it reverses up after an extreme low; it may also close on an opposite setup. Otherwise manage exits using volatility-based stops and targets, with optional trailing stops and end-of-day exits.
