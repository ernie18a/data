For each futures market, use weekly COT positions for the selected participant and the opposite participant. At each 13-, 26-, and 52-report window, rank the selected participant’s net-position spread against the opposite participant within that window on a 0–100 scale (use 50 if the range is flat). For Commercial mode, reverse the spread orientation. Weight the three readings 20%, 30%, and 50%; lower weighted readings are bullish and higher readings bearish. If any reading is unavailable, issue no signal. Otherwise, apply these rules in order: LONG BUILD if the bullish-turn rule holds and selected-participant net position rose in the latest report; BULL TURN if the bullish-turn rule holds; BUY if all three readings are at most 10, 10, and 15 respectively, or the weighted reading is at most 25 and the 52-report reading at most 30; SHORT BUILD if the bearish-turn rule holds and net position fell; BEAR TURN if the bearish-turn rule holds; TOP RISK if all three readings are at least 90, 90, and 85 respectively, or the weighted reading is at least 75 and the 52-report reading at least 70. A bullish turn requires the bullish setup, the 13-report reading to rise from the prior report, and the 26-report reading not to fall; a bearish turn requires the bearish setup, the 13-report reading to fall, and the 26-report reading not to rise. Otherwise, if the weighted reading is below 40, classify LONG UNWIND when net position fell and LONG BIAS otherwise; if above 60, classify SHORT COVER when net position rose and SHORT BIAS otherwise; otherwise classify NEUTRAL. These are COT positioning labels; the code defines no exit decision.
