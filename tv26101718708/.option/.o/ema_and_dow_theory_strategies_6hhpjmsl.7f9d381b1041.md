Use the traded instrument’s fast and slow EMAs and the same-timeframe EMAs of an external market index. A bullish EMA signal occurs when the traded instrument’s fast EMA is above its slow EMA, or when they are equal and the index’s fast EMA is below its slow EMA. A bearish EMA signal occurs when the traded instrument’s fast EMA is below its slow EMA. Track confirmed swing highs and lows on the traded instrument: its trend turns bullish when price closes above the latest swing high and bearish when it closes below the latest swing low. Enter long when flat if the bullish EMA signal, bullish swing trend, and an RSI below its overbought threshold agree; enter short when flat if the bearish EMA signal, bearish swing trend, and an RSI above its oversold threshold agree. Close a position when the swing trend turns against it. Also close on a configured loss threshold when the opposing EMA signal is absent. When profitable, reduce a long position if the combined EMA state changes from both instruments bullish; reduce a short if it changes from both bearish, provided the opposing EMA signal is absent. An opposing eligible entry signal closes the current position before opening the reverse position when the swing trend and RSI filters agree.
