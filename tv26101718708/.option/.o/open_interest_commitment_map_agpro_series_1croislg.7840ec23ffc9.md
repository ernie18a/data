Using open interest data, or a volume-based proxy if unavailable, measure its recent percentage change and normalize it against its historical baseline. Treat unusually strong positive change as expansion and unusually strong negative change as contraction. A qualifying commitment score combines normalized open-interest change, its magnitude, price movement in ATR units, persistence of the raw condition, and volume percentile. Classify as long when open interest expands, price rises enough, price is accepted above the slower trend average, the faster average is above the slower one, and the score clears its qualifying threshold. Classify as short when open interest expands, price falls enough, price is accepted below the slower average, the faster average is below the slower one, and the score qualifies. Classify as unwind when open interest contracts significantly and price moves enough; classify as absorption when open interest expands while price movement stays compressed. These latter states do not specify an exit rule.
