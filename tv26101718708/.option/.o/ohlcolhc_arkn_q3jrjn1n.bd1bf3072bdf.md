For the traded instrument, compare each cycle’s highs and lows with those of its selected correlated instruments. A bearish divergence occurs when the traded instrument’s high and a comparator’s high move in opposite directions across consecutive cycles; a bullish divergence occurs when their lows move in opposite directions. For negatively correlated pairs, treat matching movements as divergence instead. Apply the same comparisons to the highest and lowest candle body prices for hidden divergences. On a bearish divergence, decide short; on a bullish divergence, decide long. Check the configured nested intraday, daily, weekly, and monthly cycles when available. The indicator defines no exit condition.
