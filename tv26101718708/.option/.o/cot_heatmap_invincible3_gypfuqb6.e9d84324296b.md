Each week, calculate a positioning score for each selected market from five percentile readings: the 13-, 26-, and 52-week speculative-versus-commercial positioning difference, plus each participant group's 52- and 156-week net-positioning range. Weight these readings 10%, 15%, 25%, 25%, and 25%. For commercial positioning, use each reading directly; for speculative positioning, invert each reading (100 minus the reading) to treat low speculative positioning as bullish and high positioning as bearish. A weighted score of at least 75 is a bullish extreme; at most 25 is a bearish extreme. On the first week the score enters the bullish extreme, signal a long setup; on the first week it enters the bearish extreme, signal exit of longs / top risk. Within an extreme, a move back from the prior week's score by more than 2 points is a turn: in the bullish zone, rising participant net positioning in the market-bias direction signals long build, otherwise bullish turn; in the bearish zone, falling positioning in the market-bias direction signals short build, otherwise bearish turn. Outside those transition cases, scores at least 60 are long-biased and scores at most 40 are short-biased: net positioning increasing in the market-bias direction signals build, decreasing signals unwind or cover, and missing flow leaves the bias unchanged. Scores between 40 and 60 are neutral. For speculative positioning, increasing speculative net positioning counts as flow against the market-bias direction; for commercial positioning it counts as flow with it. These are weekly positioning biases based on external COT data, not price triggers.
