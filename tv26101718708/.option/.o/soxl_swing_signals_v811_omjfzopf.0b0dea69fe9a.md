For the traded semiconductor ETF, generate a long signal when any configured oversold or pullback setup fires and at least three of seven supporting factors agree. The setups combine lower-band or lower-channel positioning, weak short-term momentum, price below its recent average, a rising longer-term trend with a pullback, improving momentum, and capitulation-style declines. External volatility data supports several setups and contributes to the confirmation score: elevated volatility relative to its recent average, high volatility, or volatility turning down from a recent peak can qualify or strengthen a long. Enforce a per-direction cooldown.

Optional shorts are disabled by default. If enabled, legacy short setups use broad-market volatility alongside ETF extension, overbought momentum, or rising volatility while price remains extended. A separate bear-cycle short requires the underlying semiconductor index’s long-term average to be declining, plus the ETF below its medium-term average and making a fresh short-term closing low, without already being deeply oversold. A separate top-fade short requires the ETF near its upper volatility band, overbought, substantially above its short-term average, and closing down on a weak or stalling day. All short setups are blocked during a recent volatility-crash regime and observe the short cooldown. The underlying index’s ranging distribution classification and other displayed market context do not independently trigger trades.
