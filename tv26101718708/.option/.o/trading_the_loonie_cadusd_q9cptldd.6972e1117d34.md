Use crude-oil data as the external market input and compare its volatility-normalized position around its moving average with the traded currency’s position; track the relative divergence and their correlation. Enter long when that divergence has recently been strongly positive and is falling, the currency is rising, oil’s medium-term average is rising, and correlation is not strongly negative. Enter short when divergence has recently been strongly negative and is rising, the currency is falling, oil’s medium-term average is falling, and correlation is not strongly negative. Exit a long if momentum turns down while the currency is overbought, if strongly negative divergence coincides with a sharp oil decline, or if the currency breaks below recent support while longer-term correlation is negative. Exit a short if momentum turns up while the currency is oversold and oil has rebounded from a recent low, if strongly positive divergence coincides with a sharp oil rise, or if the currency breaks above recent resistance while longer-term correlation is negative.
