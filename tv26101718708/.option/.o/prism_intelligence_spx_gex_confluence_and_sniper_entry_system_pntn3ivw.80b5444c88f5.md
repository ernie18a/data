For the traded instrument, calculate a 12-point market confluence score: regime contributes 2 for constructive, 1 for transitional, otherwise 0; composite score contributes 2 at 10 or above, 1 at 7–9, otherwise 0; add 1 each for positive net dealer gamma, volatility index below its 10-period average below its 20-period average, front volatility below 3-month volatility, volatility-of-volatility below 100, skew below 135, equity put/call above 0.80, institutional positioning z-score above 0.5, long/short volatility barometer below 0.20, volatility-control ratio at or below 1.05, and positive market breadth oscillator. Treat the score as high at 9 or above, medium at 6–8, and low below 6. Define an uptrend as the traded instrument closing above a rising 9-period moving average. When the score is high, the instrument is above the externally supplied gamma-flip level, and the uptrend holds, go long on any of: a close crossing above its 20-period average; a cross back above the gamma-flip level; a bullish bar near the gamma-flip, put-wall, or lower-support level; the score crossing into the high band; or, during bars 3–8 of the session, a close above the 20-period average and open, above the prior close, with the 9-period average rising. Also go long on a cross above the 9-period average under the same high-score, above-flip, uptrend conditions, unless a higher-priority long trigger occurs on that bar. With a medium score, above the flip, and uptrend, mark a lower-conviction long setup on a cross above the 20-period average or gamma-flip level. Exit or flatten on a cross below the gamma-flip level at any score, when the score crosses below 6, or when the instrument crosses below its 20-period average while still above the flip in a high-score regime. No short-entry rule is defined.
