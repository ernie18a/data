For Bitcoin, compare regulated futures with a spot price chosen from major spot venues, and compare a retail perpetual price with that same spot reference. Add bullish weight when futures trade above spot, with more weight for a larger premium; add bearish weight for a discount. Add bullish or bearish weight when the futures premium over spot, adjusted for time to expiry, is respectively discounted or elevated, with a stronger bearish penalty at extreme premiums. Compare the futures-versus-spot spread with the perpetual-versus-spot spread: a sufficiently larger institutional premium adds bullish weight, while a sufficiently larger retail premium adds bearish weight. Use changes in perpetual open interest and price together: rising open interest with rising price, or falling open interest with rising price, adds bullish weight; rising open interest with falling price, or falling open interest with falling price, adds bearish weight. Treat positive funding as bearish crowding, with stronger penalties at more extreme positive funding; treat negative funding as bullish crowding, with stronger rewards at more extreme negative funding. Sum these contributions: a positive total gives a bullish bias, a negative total gives a bearish bias, and zero gives neutral; larger positive or negative totals indicate stronger bias. This is a directional bias output, not an explicit entry or exit rule.
