For the traded instrument, compare its confirmed swing highs and lows with corresponding swings in ES: if it makes a higher high while ES fails to make a higher high, treat that as bearish divergence; if it makes a lower low while ES holds a higher low, treat it as bullish divergence. A confirmed divergence can trigger a reversal entry when its confidence and available target-distance checks pass. The code also uses divergence between the traded instrument and DXY as a confidence boost for reversal signals, and compares its returns with SPY to influence directional and exhaustion confidence. USI:TICK, volatility futures and VIX9D, and XLK provide additional directional or reversal-confidence inputs; they affect whether other setups pass their confidence gates rather than acting as standalone entries. QQQ prices are mapped to traded-instrument levels that can serve as support, resistance, or profit targets. Close a triggered trade when its predefined stop or target is reached.
