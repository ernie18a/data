For the S&P 500 instrument, compare recent percentage returns of a broad set of external ETFs with the S&P 500’s return. Score each as bullish when it shows meaningful relative strength, bearish when it shows meaningful relative weakness, and neutral otherwise. Reverse the sign for volatility, the dollar, and defensive sectors; score volatility by its absolute rise or fall. Score consumer discretionary against consumer staples, and include credit, bonds, crypto, speculative growth, semiconductors, technology, utilities, and health care. Go long when at least eight signals are bullish; go short when at least eight are bearish. With five to seven aligned signals, take the corresponding direction at reduced size; with fewer, stay out of directional trades. Treat volatility rising with credit weakening, bonds strengthening with credit weakening, or semiconductors weakening while technology holds as bearish warnings. Treat defensive staples weakening while discretionary and semiconductors strengthen as bullish confirmation. A discretionary-versus-growth divergence is a caution to reduce exposure.
