Use the instrument’s price and RSI to identify regular bullish divergence (price makes a lower swing low while RSI makes a higher swing low) and regular bearish divergence (price makes a higher swing high while RSI makes a lower swing high). Extend a line between each divergence’s RSI pivots. Go long when RSI crosses above the latest bearish-divergence line; go short when RSI crosses below the latest bullish-divergence line. Also allow a directional signal when RSI breaks out of a channel formed by the latest two parallel divergence lines after remaining within that channel. Apply the selected signal strictness and any enabled filters: volume, EMA trend, RSI zone, higher-timeframe RSI, EMA spacing, key-level proximity, candle confirmation, and recent-volume confluence. A signal may be invalidated if RSI crosses back through its associated line within the configured window; no separate exit decision is generated.
