Use regular-session prices and weekly high/low levels formed from the first trading day of the week; those levels develop during that day and are then fixed for the rest of the week. Track the midpoint of the weekly range and the 50-period simple moving average. Generate entries only during the regular session, subject to enabled trade-family and first-day policy gates, setup freshness, one signal per level interaction, and a same-direction cooldown. A level interaction begins when price enters a proximity band and can re-arm only after leaving a wider release band.

Long reactions: after a recent interaction with the weekly low, go long on either a close crossing above the 50-period average while above the weekly low, or a bullish close above the weekly low while above the average, triggered by a reclaim, a fresh touch, or a still-fresh bounce. The continuation cases are invalid while price has closed at or above the weekly midpoint; this restriction does not apply to the moving-average reversal. Short reactions mirror these rules at the weekly high: go short on a cross below the average while below the weekly high, or a bearish close below the weekly high while below the average after a reclaim, fresh touch, or fresh bounce. Continuation cases are invalid while price has closed at or below the midpoint; the reversal is exempt.

Optional flip trades: a weekly high becomes support after the required consecutive closes above it; a weekly low becomes resistance after the required consecutive closes below it. A close back across the level cancels that flip. After confirmation, go long on a bullish defense or fresh bounce at former resistance turned support, while above the moving average; alternatively, go long on bullish follow-through above that level. Go short on a bearish defense or fresh bounce at former support turned resistance, while below the moving average; alternatively, go short on bearish follow-through below it. A flip setup must still be fresh and may trigger only once per pullback or confirmation. On lower chart timeframes, require more consecutive closes to confirm a flip than on higher timeframes.

When multiple same-direction candidates qualify on one bar, prefer momentum, then flip pullback, then reaction. The indicator defines no executed exit rule: its stop and take-profit levels are projections only.
