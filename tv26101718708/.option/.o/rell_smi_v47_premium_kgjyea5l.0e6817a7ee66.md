For the traded instrument, assess price structure, liquidity, zones, trend, momentum, volume, volatility, session timing, and higher-timeframe context. Build bullish and bearish confluence scores: credit each side for aligned structure breaks or reversals, liquidity sweeps, active fair-value gaps or order-block retests, trend and higher-timeframe alignment, premium/discount location, breakouts, daily-level position, session participation, relative volume, volatility regime, and supporting higher-timeframe liquidity, structure, and gap context. Add momentum, directional trend strength, VWAP position, and candle-based buying or selling pressure to the stronger side. Use a market- and regime-adjusted minimum score; a directional bias requires a clear score advantage, and a long or short candidate requires its side to exceed both that threshold and the opposing score. Prefer, in order, a confirmed demand or supply reaction with matching structure; a breaker retest; a volume-confirmed support/resistance break; a qualifying instrument-specific cycle reversal; a higher-timeframe liquidity sweep with local structure confirmation; a higher-timeframe gap retest; a local sweep reversal; a strong gap retest; a breakout retest with compatible trend context; or trend continuation from discount in an uptrend or premium in a downtrend. Treat these as setups requiring confirmation, not automatic entries. Stand aside during time blackouts, quiet compressed conditions with weak participation, opposing trap signals, insufficient confluence, or unresolved conflict; require the workflow checklist to be substantially aligned before marking a setup ready. Governance can further block trading for lockout, unapproved research, or extreme tail risk, and reduce risk for elevated tail risk, drawdown recovery, context conflict, or excessive correlation. No explicit position exit rule is defined.
