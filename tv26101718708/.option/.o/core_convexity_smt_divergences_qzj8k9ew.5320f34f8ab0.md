Compare the instrument’s current high or low with each selected correlated instrument. A bearish signal occurs when the instrument makes a new high but a correlated instrument does not confirm it, or their most recent qualifying prior highs occur at different times; a bullish signal occurs when the instrument makes a new low but a correlated instrument does not confirm it, or their most recent qualifying prior lows occur at different times. Optionally also signal when one instrument makes a new structural extreme that the other fails to match. Map bearish signals to short and bullish signals to long. Exit the corresponding signal when price breaks its divergence level in the invalidating direction on both the instrument and its paired reference; if global confirmation is selected, require the other tracked reference to break its corresponding level too.
