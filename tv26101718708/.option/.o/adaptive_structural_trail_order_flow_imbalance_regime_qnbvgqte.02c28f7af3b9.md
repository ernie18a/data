Build participation-based synthetic bars by accumulating signed volume until it reaches a volatility-scaled significance threshold. Detect bullish or bearish gaps across three completed synthetic bars when the middle bar has a sufficiently large directional-volume impulse and the gap is large relative to volatility. Track each level’s strength: decay it on touches and over time, kill it when price surges through with confirming delta, and promote it to a strong breaker when price fully reclaims it with opposing delta indicating absorption. Classify the market as uptrend, downtrend, range, transition, or high volatility using directional efficiency and an unusually large recent move. In a trend only, select the strongest surviving level above the minimum strength that is nearest below price for an uptrend or nearest above price for a downtrend; use it as the trail. Combine that level’s strength, trend state, agreement between trend direction and cumulative-delta slope, and a penalty for high flow toxicity into a confidence score. Tilt confidence using an external implied-volatility index: falling volatility favors longs and rising volatility favors shorts. If an external directional-bias input is enabled, also use its alignment in confidence and require it not to oppose the entry. On a completed synthetic bar, enter long when the regime is an uptrend, a valid trail exists, confidence clears its threshold, cumulative-delta slope is positive, and any enabled external bias permits the long; enter short under the symmetric downtrend and negative-slope conditions. While long, ratchet the stop upward to the higher of its prior value and the current trail; exit if price closes below the stop or the regime ceases to be an uptrend. While short, ratchet the stop downward to the lower of its prior value and the current trail; exit if price closes above the stop or the regime ceases to be a downtrend. When flat, range, transition, and high-volatility regimes do not generate entries.
