For the traded index future, build directional evidence from cross-market data: favor longs when its recent return exceeds the broad-market ETF’s, NYSE breadth is positive, volatility is easing, and its swing structure is stronger than the related equity future’s; favor shorts when these relationships reverse. Treat a higher high in the traded future without a confirming higher high in the related future as bearish divergence, and a lower low without a confirming lower low as bullish divergence. Combine these signals with the strategy’s other directional evidence; block long entries when the combined score is sufficiently bearish and block shorts when it is sufficiently bullish. Use round-number levels derived from the tracking ETF as support and resistance: they can affect the nearest target, and available distance to support contributes to the short-entry quality gate. External-market inputs affect entry permission and target placement; smart pattern exits are disabled, while trades close at their stop or target.
