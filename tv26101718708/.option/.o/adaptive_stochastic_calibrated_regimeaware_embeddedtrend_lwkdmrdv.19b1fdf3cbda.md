Classify the traded instrument as trending when trend strength and directional efficiency are both high; otherwise treat it as ranging, except that a volatility-clustered nontrend is classified as volatile. In a range, signal long when the oscillator has been below its instrument-specific oversold percentile and crosses upward through its signal line; signal short when it has been above its overbought percentile and crosses downward. Suppress fades when the oscillator has remained beyond the corresponding extreme long enough to indicate an embedded trend. In a trend, signal with the trend when the oscillator’s adaptive trailing trend line crosses in that direction, or when the oscillator turns that way on a pullback while still aligned with the trail. Require cycle timing for fades, adequate signal-to-noise, and—when enabled—higher-timeframe agreement for trend-following signals. An optional volatility-index spike vetoes fades. Optional cross-asset momentum contributes to conviction and vetoes a trend-following signal when it moves against the proposed direction. Combine setup quality factors into a conviction score and act only when it meets the configured minimum and no veto applies. The indicator defines no exit rule.
