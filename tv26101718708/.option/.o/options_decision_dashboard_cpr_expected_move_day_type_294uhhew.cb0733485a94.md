On intraday data, classify the prior-session pivot range by its width percentile versus its own recent history: unusually narrow favors a trend; unusually wide favors a range. Determine direction from current price above or below the pivot range; inside it has no direction. Treat implied volatility as expensive when its premium over realized volatility is rich or its historical rank is high. If data is warming or the chart is not intraday, do not trade. A wide-range day, or a neutral day type without direction, favors selling premium only when volatility is expensive; otherwise stay out. On a narrow, directional trend day, buy calls above the range or puts below it only when volatility is not expensive, expiry is not too near, and the first floor-pivot target is at least one expected move away from the session open. If the target is closer than the expected move, favor selling premium. Expensive volatility or near expiry blocks the directional option buy. Override every verdict to stay out when tail risk is flagged by extreme implied-volatility rank or a realized-volatility spike.
