For the traded instrument, estimate its equilibrium price and standardized deviation, and consider a mean-reversion direction only when the price series passes mean-reversion and stationarity checks: short when materially above equilibrium, long when materially below. Form supporting or opposing signals from available external data: futures versus spot fair value, option skew or put-call ratio/gamma positioning, and constituent-stock residual dislocations. Combine these with the core signal using a weighted directional score, adjusted for volatility/trend regime, constituent correlation, volatility risk premium, cross-asset stress, and expiry pinning. Choose a horizon when the score or directional agreement is sufficient; require the expected move back toward equilibrium to exceed estimated trading costs. Suppress signals during restricted session periods and extreme high-trend/spike regimes. Output long or short according to the resulting score; otherwise remain neutral. There is no separate exit signal.
