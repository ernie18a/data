Trade the Nasdaq instrument only during the New York morning session, or the afternoon session if enabled, and only in the direction allowed by a manually selected bullish, bearish, or mixed bias. Use the prior day's high and low to define a midpoint and no-trade zone; consider shorts only above the upper zone and longs only below the lower zone. A short setup requires price to sweep above the higher of the prior-day high and latest confirmed swing high, then show strong bearish displacement, close below a confirmed swing low, and form a sufficiently large bearish fair value gap, with these events occurring close together. A long setup mirrors this: sweep below the lower of the prior-day low and latest confirmed swing low, strong bullish displacement, close above a confirmed swing high, and form a sufficiently large bullish fair value gap. Require confirmation from the S&P 500: bearish confirmation is either below its own moving average or bearish divergence when Nasdaq makes a new lookback high and the S&P 500 does not; bullish confirmation is either above its moving average or bullish divergence when Nasdaq makes a new lookback low and the S&P 500 does not. Confirmation strictness may be configured to require the moving-average condition, accept either condition, or accept the moving-average condition unless it contradicts the trade. After a setup forms, enter only when price returns to its fair value gap, confirmation still holds, the position is flat, the daily trade limit is not reached, and the stop distance from the entry price to beyond the sweep extreme is positive and within the allowed maximum. Place the initial stop beyond the sweep extreme. Set a fixed profit target at a configurable risk multiple; once price reaches a configurable favorable risk multiple, move the stop to entry. Otherwise, do not enter.
