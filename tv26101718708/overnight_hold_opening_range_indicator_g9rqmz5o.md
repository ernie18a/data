## Description

Overnight Hold / Opening Range Indicator

[b]Overnight Hold / Opening Range Indicator[/b] is designed to help traders identify stocks and ETFs that finished the day with enough directional quality to be used in two different ways. At its core, this indicator is built to help a trader judge the quality of the close, not just the fact that price moved during the day. A stock can be active, volatile, and attractive intraday, but still finish the session with weak structure. It can break a level, trend for a while, and still fade into the close. This indicator is designed to help separate those weaker finishes from the names that actually closed with meaningful bullish or bearish quality.

The reason that matters is because the last part of the session often tells the real story. Late-day price action can reveal whether buyers or sellers stayed in control, whether the move held together into the bell, and whether the stock finished with enough strength, weakness, and structure to matter going into the next session. This indicator is built to turn that late-day read into a more organized decision-making process.

[b]Important chart setup[/b]

For the clearest and most consistent read, this indicator should be viewed on a 5-minute chart with[b] REGULAR TRADING HOURS[/b] enabled. The final decision is made at [b]3:55 PM ET[/b], so using a 5-minute chart helps the trader see the intended decision window clearly. Using [b]REGULAR TRADING HOURS[/b] also keeps the read focused on the normal session close instead of allowing after-hours price action to distract from the intended setup.

That does not mean the indicator is trying to trade the 5-minute trend by itself. The underlying read is built from daily conditions, but the 5-minute chart is the best way to monitor the decision at 3:55 PM ET and use the indicator the way it was intended.

[b]Purpose 1: Overnight Hold[/b]

The first use is to help a trader decide whether a position may be worth acquiring late in the session and holding overnight. The goal is to focus on names that did not simply move during the day, but actually closed with strength or weakness that may carry into the next session. In this context, the indicator helps a trader ask a more important question than, “Was this stock up or down today?” It helps ask, “Did this stock finish the day in a way that suggests the move may continue tomorrow?”

For overnight trading, that distinction matters. A stock may rally intraday and still fade into the close. Another may spend most of the day choppy, then finish with real directional control in the final stretch. This indicator is meant to help identify the names that actually finished well, because the close often matters more than the path the stock took to get there.

[b]Purpose 2: Next-Day Opening Range Opportunity[/b]

The second use is to help a trader build a watchlist for the following morning. A strong reading can highlight names that may be worth trading after the first 15 minutes of the new session, once the Opening Range Breakout (ORB) is established. In this case, the indicator is not being used to hold a position overnight. Instead, it is being used to identify stocks that may have the right daily foundation for a next-day continuation trade after the market opens.

This is important because many opening range trades work best when the prior day already showed strong directional structure. If a stock finished with trend alignment, a meaningful break of recent levels, a strong close, and enough expansion, it may deserve more attention the next morning once the opening range forms and the market confirms direction. In that way, the indicator helps traders do part of their watchlist filtering in advance.

In other words, this indicator is built to answer one key question:

Did this stock finish the day with enough bullish or bearish quality to either justify an overnight position or make it a strong candidate for a trade after the 15-minute ORB the next morning?

[b]Why this indicator matters[/b]

A stock can look strong or weak during the day and still fail as an overnight hold or as a next-day opening trade. Many names move intraday but do not finish with the kind of structure that leads to follow-through. This indicator is designed to help filter that out.

It does that by looking at whether the stock finished the day with:

directional control
trend alignment
a meaningful break of the previous day’s range
a strong close within the day’s range
enough expansion to show the move had real force behind it

That matters because both overnight holds and next-day ORB trades work best when the prior session closes with quality, not just activity.

[b]How to use it[/b]

This indicator should be read near the end of the trading day. For best results, use it on a 5-minute chart with REGULAR TRADING HOURS enabled and check the final reading at 3:55 PM ET. That is the intended decision point. It allows the trader to judge the stock based on how it is actually finishing the session, not how it looked earlier in the day or after the close.

[b]Use #1: For an overnight position[/b]

If the indicator shows a strong bullish or bearish reading into the close, a trader can use that information to decide whether the stock may be worth entering before the close and carrying overnight.

This use is for traders asking:

Is this stock finishing strong enough to hold?
Is the trend aligned?
Did it close with real control?
Is there a better chance of continuation tomorrow?

[b]Use #2: For a trade after the 15-minute ORB[/b]

A trader may also choose not to hold overnight and instead use the reading to prepare for the next session.

In that case, the indicator helps identify names that may be worth watching the next morning for:

an opening drive
a break of the 15-minute range
a pullback after the ORB
a continuation setup after the market establishes direction

A strong bullish reading can support a bullish ORB watchlist.
A strong bearish reading can support a bearish ORB watchlist.

[b]What the indicator measures[/b]

The reading is built from a group of daily factors that work together to show how well the stock actually finished the session. Instead of focusing on one signal by itself, the indicator looks at several pieces of information that help answer a bigger question:

Did the stock end the day with enough structure, trend alignment, and closing strength to matter?

Each part of the reading has a job.

Daily candle direction

This looks at whether the stock closed the day bullish or bearish.

That matters because it shows who had control of the session by the close. If the stock finishes green, buyers had the upper hand. If it finishes red, sellers had the upper hand. This is the most basic layer of directional information, but it is still important because overnight holds and next-day continuation ideas usually start with understanding which side won the day.

Price vs fast EMA

This looks at whether the stock is closing above or below the fast EMA.

That matters because it helps show whether price is ending the day above or below short-term value. A close above the fast EMA suggests short-term strength. A close below it suggests short-term weakness. This helps the trader see whether the stock is finishing the day in a healthy position relative to recent price action, not just whether it happened to be green or red.

Fast EMA vs slow EMA

This compares the fast EMA to the slow EMA.

That matters because it adds a broader trend filter to the reading. A bullish close is more meaningful when the fast EMA is already above the slow EMA, and a bearish close is more meaningful when the fast EMA is below the slow EMA. This keeps the indicator from treating every directional day the same and helps align the late-day read with the larger daily trend.

Break of previous day high or low

This checks whether the stock closed above the previous day’s high or below the previous day’s low.

That matters because it shows whether price actually expanded beyond an important recent reference point. A stock that finishes beyond the prior day’s range has done more than just move around intraday. It has pushed through a level that traders commonly watch, which can make the move more meaningful for both overnight continuation and next-day opening setups.

Close Location Value (CLV)

This measures where the stock closed within the day’s total range.

That matters because the close often says a lot about who stayed in control when the session was ending. A close near the high of the day usually shows buyers held control into the bell. A close near the low usually shows sellers stayed in control into the bell. This is especially important for overnight trades because a strong close often matters more than a stock that simply moved earlier in the day and then faded before the session ended.

Range expansion vs ATR

This compares the day’s range to ATR.

That matters because not every move has enough size to matter. A stock can look good visually, but if the range is too small relative to its normal movement, the session may not have had enough energy behind it. This part of the reading helps separate a real expansion day from a weaker move that may not have much follow-through potential.

Score-based confirmation

This combines the main conditions into a score and only produces stronger readings when enough of them line up together.

That matters because the best setups usually are not built on one signal alone. A stock may have one attractive feature, but still be missing trend alignment, close quality, or meaningful expansion. By requiring multiple conditions to agree with each other, the indicator becomes more selective and does a better job filtering out weaker end-of-day setups.

[b]Why all of this matters together[/b]

Each individual factor tells part of the story, but the real value comes from how they work together.

A stock may close green, but still be below important trend value.
A stock may break the previous day’s high, but fail to close strongly.
A stock may close near the highs, but with very little expansion.

Taken one at a time, those signals can be misleading. When they begin to line up together, the reading becomes more meaningful. That is why the indicator focuses on combined daily quality rather than one isolated signal.

[b]How to interpret the readings[/b]

TAKE CALLS

The stock finished the day with strong bullish qualities. This can make it a candidate for:

an overnight long position
a next-day bullish ORB watchlist
a continuation trade after the first 15 minutes if the next session confirms the idea
TAKE PUTS

The stock finished the day with strong bearish qualities. This can make it a candidate for:

an overnight bearish position
a next-day bearish ORB watchlist
a continuation trade after the first 15 minutes if the next session confirms the idea
CAUTION

There is some directional quality, but not enough full confirmation. This may still be worth monitoring, but it is not a top-tier setup.

PASS

There is no clear edge. This helps the trader avoid forcing low-quality overnight holds or weak next-day watchlist names.

WAIT

The session has not yet reached the final decision window. Before then, the indicator is still waiting for the proper late-day moment to make its final read. For the cleanest use, wait until the 3:55 PM ET bar on a 5-minute chart with REGULAR TRADING HOURS enabled.

[b]What gives it an edge[/b]

The edge comes from selectivity.

A lot of indicators become less useful because they react to just one thing. One green candle, one breakout, one moving average cross, or one push into the close can make a chart look attractive even when the overall setup is still incomplete. This indicator is different because it does not treat any single piece of information as enough on its own. Instead, it combines trend, price location, previous-day range breaks, close quality, and range expansion into one late-day decision. That forces the trader to focus on names that are not just moving, but are finishing the day with stronger overall structure.

That matters because late in the session, plenty of stocks can appear strong for the wrong reasons. Some are simply bouncing inside a weak trend. Some are closing green but still below important value areas. Some break a prior level but do not close with conviction. Others close near the highs or lows, but the move itself is too small to suggest real expansion. By requiring several factors to align at the same time, this indicator helps filter out those lower-quality names and narrow attention to the ones showing stronger confirmation.

That selectivity is important for both uses of the indicator.

For overnight holds, it helps reduce the tendency to carry weak names into the close just because they looked good intraday. A stock may have had a decent move during the session, but if it did not finish with trend alignment, a meaningful level break, a strong close within the range, and enough expansion, the odds of overnight follow-through may not be as strong. This indicator helps the trader separate a stock that merely moved from a stock that actually finished with the kind of quality that may deserve overnight risk.

For next-day ORB trades, it helps identify stocks that may already have the right daily foundation for morning continuation. When a stock finishes the session with stronger bullish or bearish structure, it can become a better candidate for the next day’s first real directional move after the opening range is established. In that sense, the indicator is not trying to replace the ORB setup itself. It is helping the trader do the filtering work in advance, so the next morning’s watchlist starts with names that already showed higher-quality closing conditions.

The real advantage is that it improves decision quality. Instead of asking, “Did this stock move today?” the trader is asking better questions:

Did it close in the direction of control?
Was it aligned with the short-term and broader daily trend?
Did it break a meaningful prior-day level?
Did it close in the right part of the day’s range?
Was the move large enough to matter?

When those answers begin to line up together, the setup becomes more meaningful. That is where the edge comes from. Not from prediction, but from better selection.

Best way to think about it

Think of this indicator as a quality-control filter.

It is not meant to replace your entries, exits, or risk management. It is meant to help answer:

“Is this actually a strong overnight or next-open candidate, or does it only look good at first glance?”

The more disciplined you are about only acting on stronger readings, the more useful it becomes.

## Source Code

````pine
//@version=5
indicator("Overnight Scanner Visual (No Column Version)", overlay=true, max_labels_count=500)

//========================
// Inputs
//========================
fastLen        = input.int(20, "Fast EMA Length")
slowLen        = input.int(50, "Slow EMA Length")
atrLen         = input.int(14, "ATR Length")

bullClvStrong  = input.float(0.70, "Bull Strong CLV Threshold", step=0.01)
bearClvStrong  = input.float(0.30, "Bear Strong CLV Threshold", step=0.01)

rangeExpMult   = input.float(1.00, "Range Expansion vs ATR", step=0.05)

decisionHour   = input.int(15, "Decision Hour (ET)", minval=0, maxval=23)
decisionMinute = input.int(55, "Decision Minute (ET)", minval=0, maxval=59)

//========================
// Time Logic
//========================
nyHour   = hour(time, "America/New_York")
nyMinute = minute(time, "America/New_York")

afterDecisionTime = (nyHour > decisionHour) or (nyHour == decisionHour and nyMinute >= decisionMinute)

//========================
// Daily Data
//========================
dOpen = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", open, barmerge.gaps_off, barmerge.lookahead_off)
dHigh = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", high, barmerge.gaps_off, barmerge.lookahead_off)
dLow = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", low, barmerge.gaps_off, barmerge.lookahead_off)
dClose = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", close, barmerge.gaps_off, barmerge.lookahead_off)

pdHigh = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", high[1], barmerge.gaps_off, barmerge.lookahead_off)
pdLow = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", low[1], barmerge.gaps_off, barmerge.lookahead_off)

dEmaFast = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", ta.ema(close, fastLen), barmerge.gaps_off, barmerge.lookahead_off)
dEmaSlow = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", ta.ema(close, slowLen), barmerge.gaps_off, barmerge.lookahead_off)
dATR = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", ta.atr(atrLen), barmerge.gaps_off, barmerge.lookahead_off)

//========================
// Core Calculations
//========================
dayRange = math.max(dHigh - dLow, syminfo.mintick)
clv = (dClose - dLow) / dayRange

bullCandle = dClose > dOpen
bearCandle = dClose < dOpen

trendBull = dEmaFast > dEmaSlow
trendBear = dEmaFast < dEmaSlow

aboveFast = dClose > dEmaFast
belowFast = dClose < dEmaFast

breakHigh = dClose > pdHigh
breakLow = dClose < pdLow

rangeExpansion = dayRange >= dATR * rangeExpMult

//========================
// Scores
//========================
bullScore = 0
bullScore += bullCandle ? 1 : 0
bullScore += aboveFast ? 1 : 0
bullScore += trendBull ? 1 : 0
bullScore += breakHigh ? 1 : 0

bearScore = 0
bearScore += bearCandle ? 1 : 0
bearScore += belowFast ? 1 : 0
bearScore += trendBear ? 1 : 0
bearScore += breakLow ? 1 : 0

//========================
// CLV
//========================
bullClvStrongRead = clv >= bullClvStrong
bearClvStrongRead = clv <= bearClvStrong

//========================
// Strong / Weak 3
//========================
bullStrong3 = bullScore == 3 and bullClvStrongRead and bullScore > bearScore
bullWeak3 = bullScore == 3 and not bullClvStrongRead and bullScore > bearScore

bearStrong3 = bearScore == 3 and bearClvStrongRead and bearScore > bullScore
bearWeak3 = bearScore == 3 and not bearClvStrongRead and bearScore > bullScore

exact3Exists = (bullScore == 3 and bullScore > bearScore) or (bearScore == 3 and bearScore > bullScore)

//========================
// Decision
//========================
string rawDecision = ""

if bullStrong3 and rangeExpansion
    rawDecision := "TAKE CALLS"
else if bearStrong3 and rangeExpansion
    rawDecision := "TAKE PUTS"
else if bullStrong3 and not rangeExpansion
    rawDecision := "CAUTION"
else if bearStrong3 and not rangeExpansion
    rawDecision := "CAUTION"
else if bullWeak3 or bearWeak3
    rawDecision := "CAUTION"
else if not exact3Exists
    rawDecision := "PASS (Not Exact 3)"
else
    rawDecision := "PASS (No Clear Edge)"

string decision = afterDecisionTime ? rawDecision : "WAIT"

//========================
// Colors
//========================
takeCallsColor = color.rgb(0, 0, 139)      // Dark Blue
takePutsColor = color.rgb(255, 0, 0)       // Bright Red
cautionColor = color.rgb(255, 255, 0)      // Bright Yellow
passColor = color.rgb(0, 0, 0)             // Black
waitColor = color.rgb(0, 191, 255)         // Light Blue

bgColor = passColor

if decision == "TAKE CALLS"
    bgColor := takeCallsColor
else if decision == "TAKE PUTS"
    bgColor := takePutsColor
else if decision == "CAUTION"
    bgColor := cautionColor
else if decision == "WAIT"
    bgColor := waitColor
else
    bgColor := passColor

labelTextColor = decision == "CAUTION" ? color.black : color.white

//========================
// Background
//========================
bgcolor(color.new(bgColor, 80))

//========================
// Big Label
//========================
var label bigLabel = na

if barstate.islast
    label.delete(bigLabel)
    bigLabel := label.new(
         bar_index,
         high,
         decision,
         style = label.style_label_center,
         size = size.huge,
         color = bgColor,
         textcolor = labelTextColor
     )
````
