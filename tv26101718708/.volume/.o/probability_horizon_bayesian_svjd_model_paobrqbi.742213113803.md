Calculation: Five directional model probabilities = each model maps directional bias and strength to 0.5 ± 0.10 × strength (strength capped at 1): short-term KAMA trend uses the normalized KAMA slope; mean-reversion uses the z-score of close minus KAMA, bullish below the negative threshold and bearish above the positive threshold; Haar trend and Kalman-smoothed trend use their normalized slopes; the Hamilton model uses Bayesian bull, bear, and range probabilities from Gaussian return likelihoods, with P(up) = 0.5 + 0.10 × (P(bull) − P(bear)).
Calculation: Ensemble P(up) = weighted average of the five model probabilities. Start with equal weights; after each evaluation horizon, update each weight by multiplying it by exp(−learning rate × log loss against whether close rose), apply the minimum weight floor, then normalize weights to sum to 1.
Calculation: Crisis-adjusted P(up) = 0.5 + (ensemble P(up) − 0.5) × (1 − crisis adjustment); crisis adjustment is zero unless Hawkes intensity divided by its rolling event-rate baseline exceeds the warning multiple, then it is the excess over that multiple divided by the multiple, capped at 0.5. Hawkes intensity decays exponentially and increases by the excitation amount on returns exceeding twice realized volatility.
Calculation: Final P(up) = 0.5 + (crisis-adjusted P(up) − 0.5) × calibration factor. For the matching probability-distance bin, once at least 30 matured predictions exist, the factor is the lesser of 1 and actual hit rate divided by the bin midpoint when hit rate is below that midpoint; otherwise it is 1. Predictions mature after the calibration evaluation horizon and are correct when the predicted direction matches whether close rose.
Long: Final P(up) ≥ bullish threshold AND (Final P(up) > 0.505 OR prior bar's Final P(up) < bullish threshold)
Short: Final P(up) ≤ 1 − bullish threshold AND (Final P(up) < 0.495 OR prior bar's Final P(up) > 1 − bullish threshold)
