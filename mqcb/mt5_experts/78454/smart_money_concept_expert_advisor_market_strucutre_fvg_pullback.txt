This Expert Advisor trades fair value gaps in the direction of market structure, the way
Smart Money Concepts
describes it. It is the third step of a series that has been built one piece at a time: In previous shared codes, the
SMC Pullback Indicator
decided which highs and lows matter, the
SMC Market Structure indicator
turned those swings into a trend with BoS, CHoCH, IDM and liquidity sweeps, and this EA trades the result.
The structure engine is not reimplemented here. Both classes are included from
SmcMarketStructure.mqh
, the same code the indicator draws from, so what the EA acts on and what you see on the chart cannot disagree.
Read this before anything else: this EA is not a profitable system, and it is not presented as one.
Its purpose is to show
how
market structure and fair value gaps can be wired into a working Expert Advisor — how a BoS arms a setup, how an IDM confirms a leg, how gaps are found and filtered, how a structural stop turns into a position size. It is a teaching skeleton and a starting point, not a finished strategy.
A robust, genuinely profitable SMC strategy needs far more than what is here:
multi-timeframe
context so the lower timeframe is read in the direction of the higher one,
pattern detection
beyond a single gap type, proper
risk and money management
across trades rather than one fixed percent,
order blocks
and
order flow
, and
entry rules
considerably more selective than a first touch. Each of those is a project in itself.
What this code gives you is a clean, readable base with the structure engine already solved, so you can bolt your own proven ideas on top instead of starting from an empty file. Treat every default as a placeholder and test everything yourself.

What counts as a fair value gap
A gap is a three-candle pattern. The middle candle travels far enough that the third candle never trades back to the first, leaving a band of price that was skipped:
Bullish gap: the
low of candle 3
is above the
high of candle 1
. Bearish gap: the
high of candle 3
is below the
low of candle 1
. The band between those two levels is the gap.
Finding gaps is easy; the useful part is deciding which ones matter. Three filters apply, and a gap has to pass all of them:
Position.
It must sit between the structure low and the bar that broke the level. Gaps older than the leg belong to a move that is already finished.
Side.
It must lie entirely below the level the BoS broke. A gap above that level is not a discount, it is chasing.
Size.
It must be at least the minimum width you set, in points or pips. This is the only noise filter in the strategy, and on a low timeframe it is the one that matters.
Each surviving gap is drawn as a box labelled FVG, and the box
extends to the right with every new candle
until price touches it. Once touched it stops growing, which leaves a visible record of how long the gap stood untested.
How deep price has to come back
A gap right under the high is a weak place to buy. Price has barely corrected, the move has not been tested, and the stop has to sit a long way off. The depth filter exists to refuse exactly that.
The leg is
A to B
. Depth is measured from B back towards A, so 0% is the confirmed high and 100% is the structure low itself. Set the minimum to 50% and price must return at least to the midpoint of the leg before any gap in it may be traded.
In the figure the touch arrives at 73% of the leg, past the 50% threshold, so it is allowed. Had the gap sat in the shaded band near B it would have been refused no matter how clean it looked. Setting the input to 0 turns the filter off.
Touching a gap and
using
a gap are not the same thing. A gap entered at its upper edge may still be too shallow, and price can come deeper into the
same
gap a few candles later. So a touched gap stops extending but stays live, and is reconsidered on every tick until it either trades or is traded straight through.
Entry, stop and target
The touch is the entry, at market. The rest follows from the structure rather than from a fixed distance:
Level
Where it comes from
Entry
The moment price trades into the gap, long at the ask.
Stop
The structure low
A
, less a buffer you set in points or pips. If the low breaks, the reason for the trade is gone.
Target
A fixed multiple of the risk. With R set to 2, the target is twice the entry-to-stop distance.
Because the stop is structural, its distance varies from trade to trade — and that is the point. The position size is worked out from the
percent of balance
you are willing to lose, so a tight stop buys a larger position and a wide one a smaller position, and every trade risks the same money. A fixed lot is available but it is not the default, because it would make a tight stop and a wide stop mean completely different things.
Two guards sit in front of every order: the EA refuses the trade if the stop would land on the wrong side of price, and if the distance is inside the broker's stop level. If the smallest tradable volume would risk more than your percentage allows, it says so in the log rather than quietly risking more.
Managing the position
Once a trade is open, two optional rules can move its stop. Both live in a separate class,
CPositionManager
, which knows nothing about market structure or gaps — only a symbol and a magic number — so it can be reused in any EA.
They share one rule that is not optional:
A stop only ever moves
forward
. Break even and trailing each propose a level, the better of the two wins, and the result is thrown away unless it improves on the stop already in place. A stop that can retreat is not a stop.
Rule
Setting
What it does
Break even
Trigger
How far the trade must be in profit before the stop is moved to entry.
Offset
How far beyond entry the stop lands. An offset above zero locks a little profit instead of leaving a true scratch, and covers the spread and commission.
Trailing
Trigger
How far in profit the trade must be before trailing begins at all.
Step
The smallest improvement worth a modify. Without it the stop would be rewritten on nearly every tick for a fraction of a point.
Distance
How far behind price the stop is held once trailing is running.
Both triggers and distances are read in the same unit as the rest of the EA — points or pips, whichever you selected. The two rules work together: break even typically fires first and parks the stop at entry, then trailing takes over as the move extends. A level the broker would reject for being too close to price is skipped rather than retried.
Standing down after a CHoCH
A CHoCH is the structure admitting the trend has turned. The EA does not treat it as a signal to trade the other way immediately — it clears every gap it was watching and does nothing at all until the
first BoS in the new direction
confirms the reversal has follow-through. This is the single biggest difference between it and a naive FVG bot, and it is what keeps it out of a range that is chopping back and forth.
A new BoS in the same direction has a similar effect on a smaller scale: the previous gaps are discarded and the scan runs again. Only the current leg is ever traded.

The display group controls the gap boxes and the structure drawing. Turn the structure off for long optimisation runs — it redraws several hundred objects a bar, which is fine visually and wasteful otherwise.
What to know before running it
Closed bars only.
The structure is evaluated on closed candles; the forming candle is never used. Entries are checked on every tick, because a gap can be touched mid-candle.
The structure repaints, by design.
A pending extreme moves until its IDM is taken, and a liquidity sweep relocates a point backwards. Trades are only taken from confirmed legs, so this does not move a stop after the fact — but the picture before confirmation is provisional and is meant to be.
It is a continuation strategy.
It will sit out reversals entirely, and in a long range it will stand down after the first CHoCH and stay down.
Trade management is deliberately plain.
One entry, one target, and an optional break even and trailing stop. There are no partial closes, no scaling in, no grid and no recovery logic, because the point is to see whether the structure plus gap premise does anything on its own rather than to bury it under management.
Test it before you believe it.
There are no tuned parameters here and no presented backtest. The defaults are reasonable starting points, not recommendations.
