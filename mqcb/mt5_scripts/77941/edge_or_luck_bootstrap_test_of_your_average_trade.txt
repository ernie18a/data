Edge or Luck reads the closed trades in your account history and checks whether your average trade is large enough to tell apart from luck. A net profit or a profit factor tells you what happened, but not how easily the same numbers could have come from chance. With only a few dozen trades, chance can explain a lot, and this script estimates how much from your own trades.
It uses a bootstrap, the resampling method Bradley Efron introduced in 1979. The script draws as many trades as you have from your own history, at random and with replacement, so some trades appear twice and some not at all, and works out their average. It repeats this 10,000 times. The middle 95% of those averages is the range your true average trade most likely sits in, and the share of averages below zero is the chance that the true average is below zero. The method makes no assumption about the shape of your returns, which suits trading, where a few large trades often carry the whole result.
The script on a USD/JPY chart, reading a demo account with 44 closed trades: the whole range is above zero.
How to read the result
The whole range is above zero: on this sample the edge looks real.
The range includes zero: the result could still be luck.
The whole range is below zero: the losses are larger than luck explains.
Fewer than 30 trades (you can change the number): the script says the sample is too small to judge. With very few trades the bootstrap can only reshuffle the trades you already have, so it never sees the losses that haven't happened yet, and the range comes out too narrow.
The result is printed in the Experts tab of the Toolbox and shown in the top left corner of the chart. The chart you run it on does not matter, because the script reads the whole account history rather than the chart's symbol. Run it again with other settings to replace the text, for example to check one Expert Advisor or only the last 30 days.

How trades are counted
A trade is every deal of one position, counted once the position is closed. Its net result is profit plus commission, fee and swap, as recorded in your broker's deal history, so it matches what you were charged. Deposits, withdrawals and charges booked outside trades are left out, and so are positions that are still open. On a netting account, a position that is reversed counts as one trade.
Parameters
Magic number
: -1 for all trades, 0 for manual trades only, or the magic number of one Expert Advisor.
Symbol
: leave empty for all symbols.
Only trades closed in the last N days
: 0 for the whole history.
Resamples
: 1,000 to 100,000 (default 10,000).
Fewest trades for a verdict
: default 30.
Notes
The script only reads the history. It never opens, changes or closes a trade, so you can run it next to live Expert Advisors.
The random numbers start from a fixed seed, so running the script twice on the same history gives the same answer.
The test describes trades that already happened. It cannot tell you whether the market will keep behaving the same way, and past results do not guarantee future results.
