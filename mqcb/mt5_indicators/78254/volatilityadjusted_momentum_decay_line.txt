The idea behind it
Most momentum indicators use a fixed lookback and a fixed smoothing constant, which means a 10-bar momentum reading behaves the same whether the market is drifting quietly or whipping through a news spike. The
Volatility-Adjusted Momentum Decay Line (VAMD)
starts from a simple raw momentum reading — the change in close price over
InpMomentumPeriod
bars — and divides it by the current
ATR
so the reading is expressed in volatility units rather than price units. That normalized momentum is then fed into a recursive decay filter: on every bar, the line moves a fraction of the way from its previous value toward the new normalized momentum, and that fraction — the decay rate — is not fixed. It is scaled by the ratio of the current ATR to its own moving average over
InpATRAvgPeriod
bars. When volatility is running above its recent baseline, the decay rate speeds up and the line snaps toward fresh momentum readings; when volatility compresses back to baseline, the decay rate slows down and the line holds its position longer, acting almost like a built-in memory of the prior move.
The result behaves less like a classic oscillator and more like a momentum reading that "forgets" faster during volatility expansions and "remembers" longer during quiet stretches. It is built for instruments and timeframes where volatility regimes shift visibly within a session, such as
EURUSD
or
GBPUSD
on an
M15 chart
, where a London-session expansion followed by a quiet Asian range is common. A separate indicator subwindow below the price chart keeps the oscillator visually distinct from price action.
How to interpret it
Decay Line (main plot)
— the single colored line in the subwindow. It oscillates around a zero level; sustained moves away from zero indicate momentum that volatility conditions are not yet pulling back down, while a line hugging zero indicates momentum has already decayed out or the market has no clear directional push.
ATR-relative decay rate
— the internal mechanism that scales how quickly the line reacts. It is never plotted directly, but it is what makes the line snap faster during the gray-shaded volatile stretch in the example figure and settle more slowly during the calmer stretches on either side.
Color / sign state
— the line is drawn in one shade when its value is at or above zero and a second, lighter shade when it drops below zero, making regime flips (bullish momentum decaying into bearish, or vice versa) visually immediate without reading exact values.
Practical usage
— in trend-following use, a Decay Line that stays firmly on one side of zero through a volatility expansion suggests the move has real follow-through, since the faster decay rate would have pulled a weak reading back to zero already. In mean-reversion use, a Decay Line that spikes away from zero and then decays rapidly back through it during a volatile patch can mark exhaustion of a short-term push, since the adaptive decay is actively working against the extended reading.
External variables (inputs)
Input
Default
Purpose
InpMomentumPeriod
10
Lookback, in bars, used to compute the raw price momentum before volatility normalization.
InpATRPeriod
14
Period for the ATR used as the volatility unit that normalizes raw momentum into a dimensionless reading.
InpATRAvgPeriod
50
Period for the ATR's own moving average, which defines the volatility baseline the current ATR is compared against to size the decay rate.
InpBaseDecay
0.15
Base decay coefficient applied when current ATR equals its baseline; controls how fast the line relaxes toward zero under normal conditions.
InpMaxDecay
0.90
Hard ceiling on the decay rate, preventing the line from becoming unstable or purely reactive during extreme volatility spikes.
Recommended use
Load VAMD in its own subwindow beneath the price chart and watch it alongside a volatility reference, such as the ATR panel most traders already keep visible, since the two reinforce each other: a VAMD reading that stays extended while ATR is climbing is a stronger signal than the same extension during a flat ATR. On lower timeframes (M5–M15) the default settings react within a session; on H1 and above, widening
InpATRAvgPeriod
to 80–100 gives the volatility baseline more bars to stabilize before it starts driving the decay rate. Combine VAMD with a basic price-action filter — support/resistance levels or a simple trend moving average — rather than trading its zero-line crosses in isolation, since the adaptive decay is designed to describe how momentum is fading, not to generate standalone entry signals.
